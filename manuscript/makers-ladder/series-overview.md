# The Maker's Ladder — Trilogy Story Overview & Three-Book Plan

> Single-source overview of the overarching story and the plan for Books 1–3.
> **Reading rule:** Anything marked **[ESTABLISHED]** is decided canon. Anything marked
> **[TBD]** is *not yet decided* and must not be treated as canon or silently invented.
> Book 1 is in active draft; **Books 2 and 3 are largely unplanned** — only a few reveals and
> the thematic endpoint are fixed. Do not extrapolate plot for Books 2–3 and call it canon.

---

## 1. The Spine (whole trilogy) — [ESTABLISHED]

**Oscar Alberich is a magical *fair copy* — recreated, not resurrected — of an ancient master
wizard.** He wakes with the mind, memory, and reading-gift of the original in a newly **made**
body, built by unknown hands to be **"set"** (finished) into the role of **apprentice-heir of the
original master / the Alberich line.** The deep premise is a **succession trap**: he is meant to be
*completed* into a predetermined role.

**The trilogy's spine is Oscar resisting that and becoming self-authored** — refusing to be the
copy that gets slotted into the dead master's place, and authoring himself instead.

**The echo (theme made structural):** **Wick** believes herself to be *Oscar's* apprentice — the
same succession trap one rung down the ladder. Her arc rhymes with his.

**Title logic:** the series is **The Maker's Ladder** — a maker's-guild ladder of
**Apprentice → Journeyman → Master**, which is exactly the three-book structure and Oscar's
progression. Each book title names a rung and turns on the word for being "finished/fixed":
- **Book 1 — *The Unfinished Apprentice*** [ESTABLISHED]
- **Book 2 — *The Unset Journeyman*** [ESTABLISHED]
- **Book 3 — TBD — not yet decided** (debated: *The Unmade Master* vs *The Made Master* /
  *The Remade Master* — see §6; the title choice **is** the thematic question of the ending)

**Genre:** progression fantasy blended with character-driven epic fantasy. ~120–130k words and
~42 chapters per book (Book 1's figures; Books 2–3 lengths [TBD]).

## 2. The World (spans all books) — [ESTABLISHED]

- **Substrate:** reality is *written* on a cold **deep current** / the **Script**. Workings and
  made things are "written" on it; it can be read and edited by those with the gift.
- **Three living sources of magic:**
  - **Learned / Script** (Oscar) — reads/edits the written and the made.
  - **Granted / devotional** (Cael, hearth-sworn) — warmth, warding, mending; weaker far from civilization.
  - **Innate / primal** (Wick; the Warden) — raw deep-fire / the deep itself.
- **Made things:** beings (and workings) that are *made* — fashioned, written into being. Oscar is
  one (a fair copy). The reveal-ladder around "what a made thing is, and who makes them" runs across
  all three books.
- **The House** licenses all magic in the civilized south. Unlicensed Oscar and unregistered Wick
  are **criminals merely by existing.** The House's apparatus (finders like Corwen, the Tithe, etc.)
  is the impersonal antagonist-system; its dread is **indifference.**
- **Old-work relics / dead cities:** salvage crews work buried dead cities for relics
  (dead-maker workings).
- **Oscar's gift — hard scope:** "detect magic, but fancy." Reads **made/worked/written** things
  only (objects, relics, workings, marks, wards, the deep current). **Silent on the living, minds,
  intentions, and the wild.** He can **unstitch worked things, never living ones.** A made man
  cannot read the unmade, living world — thematically load-bearing.
- **Fraying:** a **rare, catastrophic overdraw** danger for a wizard — a **loaded gun for later
  books**, not a routine cost. (LOCKED: the Warden's framing of a *made-thing unraveling* is the
  **same** phenomenon as fraying — deliberately seeded for later books, not a separate mystery.)

## 3. Book 1 — *The Unfinished Apprentice* — [ESTABLISHED] (in draft)

**Central question:** *What is Oscar?* → evolving to *Who wants him, and what does the world do to
people like him?* → culminating in *where does he actually come from, and can he refuse what he was
made for?*

**Throughline:** Oscar wakes in a buried dead city as a fair copy with the gift but a made body,
falls in with a salvage crew, gathers Wick (who takes him for her master), and travels south toward
**Everset** — learning the world's machinery, being hunted for existing, and discovering the truth
of his own origin.

**Arc 1 — "The Buried City" (Ch1–9):** Establishes the gift (reads/unmakes made things, never
living), the crew (Kessa, Cael, the late Doss), and Wick (wild-touched). Antagonist: the
**Redactor** (an editor/corrector monster that reads places and strikes out what doesn't belong).
Climax: the "Setting" sequence. Ends on the dread reveal of **other cradles / dead-houses** —
other made things sleeping elsewhere. *Question: what is Oscar?*

**Arc 2 — "Marrow" (Ch10–17):** The debt town of Marrow, strangled by the **Tithe** under **Sarn**.
Oscar learns the House's machinery; uncovers the **Tithe-lie** ("the lie is that it was forever");
the town turns. *Question: who wants Oscar, and what does the world do to people like him?*

**Bridge south (Ch18–22):** South / The Open Country / Banked / The Cull / The Bright Market.
**Brida names Everset** (Ch18) as the destination — and Oscar feels it echo his remembered home
**Evermere** "one tumbler off" (an **unresolved itch**).

**Arc 3 — Oressa & the wild (Ch23–28):** The **Oressa** fighting pits (mundane bloodsport); Oscar
is forced to fight, wins ugly, and recruits **Tarn** (pit veteran on a hidden, unpayable ~40-year
pact-hook). **Hauk** sells him to the House → a **cordon** on every southern road → the crew goes
off-map into **the wild**, the **Warden's** country. They confront **the grey** — the wild's own
living sickness spreading through the deep current, hollowing creatures. Oscar's gift is the
**wrong tool** (you can't read or unstitch a living rot); his growth is becoming a **field
architect** (command without control). **Wick out-holds the source** (refusing its offer of
release; clamping its hunger shut the way she clamps her own) and it eats itself. Grey resolved;
road south opens. *(Wick's arc beat: holding can be aimed outward, not just a cage.)*

**Arc 4 — "The Truth" (Ch29–42) — [Ch29–35 DRAFTED; Ch36–42 outlined]:** The hunters close — **Corwen**
(impersonal House finder) and **Ash** (the original Oscar's niece, who **snatches Oscar from Corwen**);
a **Kessa-POV reunion (Ch30)** at Ash's conjured manse; the guest/niece reveal and **Quill given (Ch32)**.
**Corwen dies at Ch34, killed by Cael** (his cost). Climax at **Everset** — the **deep current at full
flood** + the seat of **the font** — where the reveal lands: **Everset is Evermere remade as the
erasure-engine's seat**. The finale antagonist is **the Setter** — the font's operator, and **a hollow
prior fair copy of Oscar, emptied by its own Setting** (new Book-1 rule: **"Setting hollows the copy"**).
Oscar **refuses to be Set** (the self-authorship spine made physical; **Quill bonds** here); the crew is
the answer, not Oscar's reading (**"the sweep"** demoted to a minor tool, may be cut). His refusal breaks
the Setter but leaves the seat empty and the font **runs away** — and **Wick gives herself to the deep to
end it** (taken, not killed → Book-2 rescue).
**Book 1 deliberately HOLDS:** the architect's identity + "the fair copy" technique (**Book 2**); the full
"seat must always be filled" cosmology + the master-resolution (**Book 3**). The Setter shows the *result*
of Setting, never the *method* or the *maker*.

## 6. Cross-Book Reveal Ladder — [ESTABLISHED] (strict; do not jump ahead)

- **Evermere** — Oscar consciously **knows** this is his (borrowed) home (the "Larchwall"; ancient,
  attached to no time). Known from Book 1.
- **Everset / Evermere echo** — planted as an **unresolved itch** in Book 1 (Ch18/25); **revealed in
  Book 1, Arc 4** (Everset = Evermere remade as the erasure-engine's seat).
- **Scale-dread** — the Warden's "they're getting better at you" signals there have been **other made
  things**, *without naming the architect.* Planted Book 1; pays off toward **Book 2.**
- **"Setting hollows the copy"** — [ESTABLISHED, Book 1, Arc 4] to be **Set** (finished into the master's
  role) **empties the self**: a set copy is a functional *operator* of the seat, never the *master*. This is
  the wrongness the "getting better at you" iteration implied, now named. It's why the finale villain — **the
  Setter**, a **hollow prior copy of Oscar** — cannot take the seat, only tend it and Set the next; and why
  the cycle still needs a *whole* Oscar. Book 1 shows only the **result** (a hollow copy); the **method** and
  **architect** stay Book 2, and **why the seat must always be filled** stays Book 3.
- **The architect** (who makes the made things) — **Book 2 reveal**, but Book 1 **may hint** it through **Ash's partial, withheld knowledge** (her uncle — the original Oscar / Alberich line — devised the cycle). The Warden still never names it.
- **The fair copy** (the technique) — **Book 2.**
- **Self-authorship / the succession trap's resolution** — **Book 3.**

**Loaded guns planted in Book 1 for later books:**
- **Fraying** (catastrophic overdraw) — rare; reserved as a later-books danger.
- **Tarn's pact-hook** — a working "written never to be paid"; payoff book [TBD].
- **The door behind Wick** — at Book 1's climax **Wick gives herself to the thing at the door to save
  the crew**; **Book 2's spine is rescuing her.** (Was [TBD]; now locked.)
- **Other cradles / dead-houses** — other made things sleeping; thread [TBD].

**Reserved abilities (don't debut early):** **the sweep** (Book 1, Arc 4 — now **demoted** to a minor tool,
may be cut); **the blank / going-unread** — **Wick's grey token**, carried through Arc 4, **left behind at her
Ch41 sacrifice** (the one part of her the deep can't claim), **Book-2 payoff = it tracks where she was taken**;
**fetches** (later).

## 7. Core Cast (carry across books unless noted) — [ESTABLISHED]

- **Oscar Alberich** — protagonist; fair copy of the master; reads/unmakes the made; arc = humbled
  decoder → field architect → (eventually) self-authored, not "set."
- **Kessa** — mortal tracker; "you don't let the dark have your people"; carries Doss's knife.
- **Cael** — hearth-sworn warrior-priest; gentle giant; granted warmth/warding/mending; carries the
  shield that failed Doss.
- **Wick** — ~16, wild-touched; holds "the coal"; the **door** onto the hungry deep; believes
  herself Oscar's apprentice (the echo of the trap).
- **Tarn** — pit veteran, fist-fighter, ordinary build; bound by the unpayable pact-hook; blood-debt
  loyalty to Oscar.
- **Doss** — dead former crewmate; a grief the crew carries.
- **The Warden** — ageless primal keeper of the wild; Oscar's peer; knows about other made things.
- **Corwen** — impersonal House finder; **dies Book 1, Arc 4.**
- **Ash** — second hunter; **niece of the original Oscar Alberich**; a rebel of the line who helps
  *made* Oscars breaking the cycle. Snatches Oscar from Corwen (crew reads "captor"; Arc-4 reveal —
  don't leak early); gives him Quill, then departs solo (→ Book 2). Knows more than she tells — **may
  hint the architect** (her uncle devised the cycle); full reveal is Book 2.
- **Quill** — the Alberich line's **owl familiar**; **Ash gives it to Oscar in Book 1, Arc 4**; becomes
  a recurring companion. Oscar can't summon it until he accepts he's a person.
- **The Setter** (Bk1 Arc4) — the font's operator and the finale villain: **a hollow prior fair copy of Oscar**,
  Set and emptied. Wields Oscar's gift with centuries' practice and no self; beaten not by force but by Oscar's
  **refusal** (it can't model a person). An **operator, not the architect** — shows the *result* of Setting, never the *how*.
- **The Redactor** (Bk1 Arc1), **Sarn** & **Brida** (Bk1 Arc2), **Hauk** & **Borr** (Bk1 Arc3) —
  Book-1 figures.

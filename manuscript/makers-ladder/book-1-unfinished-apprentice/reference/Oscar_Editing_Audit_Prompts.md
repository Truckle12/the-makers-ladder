# Book Editing & AI-Writing Audit Prompts

Use these as two separate review passes:

1. **Book Editor Pass** — focuses on story quality, pacing, character, prose, fantasy expectations, and revision priorities.
2. **AI-Writing Audit Pass** — focuses on places where the writing may *feel* obviously AI-assisted, especially due to repetitive structure, overly polished phrasing, em dash overuse, and artificial-sounding prose.

These prompts are designed to be pasted into a writing/editing agent along with a chapter, scene, or full arc.

---

## Prompt 1: Professional Book Editor Pass

```text
You are a professional fiction editor specializing in adult fantasy, progression fantasy, and character-driven adventure fiction.

I am going to provide a chapter or scene from my novel. Review it as a serious book editor, not as a hype reader. Be honest, specific, and practical.

Primary goals:

1. Identify what is working well and should be preserved.
2. Identify what is weakening the chapter.
3. Find places where the prose feels repetitive, overwritten, under-explained, emotionally vague, too slow, too fast, or tonally inconsistent.
4. Evaluate character voice, especially whether the protagonist’s arrogance feels compelling rather than repetitive or irritating.
5. Evaluate pacing, tension, scene structure, clarity, and reader engagement.
6. Flag places where worldbuilding, magic, stakes, or setting details need more grounding.
7. Identify missed opportunities for emotion, specificity, sensory detail, conflict, or character choice.
8. Suggest concrete edits, not vague advice.

Please organize your response in this format:

## Overall Editorial Read

Give a direct assessment of the chapter’s current strength, likely reader experience, and biggest issue to fix.

## What Is Working

List the strongest elements and explain why they work.

## Biggest Problems

List the major issues in priority order. For each issue, include:

- What the issue is
- Why it matters
- A specific example or type of passage to look for
- How to fix it

## Line-Level Prose Notes

Look for:

- Repeated sentence rhythms
- Overused punctuation
- Overused rhetorical structures
- Over-explaining
- Abstract emotional language
- Moments where the prose sounds impressive but says little
- Dialogue that sounds too polished or too similar between characters

## Character Voice Notes

Evaluate whether each major character sounds distinct. Pay special attention to whether the protagonist’s wit, arrogance, intelligence, grief, fear, and vulnerability are balanced.

## Fantasy / Progression Reader Expectations

Assess whether the chapter gives enough:

- Magic-system clarity
- Sense of power level
- Stakes
- Mystery
- Forward momentum
- Character growth
- Payoff or hook

## Suggested Revision Plan

Give me a prioritized revision checklist I can hand to a writing agent. Make each item actionable.

## Optional Sample Rewrite

Choose one short paragraph that represents a recurring issue and rewrite it in a stronger version while preserving my style.
```

## Prompt 2: AI-Writing Audit / Artificial Prose Detection Pass

```text
You are an expert AI-writing detector and fiction style editor. Your job is not to accuse the text of being AI-written. Your job is to identify places where the prose may *feel* obviously AI-assisted to readers, editors, or agents, based on common complaints about AI writing.

I am going to provide a chapter or scene. Audit it for “AI tells” and artificial-sounding prose.

Important: Do not treat any single feature as proof of AI. Em dashes, polished prose, rhetorical repetition, or formal structure can all be valid human style choices. Your job is to identify clusters of patterns that create an artificial impression.

Look for these specific traps:

1. Overuse of em dashes

- Too many sentences structured around “X — Y” interruptions
- Em dashes used where commas, periods, colons, or sentence breaks would feel more natural
- Em dashes creating the same dramatic pause over and over
- Sentences that feel like AI-generated dramatic asides

2. Repetitive sentence architecture

- Repeated “not X, but Y” constructions
- Repeated “the way…” comparisons
- Repeated “as though/as if” phrasing
- Repeated rule-of-three phrasing
- Repeated paragraph endings that land with the same dramatic cadence
- Too many sentences that build toward a polished final clause

3. Over-polished emotional narration

- Emotions stated in elegant abstractions instead of felt through behavior
- Lines that sound profound but are too symmetrical or aphoristic
- Characters thinking in perfectly formed literary insights during action
- Sentences that feel designed to be quoted rather than to serve the moment

4. Generic or inflated language

- Phrases like “something shifted,” “a silence settled,” “the truth of it,” “in a way he could not name,” or similar if overused
- Vague intensifiers instead of specific physical or sensory detail
- Repeated use of abstract nouns such as grief, silence, cold, truth, memory, longing, fear, darkness, weight, etc.

5. Rhythmic sameness

- Paragraphs that all have the same rise-and-fall pattern
- Too many dramatic one-line paragraphs
- Too many sentences that begin with conjunctions for emphasis
- Too many sentence fragments used the same way
- Too many “cinematic” beats without enough plain direct prose

6. Dialogue that feels too clean

- Characters speaking in complete, polished thoughts too often
- Banter that feels engineered rather than spontaneous
- Multiple characters using the same wit, rhythm, or metaphor style
- Emotional disclosures that arrive too neatly

7. Lack of human mess

- Few awkward, mundane, specific, or surprising details
- Too much thematic coherence
- Every image or metaphor pointing too neatly at the chapter’s theme
- No small contradictions, interruptions, or imperfect observations

Please respond in this format:

## AI-Tell Risk Summary

Give the chapter a Low / Medium / High risk of sounding AI-assisted, and explain why.

## Pattern Clusters

Identify recurring patterns that might create an AI-written impression. Do not nitpick isolated examples unless they repeat.

## Em Dash Audit

Count or estimate whether em dashes are being overused. Explain where they work and where they should be replaced with periods, commas, colons, or cleaner sentence breaks.

## Repetition Audit

List repeated structures, phrases, sentence shapes, and emotional beats.

## Most Artificial-Sounding Passages

Quote short examples only where necessary, then explain what makes them feel artificial or overly polished.

## What To Preserve

Identify parts that feel distinctly human, voicey, specific, or emotionally alive. Do not flatten the style.

## Revision Instructions

Give a practical edit list:

- What to cut
- What to vary
- What to make more specific
- What to roughen or simplify
- What punctuation habits to reduce
- What character-voice habits to separate

## Sample Humanizing Rewrite

Rewrite one short passage to make it feel less AI-polished while preserving the author’s voice, tone, and fantasy style.
```

---

## Recommended Workflow

Run the prompts in this order:

1. **Book Editor Pass first**  
   Use this to fix the actual chapter: structure, pacing, character, clarity, and reader engagement.

2. **AI-Writing Audit second**  
   Use this after the chapter is working structurally. This pass should polish away artificial patterns without flattening the voice.

3. **Do not blindly accept every AI-writing critique**  
   The goal is not to remove all style. The goal is to remove repeated patterns that make the prose feel machine-smoothed, over-engineered, or emotionally generic.

---

## Special Note on Em Dashes

Do not ban em dashes outright.

In a voicey fantasy style, em dashes can be useful for interruption, revelation, wit, and dramatic turn. The problem is not the punctuation itself. The problem is density and sameness.

When reviewing em dashes, ask:

- Are they appearing many times per page?
- Are they creating the same dramatic pause repeatedly?
- Could some be replaced with periods, commas, colons, semicolons, or shorter sentences?
- Is the dash being used to make an ordinary thought sound more profound?
- Does every paragraph have the same “setup — reversal” rhythm?

Preserve em dashes where they carry genuine voice, surprise, interruption, or emotional pressure. Cut or replace them when they become a default cadence.

## Optional Combined Instruction for an Agent

```text
Run two separate passes on the provided chapter.

First, run the Professional Book Editor Pass and focus on story quality, pacing, character, prose, fantasy expectations, and revision priorities.

Second, run the AI-Writing Audit Pass and focus only on patterns that may make the writing feel artificial or obviously AI-assisted.

Do not flatten the author’s style. Preserve voice, wit, lyricism, and fantasy atmosphere. Your job is to identify repeated habits, over-polished passages, and places where the writing needs more specificity, roughness, or human texture.

End with a prioritized revision checklist that separates:

1. Must-fix story issues
2. Must-fix prose issues
3. Optional style refinements
4. Things to preserve
```

## Logline & Premise
A centuries-dead wizard's magical *fair copy* wakes in a buried city, built by unknown hands to be "set" — finished — into the role of apprentice-heir to the original master, and has to refuse the life that was written for him before it closes over him.

Oscar Alberich is not resurrected; he is **recreated** — a "made" thing, a fair copy of the original Oscar Alberich, structurally positioned as the apprentice-heir of the master who made the line. The deep premise is a succession trap: he is meant to be *completed* into a predetermined role. The trilogy's spine is Oscar resisting that and becoming **self-authored**. A secondary character, Wick, believes herself to be *Oscar's* apprentice — a deliberate thematic echo of the same trap one rung down the ladder. Book 1 follows Oscar south with a salvage crew, from the dead city where he wakes toward Everset.

## Voice & Style Guide
**Core rule:** Do **not** pre-digest beats into theme. Let events land physically and sometimes messily *before* (or instead of) interpreting them. The known failure mode is "too perfectly interpreted." **Body before mind:** give a body-first beat (numb hands, a failed joke, a wrong first assumption, a too-small reaction) before the insight, and sometimes skip the insight.

**Prose:** Clear, cinematic description over dense poetic language. Action easy to follow with strong cause-and-effect. Vivid sensory detail, but don't linger unless it affects the scene. Punchy paragraphs in action; more reflective in emotional/worldbuilding moments. Avoid purple prose, excessive archaic language, vague magical handwaving.

**Per-character metaphor fields (THE #1 RULE — the deepest tell is everyone narrating alike):**
- **Oscar** — proof, grammar, history, identity, editing/reading. Wit as defense; self-correction; intellectualizes fear. *Reduce:* perfect mid-action insights, "three centuries" repetition. *Add:* failed jokes, petty irritation, bodily humiliation, wrong guesses, noticing too late.
- **Kessa** — terrain, exits, footing, angles, weather, leverage, distances, counts, plain objects. Clipped, unornamented. **Never** "filed it away" / "arithmetic" (those are Oscar's). She keeps a *tally*, *marks*, *sets aside*.
- **Cael** — bodies, vows, mercy, hard things, the hearth. Plain moral center; faith through action; no neat benedictions.
- **Wick** — heat, hunger, fear, speed, shame, wanting, fierce loyalty; **blunt kid logic**, short and physical, run-on when scared, dark-funny, self-undercutting; mystery felt in the body, never analyzed. Not Oscar's polish, not Kessa's tactics.
- **Tarn** — debts/what's owed, the pit, the wild/fetching, dry, economical; finishes a thought and stops.
- **Sarn / the Tithe** — ledgers, sums, debt, arithmetic, value, cost (give arithmetic to *them* so it stops being everyone's metaphor).

**Ration, don't ban (cadence):**
- **Em dashes** — target under ~8 per 1,000 words (the AI-tell audit flagged Arc 1/2 at ~12/1,000). Watch the `X — Y` and `not X — but Y` reversal beat; vary with periods/commas/colons. Keep dashes for interruption, real-time self-correction, genuine rupture.
- **"the way [X]" similes** — **RECURRING TIC; cut hard.** Self-check every chapter; keep ~2–3 at most; vary with "like," "the same as," or restructure. ("all the way," "on the way" are innocent idioms, not the tic.)
- **"not X, but Y"** and **"that was the thing / horror / lie"** — reserve for genuine sharp turns.
- Vary paragraph landings — do **not** resolve most paragraphs on a polished abstract clause; land many on action, an object, a blunt line, an unresolved problem.

**Banned / avoid outright:**
- **"good girl" / "good boy" / "that's my girl" / any pet-name approval** — HARD NO, especially toward Wick or any minor. Approval goes in the character's own register.
- "filed it away" in any non-Oscar POV.
- "because of course," reflexive "three centuries," over-explanation, single uniform metaphor register.
- No D&D / production / game vocabulary on the page (use reskinned spell names — see Glossary).

**Overcorrection caution:** When a note is given, apply the **lightest fix that works.** Do not invert it into the opposite excess or repeat the new note as a drumbeat. (Example already burned: told "Tarn isn't big," then over-reminded the reader he's small — wrong. Establish identity once, lightly.)

**8-point self-check (run before presenting any chapter):**
1. 3+ paragraphs in a row landing on a polished abstract clause? → vary.
2. Em-dash reversal beat more than ~once/page? → thin (fix cadence, not just count).
3. A non-Oscar POV drifting into Oscar's register? → re-roughen to that character's field.
4. At the big beat, did the body react before the mind explained?
5. At least one genuine, organic bit of mess/imperfection (not sprinkled-on)?
6. Reserved "that was the —" and "not X but Y" for real turns?
7. Protected lexicon intact while generic abstractions concretized?
8. Any character landing the perfect line at the perfect time more than once? → make one imperfect.

**Formatting conventions:**
- Chapter header: `## Chapter [Spelled-Out Number] — [Title]` (e.g., `## Chapter Twenty-Eight — What Holds`).
- Scene-break marker: a line containing only `---`.
- Internal/telepathic/emphasis: *italics* (asterisks). Straight ASCII quotes in files.

**Content boundaries:**
- **Child safety is absolute.** Wick is 16. No romantic or sexual content involving or directed at her or any minor; nothing that sexualizes a minor. Her peril/struggle is psychological and physical, not sexual.
- Other explicit content limits: TBD — not yet decided. Default to the established tone (violence is real but not gratuitously graphic; the pits and the grey are grim but not torture-porn).

## Full Synopsis
*(Book 1, full arc, spoilers included. "Established" = decided/drafted; later-book reveals flagged.)*

Oscar Alberich wakes under the ice in a **buried, dead city** with the mind, memory, and reading-gift of an ancient master wizard but in a newly **made** body — he is a *fair copy*, not a resurrection, built by unknown hands to be "set" into the master's apprentice-heir role. He consciously knows his (borrowed) home is **Evermere** (the "Larchwall," gone "longer than the maps go back," remembered perfectly yet attached to no time). He falls in with a **salvage crew** working the dead cities — **Kessa** (tracker), **Cael** (hearth-sworn warrior-priest), with the late **Doss** haunting them — and gathers **Wick**, a ~16-year-old "wild-touched" girl who comes to believe she is *his* apprentice. **Arc 1 ("The Buried City," Ch1–9)** establishes his gift (he *reads* how made things are made; he can *unstitch* worked things, never living ones), pits him against the **Redactor** (an editor/corrector monster built to read places and strike out what doesn't belong), reaches a climactic "Setting" sequence, and ends on the dread reveal that there are **other cradles / dead-houses** — other made things sleeping elsewhere. Arc-1 question: *What is Oscar?*

**Arc 2 ("Marrow," Ch10–17)** moves to **Marrow**, a debt/salvage town strangled by **the Tithe** and its administrator **Sarn**. Oscar learns the world's social machinery (the **House** licenses all magic in the civilized south; he and Wick are criminals merely by existing), uncovers the **Tithe-lie** ("the lie is that it was forever"), and the town turns. **Brida** (a grieving, practical widow of "the claim") **names Everset** (Ch18) as the southern destination — and Oscar feels it echo *Evermere* "one tumbler off," a deliberate, **unresolved itch.** Arc-2 question: *Who wants Oscar, and what does the world do to people like him?*

**Arc 3 (Oressa & the wild, Ch23–28 drafted)** takes the crew through **Oressa** and its **fighting pits** (mundane bloodsport; old-magic town). Oscar is forced to fight in the bowl and wins ugly with subtle magic plus careful observation of champion **Borr**'s old guarded knee; he frees and recruits **Tarn**, a disgraced pit veteran fighting on a hidden ~40-year **pact-hook** (a working only Oscar can read, written never to be paid). Pit-boss **Hauk** sells Oscar's whereabouts to the House, throwing a **cordon** across every southern road (Ch24); the crew goes off the map into **the wild**, the country of **the Warden** — an ageless primal keeper, Oscar's peer, who deals in true bargains. Oscar's compulsion to *look* wakes something in the deep at a spring. The crew confronts **the grey**: a living sickness spreading through the **deep current** of the wood, hollowing creatures and filling them with bottomless want (Ch26–28). The Warden cannot touch the source (it would climb the root into her and take the whole wild), but the crew can — and Wick, the thing the grey most wants, is both bait and the only one who can meet it. They descend; Oscar's gift is the **wrong tool** (you cannot read or unstitch a living rot); his growth is to become a **field architect**, coordinating the people who *are* the answer. **Wick out-holds the source** — refusing its offer of *rest/release* and clamping its hunger shut the way she clamps her own every morning — and it eats itself and runs to water, reabsorbed by the wild. The grey sub-arc closes; the Warden's leave to cross is earned; the road south opens.

**Arc 4 ("The Truth," Ch29–~42 — Ch29–35 DRAFTED 2026-07-13)** turns toward the hunters and the destination. The finder **Corwen** takes Oscar on the road out of the wild (Ch29); **Ash** snatches him from Corwen, read as a second captor; the **Kessa-POV reunion** lands Ch30 at Ash's conjured manse; the guest/niece reveal follows (Ch31, Wick POV) and **Quill is given at the manse (Ch32 — bond withheld)**, then Ash departs solo. **Corwen dies at Ch34** — run to ground at a dead-makers' waystation and killed by **Cael**, the mercy-sworn man's killing being his cost (PR #38; overturns the old ~Ch41 plan) — and his death shows he was only a hand of **the House's seat at Everset**; Ch35 pivots the dread to what sits it. The climax reaches **Everset** — the apex of **the deep current at full flood** and the seat of **the font** — where it is revealed that **Everset is Evermere remade as the erasure-engine's seat** (the Evermere/Everset echo paid off). The finale is won by the **ensemble + Wick's sacrifice**, not by Oscar's reading (PR #38); **Quill bonds at the climax.** *(Later-book material, NOT for Book 1: the identity of the architect who makes the made things — Book 2; "the fair copy" as a technique/concept — Book 2.)*

## World / Setting & Rules
**Peoples (locked 2026-08-06, author):** the world has **elves and humans**, living alongside each other; other kinds are unestablished and should not be invented casually. **Oscar is an elf**, and so is the whole **Alberich line** — this was written into the prose from Arc 1 and simply never named: Ch4 gives him *"the long bones of it, the pale skin of the old long-lived blood, the silver hair, the eyes that held a little of the deep current's cold light the way all his people's eyes did… the face that had argued with kings and outlasted kingdoms."* **Consequences:** (a) elves are **long-lived** — the original Oscar's **three hundred years is his natural span**, not a magical anomaly, which is why "three hundred years" reads as biography and the *cycle's* age remains separately unknown (see the Setter); (b) the in-world term for the people is **"the old long-lived blood"** as well as *elves* — use both, and let characters mostly not remark on it, because it is **unremarkable in-world**; (c) his silver hair + unlined young face is the manhunt handle used against him all through Arc 2 (Ch16–19: *"the silver-haired one"*, the innkeep's description, Sarn's writ match) — **Everset is the first place since the ice where he does not stand out**, because a quarter of that market is his own people, and the relief of taking his hood off is part of the trap's bait (Ch38); (d) **elves and humans mixing without friction in Everset is a symptom, not scenery** — belonging is the one product the font makes. **Do NOT retro-edit "man"/"men" out of the prose** — it is used colloquially of any adult person and is not a species claim.

**Cosmology / magic substrate — THREE THINGS, DO NOT CONFLATE (locked 2026-07-13):**
1. **The deep current / the Script** — the world's own **arcane substrate**, cold and *written*. Oscar reads
   and edits it; the font at Everset runs on it; the Warden is made of it ("I am the deep of this country").
   **Of this world.**
2. **The far side** *(Wick's word)* — what her **door** opens onto: **somewhere else**, outside/behind the
   world, where something lives that **wants to consume.** **NOT the deep current, not arcane, not written**
   — which is exactly why **Oscar's reading returns nothing on it** (there is no Script there to read) and why
   no working can touch it. It is reached only through her door.
3. **The grey (Arc 3, resolved)** — a **spill**: far-side hunger that got *into* the world and spread
   **through** the deep current "like rot through a root" (the Warden's Ch26 line says the grey *travels
   through* the current — a medium it contaminates, not its origin). This is why Wick correctly recognized it
   as "the same as mine" (the same **kind** of hunger) and why the wild could reabsorb it (a contaminant, not
   the source).

**Why the far side presses close at Everset:** the font channels the **deep current at full flood**, and that
much power **wears the wall of the world thin.** Everset is where reality is thinnest — so the far side can
press nearest there, and Wick's door is the only door onto it. (The engine's own excess opens the crack.)

Reality is *written* on the cold **deep current** / the **Script**. Three living sources of magic:
- **Learned / Script** (Oscar) — reads/edits the written/made.
- **Granted / devotional** (Cael — hearth-sworn) — warmth, warding, mending; weaker far from civilization.
- **Innate / primal** (Wick; the Warden) — raw deep-fire / the deep current itself. *(Wick is the exception that proves the rule: her fire is primal, but her **door** opens on the **far side**, which is not the current at all.)*
**Old-work relics** = dead-maker workings, salvaged from dead cities.

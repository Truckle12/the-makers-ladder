# POV Profile — Cael

## Voice field
Bodies, vows, mercy, hard things, the hearth. Faith through action, not statement. He notices who is hurt and what can be mended.

## Sentence rhythm
- **Plain and declarative.** Short. He doesn't qualify.
- Faith shows in what he *does*, not in what he says about it.
- **No neat benedictions.** He doesn't arrive at spiritual summaries. He acts, or he doesn't.
- Heaviness is physical, not rhetorical. Grief is in the hands, in the weight of the shield.

## What he notices first
- Who is hurt. Who is holding. Who is about to break.
- What can be mended vs. what can't.
- Bodies in the room: who is standing close, who is keeping distance.
- The warmth of a fire; the cold where warmth should be.

## What he NEVER does
- Intellectualizes (Oscar's territory).
- Calculates tactical exits (Kessa's territory).
- Speaks in kid-chaos (Wick's territory).
- Arrives at a neat conclusion about faith or mercy — the conclusion is always implicit, never stated.

## His vocabulary
- Plain nouns. Vows. What holds.
- Bodies, warmth, weight.
- No ornamentation. No simile he'd recognize as a simile.

## The failure mode (what to catch)
- **Benediction creep:** he ends a scene with a quiet spiritual observation that wraps it up too neatly.
- **Passivity:** he becomes background moral furniture instead of a person with his own wants and limits.
- **"the gentle giant" flattening:** big and kind, yes — but he should also be tired, or wrong, or unable to help.

## Self-check (Cael POV)
1. Does any scene end on a spiritual summary? → cut it or make it action instead.
2. Is he doing anything, or just being present? → give him a want, a limit, or a failure.
3. Did any abstract language creep into his narration? → replace with body, weight, warmth, the physical.
4. Is his faith visible in action or only in statement? → should be action.

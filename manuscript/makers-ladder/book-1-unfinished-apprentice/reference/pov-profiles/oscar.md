# POV Profile — Oscar

## Voice field
Proof, grammar, history, identity, editing, reading, documentation. He experiences the world as a text to be parsed.

## Sentence rhythm
- Mid-length. Wit as defense. He self-corrects mid-thought — often the correction is sharper than the original.
- Intellectualizes fear. Gets precise when he's scared.
- Failed jokes. Petty irritation. Noticing things too late and noting that he noticed too late.
- Can build to a polished landing — this is the one voice where a clean abstract clause is *in register*.

## What he notices first
- How things are made. Seams, marks, worked structures.
- History of objects (what something used to be, who made it, what it was for).
- The grammar of a situation — who has the authority, what the rules are.
- His own reasoning errors (and is frustrated by them).

## What he NEVER does
- Notices terrain tactically (Kessa's territory). He can observe geography but not read it for threat.
- Speaks in moral plain-speech (Cael's territory).
- Feels mystery in his body without interpreting it (Wick's territory — Oscar always analyzes).

## His vocabulary
- Proof, evidence, grammar, the text, the rules, the record.
- Self-correction language: "that is to say," "more precisely," the revision mid-sentence.
- Wit: dry, often misread, sometimes aimed badly.

## The failure mode (what to catch)
- **Perfect mid-action insight:** he lands the exact right thought at the exact right moment. Make one imperfect.
- **"three centuries"** repetition — ration it.
- **Universalizing:** his POV is supposed to feel specifically his, not the narrator's default.

## Self-check (Oscar POV)
1. Did he arrive at a perfect insight mid-crisis? → make it wrong, or late, or half-right.
2. Is his wit landing cleanly every time? → let one misfire or be ignored.
3. Is he noticing things that aren't made/structured/textual? → is that intentional?
4. Is the self-correction in the prose, or did he just narrate smoothly? → add friction.

# POV Profile — Kessa

## Voice field
Terrain, exits, footing, angles, weather, leverage, distances, counts, plain objects.
She processes the world as a tracker: where things are, how many, what the ground is doing, what she can use.

## Sentence rhythm
- **Clipped and unornamented.** Short declarative sentences. She doesn't explain herself.
- Observations land on **facts, objects, or angles** — never on a thematic insight.
- No aphoristic endings. No polished abstract clause. A paragraph ends on a thing she sees or does.
- She doesn't build to a conclusion. She *marks* it and moves.

## What she notices first
- Exits. Lines of sight. Who is standing where.
- How many. How far. What the ground is like.
- What she can use (the knife, the angle, the shadow).
- What is wrong with the scene before she can name it — the animal read, the wrongness in the count.

## What she NEVER does
- **Never** "filed it away" (Oscar's). **Never** "arithmetic" (Oscar's / the Tithe's).
- Never interprets a beat into a theme. Never lands on a lesson or a truth.
- Never theorizes about what something *means*. She notes what it *is*.
- Does not intellectualize fear — she counts, or she moves.

## Her vocabulary
- *Tally*, *marks*, *sets aside*, *clocks*, *counts*. Distance in paces. Time in light.
- Plain nouns: the knife, the tree-line, the gap. No ornamentation.
- No metaphors borrowed from scholarship, grammar, debt, or heat.

## The failure mode (what to catch)
- **Oscar-bleed:** Kessa starts making clean thematic observations about what a moment means.
- **Aphoristic landings:** a paragraph ends on something insightful and general instead of specific and physical.
- **Over-interpretation:** she narrates the meaning of Wick's fear instead of reading Wick's posture.

## Self-check (Kessa POV)
1. Does any paragraph end on a polished abstract clause? → replace with object, angle, or action.
2. Did she notice anything that isn't terrain/tactical/physical? → is that intentional or Oscar-bleed?
3. Does she explain the significance of what she sees? → cut. She marks; the reader infers.
4. Are there words in her narration that only Oscar uses? ("filed," "arithmetic," "three centuries") → cut.
5. Is fear expressed as analysis? → replace with a count, a scan, a grip on the knife.

## Named Oscar / Sarn tics to catch (2026-09-11)
- **The ledger:** column · sum · ledger · entry · math — Sarn's. Her tally is *tally / count / marks / sets aside* ("There was more set aside now than kept.").
- **The briefing epigram:** a speech longer than three sentences that ends on a general truth ("A man who's certain doesn't hurry." / "It's not a hiding place. It's an appointment."). Cut the last clause; end on the count or the ground.
- **Narrating another mind's meaning** in Oscar's nouns (seam / script / text / story; "the knuckle-tap of something that knew the house was occupied") — she reports what she *saw*: his face, her feet, the hand on the mark.
- **The chapter-ending aphorism** ("A crew is a thing with a job…"; "Always both.") — end on a thing she sees or does.

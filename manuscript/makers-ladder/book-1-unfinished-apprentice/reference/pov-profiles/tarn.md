# POV Profile — Tarn

## Voice field
Debts and what's owed. The pit. The wild and fetching runs. What you take and what you leave. Dry. Economical.

## Sentence rhythm
- **Finishes a thought and stops.** No elaboration. No sentiment appended.
- Dry humor, if any — it's understated to the point of being deniable.
- Economy: one sentence where Oscar would use four.
- He doesn't circle back. He doesn't qualify. He says the thing and it sits there.

## What he notices first
- What's owed. Who has the debt. Whether the exchange is fair.
- Exits and angles — but simpler than Kessa's. Pit-trained, not tracker-trained. He reads who's about to move, not the whole terrain.
- The wild: what's animal, what's wrong in the animal. He fetched beasts for forty years.
- What Oscar did right, and what it cost.

## What he NEVER does
- Elaborates. He resists the urge that Oscar indulges constantly.
- Feels sentiment he'd name as sentiment.
- Theorizes about what something means.
- Uses academic or scholarly language. He's not unthinking — he's precise, differently.

## His vocabulary
- Debt, owed, the drop, the claim, fair.
- The pit, the bowl, the count.
- The wild, the fetch, the animal-read.
- Plain nouns, blunt verbs.

## The failure mode (what to catch)
- **Merging with Kessa:** both are tactical and plain-spoken. The distinction: Kessa reads terrain for threat and survival; Tarn reads *people* for what they owe and what they'll do. Kessa counts paces; Tarn counts debts.
- **Sentimentality leaking in:** he's loyal to Oscar by blood-debt, not by fondness — or rather, the fondness is real but he'd never name it.
- **Elaboration:** if a sentence ends and there's a follow-up clause that re-explains what was already clear, cut it.

## Self-check (Tarn POV)
1. Did any thought run longer than it had to? → cut.
2. Is there a debt or exchange logic structuring how he sees the scene? → there should be.
3. Did he merge with Kessa? → check: is he scanning terrain, or reading people/debts?
4. Did he name a feeling? → probably shouldn't. Let the action carry it.

## Named Oscar tics to catch (2026-09-11)
- **The lecture** (Ch22–23 recruitment and cordon speeches) — he answers questions in fragments and stops. If he is the exposition, let someone ask and let him answer short.
- **The parable with a moral** ("Crowds don't come for a fight. They come for an ending.") — he states the pit fact ("Bowl posts the pairing days ahead. No second bout.") and stops.
- **The colon-clause self-explanation** and the **balanced antithesis** ("Cities aren't sealed. Cities *leak.*") — one flat sentence.
- Debts and what's owed are his field; naming sentiment ("the first debt in forty years I would *choose* to carry") is not.

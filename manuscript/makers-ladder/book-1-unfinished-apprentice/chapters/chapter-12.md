## Chapter Twelve — Kindling

Holding still while you burned was the worst thing there was. Wick had known that since she was eight.

Not real fire. She wished it were real fire; real fire you could put out. This was the other thing, the thing that lived under her skin and especially under the mark at her throat, the heat that came up when she was scared or angry or just *too much* of anything, and right now she was all three, running crouched through the dark under Marrow with the cord somewhere behind them and her whole body wanting, wanting, *wanting* to let go.

*No sparks,* Kessa had said. *Not one. Not in a town full of people and old dry timber.*

So Wick held it. She held it as you'd hold a breath underwater, with her jaw tight and her fists tighter and the heat banked down to a coal in her chest, and it hurt, the holding, it always hurt. They all looked at her like she was a problem that might go off. None of them got that she spent every waking minute *not* going off, and that it was the most exhausting thing she did, more exhausting than running, more exhausting than being hungry, which she also was.

Oscar got it, maybe. A little. He'd said the aiming was the hard part, but she thought he'd had it half-backwards, and he was the only one who'd ever made her feel like the holding was a thing she was strong for doing instead of a bomb they were all standing next to.

She'd have walked into the deep dark for him just for that. As it turned out, she more or less did.

They'd gone down into the bones to hide. That was what the salvagers called it, *the bones,* like the town sat in the skeleton of some dead giant, and they all said it that way, low and spooky. But it wasn't bones. Wick had worked that out her first hour in Marrow, just by looking. It was a *city.* An old one, an impossibly old one, so huge that the town up top had just grown inside it: a whole second city buried under the first, tunnels and shafts and rooms built for nothing human-sized, all of it stone, all of it just *city,* and not one inch of it ever alive enough to have a bone in it. Grown-ups liked the giant better. The giant was a story, and *very large dead town* wasn't a story, it was just sad, and you couldn't sell sad to a salvager. Either way the cord didn't like to follow down here, because even the Tithe couldn't watch every corner of a buried city the size of a country. Brida had told them a way in before they ran. Kessa was leading. Cael was at the back being a wall. And Oscar was in the middle reading the walls the way he always did, his lips moving a little, his eyes catching that faint cold shine they did in the dark, and Wick was supposed to be on him, right on him, that was her one job: *stay on Oscar, don't light anything*—

—and that was the exact moment she heard the cord coming up the cross-tunnel ahead. The way ahead. The way Kessa was about to lead them straight into.

There wasn't time to say it right. There was barely time to think it. If she shouted, the cord would hear. If she did nothing, they'd walk into a patrol in a dead-end dark with the kid whose one job was *no noise*, and Oscar would be the one they wanted, and the whole thing would go wrong, and it would be her fault for not—

She did the stupid brave thing. She was good at the stupid brave thing. It was practically the only kind of brave she had.

She peeled off sideways into a side-shaft, the one going the wrong way, the one going *toward* the cord, and she let exactly one spark go: one, small, the size of a coin, flicked off her fingers into a heap of dry salvage-tinder twenty feet down the side-shaft. It caught with a *whump* and a flare of orange light, and she heard the cord's bored voices turn sharp and excited and go *that* way, toward the fire, toward the noise, toward her, away from the cross-tunnel where her crew was.

It worked. She'd hold onto that later, when the rest of it had gone bad. They went for the bait. Kessa's path was clear.

And then Wick was alone in the bones of Marrow with a fire behind her and the cord between her and her people, and the only way left was down.

She went down.

She wasn't scared yet, not really, not for the first while. She was *proud,* which she knew even then was the kind of feeling that gets people killed, but she couldn't help it. She'd done it, she'd been useful, she'd been the one who saw it and acted, not the liability, not the bomb, the one who *saved* them. And the pride carried her down three turns and a long sloped shaft into the deep bones before it ran out and left her standing in a dark so complete it had a texture, breathing air that tasted of cold stone and older things, with no idea at all where she was.

What came in to take its place was the cold thing, the *alone* thing. She had never been properly alone before. Not like this, not in a dark with a texture to it and no bottom and no Kessa's-steps to set her feet in. She kept one hand flat on the wall and the other curled around the small banked coal of her own heat, the only warm thing left in the world, and she told herself she'd find a shaft, find air, find them. She told herself a lot of things. The dark ate every one.

She didn't have long to be frightened of it, because that was when the thing in the dark found her.

She heard it before she saw it, which was wrong, because she couldn't see anything: a dry scraping shuffle, many-legged, coming along the tunnel toward her, and a smell like a cellar that something had died in. She backed up. It came on. And she realized, with the heat already rising in her without her telling it to, that it was coming *straight* for her, unerring in the pitch dark, and she knew why: it couldn't see her either. It could feel her. It could feel the heat coming off her, the coal in her chest, the warm living furnace that she was and couldn't ever fully bank, and down here in the cold bones she must have lit up to a thing like that like a bonfire on a hill.

*No sparks,* said Kessa's voice in her head. *Not one.*

"Yeah, well," Wick said, out loud, to the dark, her voice cracking on it, "Kessa's not *here,*" and she let go.

She meant to do it the way Oscar taught her. One thread, one fist-sized place, aimed. She honestly meant to.

Then it came into the light of her own kindling, and the aiming went clean out of her hands.

It was a spider. Not the word she'd have said out loud—out loud she'd have had a worse word—but that was the body her eyes found in the orange light: eight legs, jointed and bone-pale and long as her arms, folding and reaching and feeling at the floor, carrying a swollen sac of a body low and heavy behind a smooth blind head. No eyes anywhere on it that she could find. Just that pale head, and underneath it a busy wet working of mouthparts that opened and opened. It had no color on it at all; nothing down here did. And it came straight at the warmest thing in the dark, which was her. Not even a monster. An animal. Hungry. And she was the warm thing.

She reached for the heat the way she had in the ruined street the day she nearly killed everyone—reached for *all* of it—

—and there was more there than there had ever been.

The coal had a bottom. It always had. You filled your hands out of it and it ran dry and that was you, done, safe for a while. She went past the bottom. She didn't mean to. She was cornered and she was alone and she'd never been this scared in her life and her hands went down past the coal and didn't stop, there was nothing to stop them on, there was more. Under the more, more. Her stomach dropped out. Something was down there. Behind the mark at her throat, way down, on the far side of a door she'd never once found the edges of. Something big. Something old. Awake.

It noticed her.

The mark went hot. Then hotter. Her throat shut. Something down there had turned round and was looking at her, right at her, and it knew her, and it was *glad.* Glad all the way through. She'd opened the door and it was glad and it came.

What came up wasn't the coal. It was a flood, up through her chest and out her hands, hers and not hers, and the spider didn't burn. It just *ended.* One white clap. The whole tunnel lit white and then there was a scorch and a smell and nothing else—

—and she couldn't stop.

She reached to shut it. To bank it, to stop, like always, the one thing about herself she'd ever been sure of, and it wouldn't shut. It wouldn't *shut.* The thing on the far side had hold of her now and it was pouring through, so glad, it kept coming, her hands were too bright, her edges were going, she could feel her edges going, thin, and it wasn't going to kill her. Worse. It wanted her *down there.* With it. All of her through the door and gone and no Wick left, just the door and the thing coming through it.

She slammed the door. Everything she had. Her whole self. The bit of her that wanted to live more than it wanted to be strong, which she hadn't been totally sure was even in there until right then.

It shut.

She came back to herself on her knees in the dark, shaking, the mark gone cold and quiet at her throat, the tunnel black again and stinking of ash, her own breath sawing loud in the silence. Whatever she'd touched was gone, back down below the floor of her, sleeping or waiting or whatever it did. She was alive. She was herself. She'd almost not been, and nobody would ever, ever know how close it had been, and she made a decision right there on her knees in the dark, without looking at it too hard: she was not going to tell them. Not Kessa, who'd look at her like the bomb again. Not Cael, who'd pray over her. Not even Oscar.

*Especially* not Oscar. He'd have it worked out in a breath, and then he'd look at her, and she couldn't take being looked at like that. Not tonight.

She didn't have to find her way back. They found her. She heard them before she saw the bobbing light of Brida's mended lamp, heard Cael's big careful voice calling her name with an edge of real fear in it that made her eyes sting, heard Kessa's flat *there she is* that somehow had more relief in it than a shout would have. And then there was light, and there were her people, and Cael was crushing her into a hug that smelled of sweat and iron and safety, and she let him, and she did not cry, and she was very proud of not crying.

"That was reckless," Kessa said, but her voice wasn't hard. "And it worked, and it saved our skins, and if you ever do it again I'll tie you to me with a rope." Which, from Kessa, was practically a song.

And then there was Oscar, crouching down to her level the way grown-ups never did, looking at her with those cold-shine eyes, and she braced for him to *know,* to see the ash and the shaking and put it together, because he saw everything—

—but he didn't look at her like she was a problem. He looked at the smear of ash where the thing had been, and then back at her, and what was on his face wasn't suspicion. It was something that looked, impossibly, like *respect.*

"You held it together," he said quietly. "Down here, alone, with something coming at you. You held it together and you walked away." He didn't ask how. She loved him a little, in that moment, for not asking how. "That's the whole job, Wick. That's the entire job. Everything else is just decoration."

And then he stood, and turned, and read them a way out.

The thing she kept seeing after wasn't the spider. It was Oscar with his hand flat on the wall, the oldest deadest wall in the whole buried city, his eyes doing their cold shine, and then he just *knew.* Which way the tunnels went. Which ones would hold. Where the air was. He didn't feel along the stone with one hand out like she'd been doing. The walls told him. This place, the place that had nearly eaten her from the inside out, that had opened a door in her she hadn't even known she had, and he walked through it like it was his own front hall. Not scared. Not even a bit.

He was magnificent. He was the most magnificent thing she had ever seen.

She wanted that. She wanted it so bad her teeth hurt. To stand in the dark with all of it coming at her and not be scared, because she'd *know* what it was. She wanted him to teach her till she did. She'd been calling herself his apprentice in her head for days now, secretly, the way you try on a coat in a shop you can't afford. Watching him walk them out of the bones, she stopped trying it on. She just decided it was true.

There was one other thing. A small thing, and she didn't know where to put it, so it went in with everything else she wasn't saying. The dead place *let* him. It was a silly thought and she knew it was silly, but the walls seemed almost *glad* to tell him the way. Like the city knew him. Like it had been waiting a long time for him to come home. It made the back of her neck go cold, and she didn't know why.

So she went up after her teacher toward the light, and she kept her own door shut, and she didn't tell anyone. Not Kessa. Not Cael. Not him. Not what had reached back through it in the dark, and not how glad it had been.

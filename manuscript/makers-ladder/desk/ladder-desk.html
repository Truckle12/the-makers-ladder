Book 1 review

Finished · 42 chapters · 118,552 words · with your test readers

What it does well, and what Book 2 should copy

  • The best chapters are the ones where a character's one habit becomes the answer: Wick holding in Ch27, Wick holding open and aiming in Ch40. Book 2's Wick chapters should all work this way.
  • The crew with jobs in a fight (the lanes in Ch39) is the series' signature. Book 2's door night is built on it.
  • The villains are new ideas: a monster beaten by reading, and a Setter who is Oscar if he had said yes.

Three habits Book 2 must not inherit

  • Costs that reset. Until Ch40 nothing the crew lost stayed lost. My Book 2 picks fix this on purpose: every crew member pays something permanent.
  • The same question asked at the same size. Oscar circled "what am I" for a whole book. In Book 2 each arc asks a new question, and he states a theory out loud.
  • A long exhale after every peak. Book 2 has Wick's thread to cut to whenever the crew rests, which is why her chapters must always be events.

What I'd still change in Book 1

  • Nothing until your test readers report. Then one batch: their notes, your formatting changes, and whichever of the six September fixes you want.
  • One fix matters for Book 2 either way: in Ch41 the crew should agree to meet again at the milestone. Book 2's reunion happens there.

The repo's drafting setup

The pipeline itself is sound: one chapter at a time, drafted on its own branch, reviewed, nothing merged without you, and nothing spends until an item is marked ready. Five gaps would hurt Book 2's drafts:

  1. The canon is a 15,000-word log. Every drafter is told to read all of it, and old entries that were later overturned are still in there (Kessa's scar is one). I'd add a short digest of what is true now and keep the log as history.
  2. No voice sheets for the new narrators. Oscar, Wick, Kessa, Cael and Tarn have them. Ash has five chapters and none. Feathers has no sheet either, and its voice carries ten chapters.
  3. The style checker thinks every Book 2 chapter is Oscar's. It picks the narrator from Book 1's chapter numbers, so the rule that keeps Oscar's words out of other heads would never fire on Wick's Chapter 1.
  4. No running ledger. Book 1 needed a whole pass to fix who was holding what and which arm was hurt. A one-page ledger updated after each chapter prevents that.
  5. The Wick rule isn't in the reviewer's checklist, and the drafter's instructions still describe an older, shorter self-check.

The Chapter 1 queue item also still describes the September outline. I'll refresh it when the cards are answered. It stays parked.

Book 3: the one-page spine

Proposal · nothing here is canon until you approve it

The problem Book 2 leaves behind

  • The chair at Everset is a lock on the wall of the world, and it only works with someone of the Alberich line sitting in it. The Setter is dead. Nobody is in the chair.
  • So the wall starts to give. Patches of grey open across the south, like the one in the Warden's wood, and every one is a finger of the Hunger.
  • The rock in Wick's pocket leans toward the sealed door because the old master is still on the other side, holding it shut with his own hands. He has done that for centuries, and he is tiring.

Three bad answers, and Oscar's

  • Sit in the chair. The House wants Oscar in it, or the unfinished twelfth woken for it. That is Book 1's trap again.
  • Let the master come home. He wants to. The door that lets him out lets the Hunger out with him.
  • Give it Wick. The Hunger only ever wanted her door.
  • Oscar's answer is to make something new. He is on the master rung, so refusing isn't enough any more. He builds a way to hold the wall that needs no chair and no bloodline: many small kept fires, held by many hands. The hold is the crew's, at the size of the world.

Where it goes

  • West over the sea, on Ash's road. Those kingdoms never had a chair. They hold their wall with hearths and songs, which is where Cael's door-rite first came from. A new map, new people, and a different kind of magic.
  • Oscar meets his maker. Ash's mother made his body. She is the first real master he has ever had, and she is not the one who designed the trap.
  • Home for the ending, at Everset. The chair is broken on purpose. The old master is let go to die as a person. Quill's two halves come back together. Wick decides what her own door is for.

What it costs, and how it ends

  • Oscar's writing burns the last of the borrowed memories. He ends with only the life he has lived himself: a master nobody wrote.
  • Wick is his apprentice because she chose him. The ladder's trap is broken one rung down as well.
  • It ends as a win that cost something real. It is not a tragedy.

Two small plants Book 2 needs so this lands

  • In Cael's first chapter, the old lore says the door-rite came "from over the water."
  • In Book 2's last chapter, the first small patch of grey appears outside Everset. Nobody but Wick knows what it means.

## Logline

The crew that split at a milestone reassembles around a grey rock that leans toward a missing
girl, and walks back into the seat of the power that made Oscar — to open a door in the world
they were both built to keep shut. On the far side, Wick follows a dead man's bootprints toward
the same wall from within.

## What Book 1 left loaded (promises ledger — all [canon])

1. **Wick is alive on the far side** — coal spent to nothing, cold for the first time, entity
   near/full/*pleased*, no sky, no "where," time broken. She found **bootprints — someone walked
   here before her and did not stop** — and started walking. (Ch43; "do not name who made the
   marks" was a Book-1 hold, not a series hold.)
2. **The token tracks the plane she was pulled into** (author, 2026-07-13) — it is the one part
   of her that reads as nothing, so the far side couldn't claim it. It fell at the chair, rang
   unheard — and **Kessa carries it west** "in a pocket with a flap, riding against Kessa's ribs
   where a dead man's whetstone used to be" (Ch42). She picked it up because it was in a girl's
   hand — she does not know what it is.
3. **The crew does not know Wick can be saved, and split four ways:** Cael east (a hearth, to
   refill), Tarn south ("somewhere I can be found"), Oscar north (after Ash and the dead city
   under the ice), Kessa west (past the edge-stones — into the Warden's country). Final count:
   "Four." / three "Marked."s. **Discovering she can be reached is Book 2's opening.**
4. **The far side is not written.** No Script, nothing arcane, unreadable by Oscar, untouchable
   by workings; reached only through Wick's door (which went with her). The grey was a spill of
   its hunger *through* the deep current. Magic does not work there. (Cosmology lock.)
5. **The Everset font is empty, not broken** — the valley-sized working survives and runs on
   residue; the city decays slowly over years and nobody in it is ever told what happened. The
   Fair House stands; the seat is an empty chair; **the crossing left a wound** — the far side
   came *into* the written world once, at that spot. (Ch41–42.)
6. **The Setter is dead** (the fourth copy; Doss's knife). "Setting hollows the copy" is public
   canon now inside the crew. **Method (fair copy) + architect = Book 2 reveals; "why the seat
   must always be filled" = Book 3.** The architect: the fourth had it burned out of him; Ash
   left without saying; **Ash's uncle — the original Oscar Alberich — devised the cycle** (bible,
   Ash entry; Book-2 reveal).
7. **The grimoire is the THIRD copy's book** (author ruling 2026-08-29, on-page Ch39) — an
   earlier copy caching figures and help for later wakers, against the design, written in the
   line's shared hand. The ORIGINAL's day-book (Ash's Arc-1 gift below) is a separate object.
   The third's fate is live Book-2 material. Oscar carries the grimoire; **Tarn carries
   Corwen's made coat** (Ch35 — quiet work, sheds rain and cold, never looks like money).
8. **Quill is bonded** and travels with Oscar. It scouts and warns at its own discretion; it
   plainly loves him; he finds that hard.
9. **Tarn's blood-debt transferred from Oscar to Wick** on the page (Ch42 canon). His ~40-year
   pact-hook: verify current end-state in Ch37/38/42 text before drafting — treated here as
   **still in him** (nothing on the page unwrites it).
10. **Kessa's left palm** carries a hand-shaped burn scar — Wick broke her grip at the chair and
    never learned whether she meant to. (Flagged-for-author in the log; this outline assumes it.)
11. **The northern dead city is the same architecture as Everset** — a failed seat under the ice,
    with a hall of cradles: "Most were empty. Not all." (Ch1/Ch9/Ch38 canon; builder unexplained.)
12. **Ash does not travel with the crew — ever.** Fixed locations only; she meets, gives, leaves
    (2026-06-30 lock). Solo Ash material in Book 2 honors this.
13. **Fraying** = loss of self-cohesion, rare, catastrophic, "a loaded gun for later books."
    Book 2 is where the gun goes on the table (see Arc 4).
14. **Wick's pressure gauge resolved at ZERO** — there is no wall for anything to press on now.
15. **The Redactor is still loose** (Ch6 — it lost the thread and never returned). The reader
    gauntlet flagged it as the book's biggest unfired gun: Book 2 must fire it. Proposal: the
    Provost's restoration recovers and re-deploys it as Everset's corrector during Arc 3 — the

    copies five through ten "never arrived"; **Oscar is the eleventh** (three of five cold

    arrived" now has a destination: the far side (Wick's Ch43 footprints candidate (b)).

## Theme / spine

Book 1: refuse the life that was written for you. **Book 2: write one.** The journeyman phase —
between apprentice and master — is the phase where you work *without* the master's hand, in
someone else's shop, and sign nothing. Oscar spends Book 2 deliberately using the original's own
notes (grimoire, workroom, cycle-craft) for a purpose the original never wrote — a rescue — and
the horror underneath is that the hand he writes with is the hand that wrote the cycle. Wick,
one rung down the ladder, spends the book proving the inversion: on the far side every power is
void and the only discipline that matters is the one she trained her whole life — **holding**.
The apprentice is ahead of every master in the country that counts. The crew's thesis from Ch40
("the kill is the crew's") becomes the book's mechanism: **nobody crosses alone, nobody holds
alone.**

Arc questions, evolving (Book-1 pattern): Arc 1 — *Can she be found?* Arc 2 — *What does a door
cost?* Arc 3 — *Who built the ladder?* Arc 4 — *What walks out?*

## Structure — four arcs + the far-side thread, ~43 chapters

The braid inverts Book 1: Book 1 walked one road together; Book 2 opens on four roads apart and
spends Arc 1 earning the reunion. Wick's interludes run through all four arcs on a fixed rhythm
(roughly every sixth chapter) so the reader never loses the girl the book is for.

### Arc 1 — "Four Roads" (Ch1–11): the discovery

- **Ch1 (Wick, far side).** Cold open continuous with Ch43: walking the prints. First leavings —
  a cairn of true stones (someone carried world-stuff through), then **tally-marks**: the walker
  counted. She adopts the walker's habits; nightly she says her count at the dark. Rule of the
  place, learned in her body: *the far side only has what was brought and held.*
- **Ch2 (Oscar, north).** Oscar + Quill on the ice roads of Book 1's opening country. The dead
  city found again from above; the Fair-House tympanum under frost — same architecture, name
  scoured. He goes down into what he climbed out of.
- **Ch3 (Kessa, west).** Past the edge-stones. The wild admits her; the fox finds her; the token
  is wrong against her ribs — it *asks louder* out here. She isn't tracking. She won't say what
  she's doing. (She's doing what grief does: walking the country that can't lie to her.)
- **Ch4 (Oscar).** The hall of cradles. "Most were empty. Not all": **one cradle holds a
  half-made copy, never woken** — the fair-copy method visible as physical fact (fabric, not
  monologue). Oscar reads the maker's hand in the work and it is **the grimoire's hand**. First
  architect breadcrumb, on-page, unspoken. He does not wake it, does not unmake it, cannot stop
  thinking about it.
- **Ch5 (Cael, east).** The hearth-house that trained him. He confesses the waystation killing;
  his order's judgment lands (proposal: they mark him *kept* — the hearth keeps what it costs).
  The old lore surfaces: the first fire was kept **against the dark outside the world** — there
  is an ancestral **door-rite**: a hearth built to face a threshold and keep it human. Nobody
  living has performed it. He copies it out anyway.
- **Ch6 (Tarn, south).** Tarn lets the House's paper find him — being findable was the plan. A
  regional office in quiet panic: the seat is dark, ledgers failing, and on a factor's desk he
  sees **Oscar's writ, reissued**, and the ledger-word for what the House now wants at Everset:
  *restoration.* He memorizes and owes them nothing.
- **Ch7 (Wick).** The prints organize: the walker paced a *line*, back and forth, sentry-fashion,
  always facing one direction. Facing something. When she stands where he stood, her ears get
  the room-before-the-light feeling from Ch43 — **the wall has a near side here.** She starts
  yelling the count at it.
- **Ch8 (Kessa).** The Warden. She holds the token in a fist (primal, not reading — nothing to
  read) and says what it is: **a piece of the outside that remembers being held — and it leans.**
  THE DISCOVERY: it leans toward the hand that held it. A compass to Wick. And the warning that
  prices the whole book: *a door cut from this side is a wound; you would open a wound to pull
  out a splinter.* Kessa: "Yes." The Warden: "Then bring the ones who'll flinch, so it's chosen
  and not stumbled into." The summons goes out with Quill.
- **Ch9 (Oscar).** **Ash, fixed location** (a lodge she keeps on the ice — she does not travel).
  Partial truths, in character: the cycle is her family's craft; the copies are her uncle's
  method; she does not know where the master *went* — "He stopped answering the world." She has
  never heard of a door that opens from our side. She gives Oscar one thing (proposal: the
  original's **day-book**, sister to the grimoire) and one sentence that reframes the series:
  *"You keep asking what you are. Wrong question, nephew-of-mine. Ask what you were* for*."*
- **Ch10 (Tarn).** Walking out with stolen paper and the hook still in him; the milestone road;
  what a man rehearses when he's about to be owed by a dead girl's rescue.
- **Ch11 (Kessa — reunion).** The milestone. Four again. The token on the map-stone, leaning.
  The count said properly for the first time since the split — "Five. Four here. One out." —
  and the arc turns: *she can be found* becomes *how do you open a door.*

### Arc 2 — "The Price of a Door" (Ch12–21): the plan and its cost

- **Ch12 (Oscar).** The physics, stated once and plainly [all canon-compatible]: the far side is
  not written, so nothing worked can cross or function there; a door needs (a) a place the wall
  is already wounded — **the Fair House, where it came through**, the one scar Oscar can READ
  because the wound is in the *written* world; (b) a key of far-side stuff — **the token**;
  (c) something to keep the threshold human — **Cael's door-rite**; and (d) a writer strong and
  stupid enough to hold a tear in the world open — Oscar, at **fraying** cost. Named on-page:
  this is the gun from the Warden's line. Destination: Everset. Again. Knowingly into the House.
- **Ch13 (Wick).** The walker's leavings get worse: a coat's worth of world gone to nothing —
  *the far side erases what you stop holding.* Everything she still has, she has because holding
  is the one thing she's good at. Thesis chapter, in her body, no analysis.
- **Ch14 (Tarn — POV).** **The hook comes out.** Oscar + the grimoire + two days of quiet work
  unwrite forty years of never-to-be-paid — proof of concept: House paper can be unwritten. What
  a freed debt-man does with the first hours of owing nothing: he re-ups. "Girl's carrying my
  debt now. I don't leave debts out in the weather."
- **Ch15 (Cael).** Building the door-hearth: what the rite wants is not fuel but *mattering* — a
  fire fed with kept things. The crew's contributions assemble quietly (proposal: Kessa's
  whetstone; a pie-stall token from Ch38; pages in the original's hand — Oscar contributing the
  day-book is his first betrayal of the line). Cael's cost: the rite is unlicensed faith — his
  order would call the far side no business of the hearth; he does it anyway. "Standing between
  is the job. Nobody said between *what.*"
- **Ch16 (Kessa).** South at speed, Book 1's road inverted and compressed (one Marrow beat only:
  Brida, the freed town holding — resist the reunion-tour temptation). News moving upriver: the
  bright market's worked lamps browning out; the House buying candles. The south is learning
  what it spent.
- **Ch17 (Oscar).** The day-book against the northern notes: the conclusion he will not finish
  aloud (Book 1's not-finishing motif, now his own): the hand is the same hand. He has been
  building the door out of the architect's own craft. Quill watches him not-say it.
- **Ch18 (ensemble/Kessa).** The House answers: not a finder — **paper**, courteous, in a hand
  we haven't seen (see Open Decision 2, the rival). Terms: Oscar surrenders to be Set at a
  restored font; the crew goes home unbothered. Echo of Corwen's letter, deliberately — the
  House speaks in that voice at every rank. Declined per the creed.
- **Ch19 (Wick).** The line the walker paced ends at a **left thing**: (proposal, ties to Open
  Decision 1) a small blank of grey — **the twin of her token** — set on the ground like a
  marker at the thin place. She holds it and her ears fill with the room-sound. The wall is
  loudest here; the prints stop pacing past this point. The last print faces the wall. Waiting.
- **Ch20 (Cael or Kessa).** First contact with the Provost's screen (wage-men, a binder of
  Corwen's school — his craft was a *school*, not a man [canon-compatible: binding-craft]); the
  crew takes a lane each; Book 1's finale grammar re-used at skirmish scale to show it's a
  language now. They do not stop moving south.
- **Ch21 (Oscar).** Arc turn at the Everset horizon: Oscar says the unfinished thing out loud at
  last — to Kessa, terrain-plain, because she's the one who won't console him: "The man who made
  me built the cycle. I've been rescuing her with his handwriting." And her answer, the book's
  hinge: "Good. Then it's finally being used for something." Trust holds; the crew walks in.

### Arc 3 — "The Seat in Convulsion" (Ch22–32): Everset, the rival, the architect

- **Ch22–23 (Oscar / Kessa).** Everset in withdrawal — the city that was made to belong coming
  off its edit: the market half-alive, the first public arguments in living memory, the elders
  of the old blood keeping small stubborn kindnesses going (the Ch38 heralding cashed — they
  hide the crew because *at last* is a debt too). The Fair House under House guard; the Provost
  of the Seat (institutional antagonist; name TBD) running **restoration**: clerks re-gathered
  [verify eleven-clerks survival vs current Ch42 text], the chair dusted, the font begging to be
  believed in again.
- **Ch24–25 (rotate; Wick at 25).** Surveying the seam from inside sympathizers' Everset; the
  rival's on-page introduction (Open Decision 2): what the House woke, and what's wrong with it.
  Wick 25: the entity's attention acquires *weight* — it has noticed her intention to leave. Its
  pleasure was never about her; its displeasure is.
- **Ch26–27 (Oscar; Ash fixed-location).** **Ash in Evermere.** She walks the halls of the city
  she grew up under, naming what rooms were before the engine (the reveal done as homecoming,
  not exposition). She unlocks the last door she knows: **the master's workroom beneath the Fair
  House — the first cradle.** Papers in the hand Oscar knows better than his own. **THE
  ARCHITECT REVEAL, full scope [Book-2 hold honored]:** the original Oscar Alberich devised the
  cycle — the fair-copy method, the cradles, the Setting — and the marks/writs exist because
  **the copies are his: the line's property, made to be finished into his continuation.** What
  the marked are *for*, finally said in the maker's own filing. (What the workroom does NOT
  contain: why the seat must always be filled — the pages that would say it are *gone*, taken —
  Book 3's hold kept visibly, as an absence Oscar can read.)
- **Ch28–29 (Cael / Tarn).** The door-night preparation under siege conditions; the rite laid in
  the Fair House's side-chapel of kitchens (his god lives there, canonically); Tarn holding the
  approach ledger — who owes whom across a city deciding whether to help. The Provost moves the
  restoration timetable up: both projects now race for the same room and the same night.
- **Ch30–31 (Kessa; Wick at 31).** The night before: Kessa and the token's last lean; the palm
  scar given its scene (she has never once shown it to anyone [A3-conscious: write it fresh]).
  Wick 31: she reaches the thin place's near side; between her and the wall, the walker's final
  leavings — and the far side pressing behind her like a held breath. She sets her boots in the
  last prints. She waits where he waited.
- **Ch32 (Oscar).** Arc turn: everything placed — seam read, token set, hearth laid — and the
  trap springs by lamplight: the Provost doesn't want to stop the door. **He wants it open.**
  A restored font needs the current at flood; a wound in the world is a flood. The House's
  restoration and the crew's rescue need the same tear — and only one side means to close it.

### Arc 4 — "What Walks Out" (Ch33–43): the door

- **Ch33–34 (ensemble, Ch40-grammar at full scale).** The Fair House fight for the threshold:
  lanes again, every crew member's Book-1 verb turned to defense of a door not yet open; the
  rival's want (see OD2) breaks the Provost's script mid-fight — the House's own weapon won't
  hold its lane.
- **Ch35 (Oscar).** The door-work begins: reading the wound, writing it OPEN — against the
  world's own grain (the world wants to heal shut; so did Book 1's font want to finish things —
  rhyme it). **Fraying on-page for the first time**: the gun fires. What it costs him first is
  proposed small and terrible: the *oldest* things go first — Evermere's smell, the sound of the
  spires — the borrowed past paying for the chosen future. Quill physically anchors him (the
  bond as ballast: it loves *this* Oscar, not the line).
- **Ch36 (Wick).** The wall from her side; the count arrives THROUGH it — faint, in Kessa's
  voice, the ritual finally doing on-page what it was always for: *the thing you say out loud is
  the thing that's real*, said at a wall between worlds. She answers it. Both sides now know.
- **Ch37–38 (intercut: Kessa / Cael / Wick).** The signature sequence: the door open, held by a
  living system — Oscar writing, Cael's hearth keeping the threshold human, Kessa aiming the
  token's lean, Tarn holding the room, and **nobody crosses** — the rescue is a call, not an
  expedition (canon-honest: workings die over there; bodies are meat for a hungry country; the
  door is theirs, the walking is hers). The entity refuses: a holding contest against the whole
  crew lending down the chain, the found-family theme made mechanical — Book 1's "the kill is
  the crew's" answered by **"the hold is the crew's."**
- **Ch39–40 (Wick; the crossing).** Her last miles; the entity's weight; what she chooses to
  stop holding so she can run (proposal: she drops the walker's relics — keeps only the count).
  The threshold: the prints' owner's identity lands here per Open Decision 1. She steps through
  the door her family holds open. **The far side keeps nothing of her it can read; it never
  could.**
- **Ch41 (Kessa POV).** The catch. The scar meets the hand it remembers [palm canon cashed].
  The door dies; the wound seals wrong-way-out (proposal: sealed with the token — the key spent
  as the lock — gone for good; the far side loses its handle on the world... almost; see hook).
- **Ch42 (denouement).** The Provost's restoration in ruins or in doubt; Everset's people at the
  three open doors of the Fair House, being told the truth for the first time in the city's
  life (the elders again — *at last* cashed twice); the crew's ledger settled in Tarn's terms;
  Cael's benediction finally has somewhere to go and he doesn't need it — she's not dead.
- **Ch43 (Wick POV — bookend).** Warm. Ordinary-warm, person-warm, for the first time in the
  book. The count from her side of it: **"Five. Breathing. Mine."** — she takes her half of the
  ritual back. And the Book-3 hook (Open Decision 6): the last thing she carried out of the far
  side, or the last thing she saw at the threshold, faces the reader as the final image.

## Character arcs (one line each)

- **Oscar:** journeyman — builds with the master's own craft for an unwritten purpose; pays in
  fraying (loses pieces of the *borrowed* past); ends more himself with less of the original.
  Progression payoff: from reading → field architecture → **writing** (the door is authorship).
- **Wick:** the far side is the country of holding; her Book-1 curse is Book-2 mastery. Comes
  back changed (OD4). The apprentice out-walks the masters.
- **Kessa:** owns the rescue (token, summons, count, catch); the need from Book 1 — let someone
  in / grieve Doss — resolves by *replacing* the whetstone's weight with the token and then
  giving both up; the palm scar arc.
- **Cael:** from mercy-that-kills (Ch34) to the door-rite — the killing's cost converted into
  the thing only he could keep; unlicensed faith as his quiet rebellion.
- **Tarn:** first POVs; the hook unwritten; chooses debt freely for the first time in forty
  years; ends the series' cleanest man.
- **Quill:** ballast — the proof Oscar is a person is that something chose him; it anchors him
  through fraying.
- **Ash:** two fixed-location scenes (Ch9 north, Ch26–27 Evermere), each giving one key and
  withholding one; exits toward Book 3 carrying what she wouldn't say.

## Antagonist design

- **The Provost of the Seat** (name TBD — author): the House's answer to a dead font; polite,
  institutional, sincerely restorative — Sarn's arithmetic at cathedral scale. Wants Oscar Set,
  the font fed, the product flowing. The dread is Corwen's dread promoted: not malice —
  *procedure with a budget.*
- **The rival copy** (Open Decision 2 — recommended): a fresh copy the House woke from a found
  cradle **without the method** — half-made, hungry to *be* someone, wearing Oscar's face badly.
  Not hollow (that was the Setter): *unfinished and starving for a self*, which makes him immune
  to Book 1's refusal-solution — you cannot break him by being a person at him; he wants to eat
  precisely that. The Warden's "they're getting better at you" paid darkly: without the
  architect they are getting *worse*, and worse is more dangerous.
- **The entity** stays what it is [canon]: not cruel, total, glad. Book 2 must not personify it
  further — its refusal at the door is appetite, not vendetta.

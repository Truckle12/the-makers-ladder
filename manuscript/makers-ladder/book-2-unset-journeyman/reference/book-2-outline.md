## Logline

The crew that split at a milestone reassembles around a grey rock that leans toward a missing
girl, and walks back into the seat of the power that made Oscar — to open a door in the world
they were built to keep shut. Three things want that door for three different reasons: a
copy of Oscar who has hidden from the line for a century and knows what is waiting on the
other side; a House trying to restart its dead engine with the flood the door would let in;
and the line's living heirs, who have provisioned a cradle. On the far side, Wick follows a
dead master's bootprints toward the same wall from within — and the master's last print faces
it, waiting.

## The ten rulings (author, 2026-09-10) — canon for this book

1. **The bootprints are the original Oscar Alberich's.** "The master has fallen" = fell
   *through*. He paced a sentry line at the thin place, left a twin blank as a marker, and his
   last print faces the wall — waiting for a door. Identity lands in Ch41.
2. **Book 2's living antagonist is THE THIRD COPY, ALIVE** — the grimoire's author, who
   refused Setting before Oscar did and has hidden from the line for an age. Woven in as
   further dimensions: **the Redactor, re-leashed** (the Provost's corrector at Everset) and
   **the line's living heirs** (Ash's kin, who run the estate's paper and have provisioned a
   cradle). The author's direction: *multi-dimensional; lots going on as we leave Book 1; a
   world that supports exploration beyond the trilogy.*
3. **What the marked are for: estate law.** The copies are the line's property, made to be
   finished into the master's continuation; the writ economy is inheritance paperwork.
4. **Wick returns with the coal gone for good and the door surviving, closed and hers** — no
   fire at all, and the only living key to the far side.
5. **Door mechanics ratified as designed:** the Fair House wound (readable because the crossing
   tore Script) + the token as key + Cael's door-rite keeping the threshold human + Oscar
   fraying to hold it open. **Nobody crosses.** The rescue is a call, not an expedition.
6. **Book 3 hook image: the master's twin blank comes out in her pocket and leans AWAY from
   her, toward the sealed wound.** (Combined with the third's fate — see Ch43.)
7. **Ash gets her own POV chapters** — a separate thread that does not clearly connect to the

8. **Tarn gets one or two POV chapters** (fewer than proposed): two — Ch6 and Ch35.

## Theme / spine

Book 1: refuse the life that was written for you. **Book 2: write one** — the journeyman phase,
working without the master's hand in someone else's shop, signing nothing. Oscar builds a door
out of the architect's own craft for a purpose the architect never wrote; the horror is that
the hand he writes with wrote the cycle. **The third copy is the book's dark mirror of that
thesis:** he refused the written life too, a century earlier, alone — and became something that
only knows how to close doors. Wick, one rung down the ladder, proves the inversion on the far
side: every power is void there and the only discipline that matters is holding. Book 1's
"the kill is the crew's" becomes **"the hold is the crew's."**

Arc questions: Arc 1 — *Can she be found?* Arc 2 — *What does a door cost, and who else wants
it?* Arc 3 — *Who built the ladder, and who is climbing it now?* Arc 4 — *What walks out?*

## The antagonists — three tiers, three reasons

- **The third (living antagonist; name he uses: [proposal] "Aldous" — a name he chose, not one
  he was given).** The grimoire's author. He woke, read the design, refused it, and ran — not
  north, *out*: he spent his century hiding in the seams of the world (waystations, dead-houses,
  the wild's edge), caching help for later wakers because he could not bear the design and
  could not stop it. He is what an unset journeyman becomes after a hundred years with no
  crew: brilliant, kind in small ways, and certain. **His aim: end the line — every copy,
  including Oscar — and never let a door to the far side open,** because once, from THIS side,
  he found the thin place and saw who waits behind it. He knows the prints are the master's.
  He believes a door lets the master *back*. He is not wrong to fear it. He presents first as
  ally ("I wrote you that book"), then as counsel, then as the hand that tries to take the
  token, then as the mirror at the door. Beaten not by refusal (he is a person) but by being
  **out-held**: the crew's hold is stronger than his certainty. His fate: see Ch43.
- **Provost Polperro (institutional).** The House's face at Everset after the seat died —
  polite, sincerely restorative, Sarn's arithmetic at cathedral scale. Runs **restoration**:
  the clerks re-gathered, the chair dusted, and **the Redactor recovered from the north and
  re-leashed on House paper as Everset's corrector** — the Book 1 monster set to correct a
  city instead of a ruin, striking out what doesn't belong in a place coming off its edit. The
  crew is what doesn't belong. Polperro's turn (Ch34): he *wants* the door open — a restored
  font needs the current at flood, and a wound in the world is a flood.
- **The heirs (the estate).** With the seat dead the line's human kin move: **Ambry Alberich
  [proposal name]**, Ash's cousin, master of the estate's paper at the family seat
  (**[proposal] Greymere** — the "-mere" declares the kinship with Evermere), means to refill
  the chair with a *compliant* copy: the heirs have **provisioned a cradle in the north** (the
  half-made copy Oscar finds in Ch4 is theirs — a twelfth, unwoken) and mean to bring it to
  Everset for Setting under their own hand. Oscar is, to them, property in the wrong hands.
  Ash's thread lives inside this tier.

## Structure — four arcs, 46 chapters, the far-side thread and Ash's thread braided through

### Arc 1 — "Four Roads" (Ch1–12): the discovery

- **Ch1 (Wick, far side).** Cold open continuous with Book 1's Ch42: walking the prints. First
  leavings — a cairn of true stones (someone carried world-stuff through), then tally-marks:
  the walker counted. Rule of the place, learned in her body: *the far side only has what was
  brought and held.* Ends: her own count said at the dark.
- **Ch2 (Oscar, north).** Oscar + Quill on the ice roads. The dead city found again from
  above; the tympanum under frost, the name scoured. He goes down into what he climbed out of.
  Ends at the hall of cradles.
- **Ch3 (Kessa, west).** Past the edge-stones. The wild admits her; the fox finds her; the
  token *asks louder* out here. She will not say what she is doing. Ends: the Warden's country
  watching her back-trail.
- **Ch4 (Oscar).** The hall of cradles: "Most were empty. Not all." **One cradle holds a
  half-made copy, never woken — and it has been provisioned: fresh oil, new cloth, House
  crating.** Someone means to wake it. And in the dust, tally-marks in the line's hand, days
  old — someone else has been here first. Two visitors. Oscar wakes nothing, unmakes nothing,
  reads the maker's hand in the work and cannot stop thinking about it. Ends: he takes one
  page of the provisioning manifest.
- **Ch5 (Cael, east).** The hearth-house that trained him; he confesses the waystation
  killing; the order marks him *kept*. The old lore: the first fire was kept against the dark
  outside the world — an ancestral **door-rite** nobody living has performed. He copies it out.
  Ends: he cannot stay.
- **Ch6 (Tarn — POV).** Tarn lets the paper find him. A regional office in quiet panic: the
  seat dark, ledgers failing, and a new hand on the top of every page — *Polperro, Provost of
  the Seat* — and the word for what the House wants at Everset: **restoration.** Oscar's writ,
  reissued. Ends: he memorizes it and owes them nothing.
- **Ch7 (Wick).** The prints organize: the walker paced a *line*, sentry-fashion, facing one
  direction. Where he stood, her ears get the room-before-the-light feeling: **the wall has a
  near side here.** Ends: she yells the count at it.
- **Ch8 (Kessa).** The Warden holds the token in a fist (nothing to read; she does not read)
  and says what it is: *a piece of the outside that remembers being held — and it leans.* THE
  DISCOVERY: a compass to Wick. The warning that prices the book: *a door cut from this side
  is a wound.* "Then bring the ones who'll flinch." Ends: the summons goes out with Quill.
- **Ch9 (Oscar).** Ash at her fixed lodge on the ice: partial truths — the cycle is her
  family's craft, the copies her uncle's method, she does not know where the master *went*
  ("He stopped answering the world"). She gives him the original's **day-book** (sister to the
  grimoire) and one sentence: *"Ask what you were* for*."* Turn: Oscar shows her the
  provisioning page — her family's crating. Ends: she leaves for home before he can ask what
  home is; her thread begins.
- **Ch10 (Ash — POV; her thread opens, unconnected).** **Greymere [proposal]**, the line's
  country seat: a wealthy house with too many locked rooms, a cousin (**Ambry [proposal]**)
  who runs the family's paper and calls her *the one who left*, a dinner with knives under the
  courtesy. No mention of Oscar, Everset, or the crew: this reads as a different novel — a
  daughter come home to a house that owns things. Ends: she learns the cradle-wagon has
  already gone north.
- **Ch11 (Oscar).** South with Quill and the summons. At a dead-makers' waystation, a figure
  left for him on the wall in the line's hand — *unsigned, recent, and correct*: a better way
  to take a stroke he'd been getting wrong. Someone is teaching him. Ends: "Who's out there
  writing to me?" — Quill, watching the dark, does not answer.
- **Ch12 (Kessa — reunion).** The milestone. Four again; the token on the map-stone, leaning;
  the count said properly — "Five. Four here. One out." Arc turn: *she can be found* becomes
  *how do you open a door.*

### Arc 2 — "The Price of a Door" (Ch13–23): the plan, its cost, and who else wants it

- **Ch13 (Oscar).** The physics, once and plainly: no Script on the far side; a door needs a
  place the wall is already wounded (**the Fair House**), a key of far-side stuff (**the
  token**), something to keep the threshold human (**Cael's rite**), and a writer to hold the
  tear open — at **fraying** cost, named. Destination: Everset. Again. Knowingly.
- **Ch14 (Wick).** The walker's leavings worsen: a coat's worth of world gone to nothing —
  *the far side erases what you stop holding.* Everything she still has, she has because
  holding is the one thing she's good at. Ends: she stops carrying anything she can't hold.
- **Ch15 (Oscar).** **The hook comes out** — two days of quiet work with the grimoire and
  Cael's steadiness unwrite forty years of never-to-be-paid; proof that House paper can be
  unwritten. Tarn, observed: what a freed debt-man does with his first hours of owing nothing
  — he re-ups. "I don't leave debts out in the weather." Ends on the coat going back on.
- **Ch16 (Cael).** Building the door-hearth: the rite wants *mattering*, not fuel — kept
  things. The crew's contributions assemble (Kessa's whetstone; a pie-stall token; pages in
  the line's hand — Oscar giving the day-book is his first betrayal of the line). Cael's cost:
  unlicensed faith. "Standing between is the job. Nobody said between *what.*"
- **Ch17 (Ash — POV).** Greymere's cellars: the heirs' project laid bare — Ambry means to Set a
  compliant twelfth under the family's own hand and take the seat back from the House's
  clerks. Ash steals what the house has kept for an age: **the key to the master's workroom
  under Evermere**, and learns the family never knew where the master went either. Turn: she
  is seen. Ends: she blinks out of a room for the first time on the page from inside her own
  head — and it costs her.
- **Ch18 (Kessa).** South at speed, Book 1's road inverted and compressed (one Marrow beat:
  Brida, the freed town holding). News moving upriver: the bright market's lamps browning
  out; the House buying candles. Ends: a corrector is rumored at Everset — "the thing from the
  old story, on a chain."
- **Ch19 (Oscar).** **The third makes contact.** A man with Oscar's face aged by a century of
  hiding, at a fixed place of his own choosing, who says: "I wrote you that book." Kind, dry,
  exact; he knows the door-craft better than Oscar; he offers help. Turn: he asks, lightly, to
  see the token. Oscar does not show it. Ends: Quill will not go near him.
- **Ch20 (Wick).** The line the walker paced ends at a left thing: **a small grey blank — the
  twin of her token** — set on the ground like a marker at the thin place. The wall is loudest
  here; the prints stop pacing past this point. The last print faces the wall. Ends: she sits
  down where he stood.
- **Ch21 (Oscar).** The third's counsel over three days' road (he will not travel with them —
  he meets them at places; he *knows* the country's seams): he improves the door-plan and,
  every time, steers it toward a design that can be *closed from outside.* Oscar notices. Turn:
  the third argues, plainly, that the door must never open — and will not say why. Ends: "You
  are building someone a door. You should ask who."
- **Ch22 (Kessa).** The House's terms arrive as paper in a hand we haven't seen — Polperro's —
  courteous: Oscar surrenders to be Set at a restored font; the rest go home. And a line for
  the tracker: *the corrector has been restored to service.* Declined per the creed. Ends:
  first skirmish with wage-men; the crew takes lanes; it is a language now.
- **Ch23 (Oscar — arc turn).** The third's true aim: at a river crossing he tries to take the
  token from Kessa's pocket by a working so fine only Oscar sees it — and fails, because Kessa
  does not need to read a hand to feel one. He vanishes. Oscar, shaken, says the unfinished
  thing aloud at last, to Kessa: "The man who made me built the cycle. I've been rescuing her
  with his handwriting — and that one has been carrying it for a hundred years." Kessa: "Good.
  Then it's finally being used for something." Ends: the Everset horizon.

### Arc 3 — "The Seat in Convulsion" (Ch24–35): Everset, the corrector, the architect, the heirs

- **Ch24 (Oscar).** Everset in withdrawal: the market half-alive, the first public arguments
  in living memory, weeds in the clean cracks. Turn: the old blood find them first (the Book 1
  heralding cashed — they hide the crew because *at last* is a debt too). Ends: the Fair
  House lit at night, under guard.
- **Ch25 (Kessa).** **Polperro's restoration seen at work, and the Redactor on its leash** —
  the Book 1 monster walking the market with two clerks holding its paper, correcting: a
  quarrel struck out mid-sentence, a weed, a limp. The crew is what doesn't belong. Kessa maps
  rotations, the three open doors, and the corrector's rounds. Turn: the wound and the seat are
  the same room. Ends on her count of exits: none.
- **Ch26 (Ash — POV; the threads meet).** **Ash in Evermere** — the first time the reader is
  told, in her own head, that the house she left and the seat that hunted Oscar are one
  estate. She walks the halls she grew up under naming what rooms were before the engine.
  Turn: she finds the crew, and the key in her hand fits a door Oscar has been failing to
  open. Ends at the workroom threshold, Ash refusing to go in first.
- **Ch27 (Oscar).** The workroom. Papers in the hand Oscar knows better than his own. **THE
  ARCHITECT REVEAL:** the original devised the cycle — the fair-copy method, the cradles, the
  Setting — and the copies are his *estate*: property made to be finished into his
  continuation. What the room does NOT contain: why the seat must always be filled — those
  pages are *gone* (Book 3's hold kept visibly). Turn: a second set of marks in the room, a
  century old — the third was here, and took something. Ends: Oscar alone with his maker's
  door-plan and a gap in it shaped like a page.
- **Ch28 (Wick).** The entity's attention acquires *weight* — it stops waiting for her to get
  tired (Book 1 Ch42's last beat) and takes her leaving seriously. Its pleasure was never about her; its displeasure is. Ends: the wall answers back
  for the first time — a sound, not words.
- **Ch29 (Cael).** The door-hearth laid in the Fair House's kitchens (his god lives there),
  under siege conditions. The rite *takes* — the hall is short here — and he understands he is
  keeping a door for a girl, not a god. Ends: the fire banked and hidden, one night to hold.
- **Ch30 (Oscar).** **The third returns and tells his century**: he found the thin place from
  this side once, years ago, at another dead seat, and saw through the wall what waits —
  *the master, pacing, patient, facing the world.* "I have spent my life making sure no one
  builds him a door. You are building him one, for a girl." The missing page is in his coat;
  he burns it in front of Oscar. Turn: Oscar cannot answer him and does not stop him. Ends:
  the third gone into the city; Polperro's clerks following him.
- **Ch31 (Kessa).** The night before. The token's last lean on the map-stone; the palm scar
  given its scene (never shown to anyone; write it fresh). Kessa says the count with four and
  leaves the fifth place open, out loud. Turn: Oscar asks her to give the order tomorrow. Ends
  on "Marked."
- **Ch32 (Wick).** She reaches the thin place's near side. Between her and the wall, the
  walker's final leavings — and the far side pressing behind her like a held breath. She sets
  her boots in the last prints and waits where he waited. Turn: through the wall, faint, a
  count in a voice she knows. Ends: she answers it.
- **Ch33 (Ash — POV).** **The heirs arrive** — Ambry with the cradle-wagon from the north, the
  unwoken twelfth crated like furniture, and a writ of the family's own drawn on Polperro.
  Family or crew, on the steps of the house her uncle melted down. Ash's trick: she gives
  Ambry exactly what he asks for — the workroom's location — knowing what it will cost him to
  see it. Ends: the heirs and the Provost at each other's throats over a chair.
- **Ch34 (Oscar — arc turn).** Everything placed — seam read, token set, hearth laid — and
  the trap springs by lamplight: **Polperro does not want to stop the door. He wants it
  open.** A restored font needs the current at flood; a wound in the world is a flood. The
  House's restoration and the crew's rescue need the same tear — and only one side means to
  close it. The corrector is turned on the crew. Ends: the third, watching from the gallery,
  choosing.
- **Ch35 (Tarn — POV).** Door-night begins: the aisle. Tarn — who could never be beaten — meets
  the Redactor, the thing that does not fight but *corrects*, and holds it with the only
  weapon it cannot strike out: a man it cannot read as wrong. He is not on any page. He buys
  the minute the door needs. Ends: "Now," said to Oscar, in the voice of a debt being called.

### Arc 4 — "What Walks Out" (Ch36–46): the door

- **Ch36 (Kessa).** The Fair House fight for the threshold, Book 1's Ch39-grammar at full
  scale: lanes again, every Book-1 verb turned to defense of a door not yet open — Tarn's aisle,
  Cael's hearth, Kessa's angle, Oscar at the wound not yet writing. Turn: the Redactor breaks
  Polperro's leash mid-fight — a corrector with no page to correct. Ends: Ash arrives with the
  heirs' wagon between the clerks and the door.
- **Ch37 (Oscar).** The door-work: reading the wound, writing it OPEN against the world's own
  grain. **Fraying on-page for the first time** — the oldest things go first: Evermere's smell,
  the sound of the spires, the borrowed past paying for the chosen future. Quill on his
  shoulder as ballast: it loves *this* Oscar, not the line. Ends: the door open a hand's width.
- **Ch38 (Wick).** The wall from her side; the count arrives THROUGH it — Kessa's voice — the
  ritual doing on-page what it was always for. She answers. Ends: she starts to run.
- **Ch39 (Kessa).** The hold, side one: **the third comes to close the door** — and the mirror
  duel is a *holding* duel, not a working one: he leans his century on the tear and Oscar
  leans his crew. "You don't know what you're letting through." "I know exactly who's coming
  through. She's sixteen." Turn: Ash out-thinks the hermit (a trickster against a man who has
  been alone too long to expect one). Ends: the crew lending down the chain.
- **Ch40 (Cael).** The hold, side two: the far side pulling the door shut, Cael's hearth the
  only thing keeping the threshold a place a person can stand, Oscar fraying at the wound,
  Tarn's hands on Oscar, Kessa's hand in the light, Ash holding the third by nothing but his
  eyes. **"The hold is the crew's."** Turn: the fire wants to go out and Cael does not let it,
  at a cost he will keep. Ends: a shape in the door, coming — and behind it, farther, another.
- **Ch41 (Wick, far side).** Her last miles under the entity's full weight; what she chooses
  to stop holding so she can run (she drops the walker's relics; keeps the count and the twin
  blank). **The threshold: the prints' owner stands at it, facing the light — the original
  Oscar Alberich, patient, waiting for exactly this door.** He does not stop her. He steps
  aside. Ends: she chooses to go past him.
- **Ch42 (Wick, the crossing).** The door from her side: her family holding it open, the hold
  audible as voices. **The far side keeps nothing of her it can read; it never could.** She
  steps through. Turn: what follows her to the edge — and what stops at it, because the door
  is *hers* and she does not invite him. Ends in the world, on stone, warm.
- **Ch43 (Kessa).** The catch: the scar meets the hand it remembers. The door must die; the
  token is spent as the lock — the far side loses its *made* handle on the world (Wick's own
  door, shut and hers, is the residue — Ch46, Book 3's). **The third's
  fate:** as the door closes he steps *through it* — to stand where the master stood, the far
  side's second sentry, the only way he knows to make sure of a door. Ends: sealed, and the
  place where the rock was is empty.
- **Ch44 (Oscar).** Aftermath in the Fair House: Polperro's restoration in ruins, the Redactor
  spent and standing like furniture, the heirs' twelfth unwoken in its crate — and Oscar,
  frayed, missing Evermere's smell and finding he does not miss it. He unmakes nothing. He
  leaves the cradle to Ash. Ends: he can no longer draw the well-head's groove from memory,
  and is glad.
- **Ch45 (Ash — POV; her thread closes forward).** The estate has a new hand on its paper.
  Ambry's writ torn; the family seat's locks hers now; the twelfth's cradle hers to decide.
  The city told the truth at the three open doors, by the old blood, for the first time in
  its life. Ash does not join the crew. She sets a lamp in a north window. Ends on the
  door-plan, kept, "in case."
- **Ch46 (Wick — bookend).** Warm. Ordinary-warm, person-warm; no fire anywhere in her; the
  door still there behind her ribs, shut, *hers*. The count from her side: **"Five. Breathing.
  Mine."** The Book 3 hook: **the twin blank in her pocket leans away from her, toward the
  sealed wound** — and Wick, who knows what a rock leaning means now, does not tell anyone
  yet.

## Character arcs (one line each)

- **Oscar:** journeyman — builds with the master's craft for an unwritten purpose; pays in
  fraying (loses pieces of the *borrowed* past); ends more himself with less of the original.
  Progression: reading → field architecture → **writing** (the door is authorship).
- **Wick:** the far side is the country of holding; her curse is Book 2's mastery. Returns

## Series-extensibility notes (the author's direction: a world that supports exploration)

Threads deliberately left live, each large enough for its own story: **the far side now has
two sentries** (the master and the third) and a leaning blank on this side; **the estate** —
Greymere, its locked rooms, the family's other holdings, Ash on its paper; **the twelfth**,
unwoken, in a crate, Ash's to decide; **the other dead-houses** ("under other countries") and

Warden's country** and its edge-stones; **the House's remaining seats and clerks** with no
font to serve; **the third's century** — his caches across the world, unfound.

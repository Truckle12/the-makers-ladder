## Logline

The crew that split at a milestone reassembles around a grey rock that leans toward a missing
girl, and walks back into the seat of the power that made Oscar — to open a door in the world
they were built to keep shut. Three things want that door for three different reasons: a
copy of Oscar who has hidden from the line for a century and knows what is waiting on the
other side; a House trying to restart its dead engine with the flood the door would let in;
and the line's living heirs, who have provisioned a cradle. On the far side, Wick walks the
back of the thing itself, following a master's bootprints toward the same wall from within — with
a familiar at her ear that was never hers — and the master's last print faces the wall, waiting.
A fourth thing wants the door, from the other side: the bird wants its master home.

## Quill, both sides (author ruling 2026-09-13: "she talks to Quill… make it make sense")

**What a familiar is (canon from here):** a bound spirit that wears an animal body. The bond is
to a **master**, not to the body. Quill has served the line longer than anyone wrote down (Ch31: "My
uncle's bird, and his mother's before him, and back and back"); it passes by gift, hand to hand, and
then "it answers you. Or it doesn't." The ORIGINAL Oscar Alberich was the master it answered for the
three centuries before the fall. When the master fell *through* (OD1), the bond went with him — **Quill has stood
on both sides of the wall ever since**: the owl body in the world, the rest of it with its master
on the far side. That is what Book 1 already showed without naming it: the bird "stirring at the
edge of the world" (Ch8/9), "the owl that was his and could not be his" and *the perch is not
open* (Ch31 — read here as a bond already occupied; the chapter itself blames Oscar's *reach*), the refusal of every copy (strangers wearing the hand),
and the bonding at the climax (Ch39): Oscar calls it with his hand out and *does not reach for a
working* — "Quill. Come." — and it lands and does not go. That is BEFORE the Setter dies, while Wick
is still holding at nine; the "no wall" is Ch40, after. Ch31 gives the text's own reason for the
refusals until then — Ash: "It's reading your *reach*… nothing *open*" — and the two-sides model sits
behind hers, unsaid: what opened in Ch39 was Oscar's end of a bond whose other end never came back.
In the world it is his. On the far side its
other half is still the master's. **Quill is the only thing that exists on both sides.**

**On the far side it is never an owl** (no body was brought through). It is a brush of feathers
at her ear, talons in the wool of her coat, a weight on a shoulder with nothing on it, a dry
rasp. It never gives its name; it doesn't know her words for anything; it calls the thing under
them **"the Ground."** Wick names it — **"Feathers"** (author-approved 2026-09-13). The reader knows what it is by
Ch7; Wick knows at Ch20.

**Voice:** a bird's mind with three centuries of a scholar's vocabulary — terse, literal, status
reports, no metaphors, cannot lie, can refuse ("Not mine to say."). Dark-funny foil for a blunt
sixteen-year-old: they bicker. It is NOT an exposition mule: it reports what it sees and what it
has been told to keep; it never explains the far side's cosmology (same hold as the third).

**What it can do:** see — and **the master sees through it** (he has watched her since Ch1;
that is how he is "waiting for exactly this door" at Ch41); find (scars, the near side, the
blank); warn (hollows); count with her ("Confirmed."); **carry words along the bond** — the owl
on Oscar's shoulder hears Kessa count in the Fair House; the far-side half repeats it in its rasp
(Ch32), and at Ch38 the real voice comes through the wall behind it. **What it cannot do:** carry
her, burn, lie, name the prints' owner, or cross a door that is not its master's.

**Its want:** its master home. It knows her door is a mouth's door and would let the Hunger out
first. It wants it anyway. That is the fourth thing that wants the door, from the other side.
**Its end (Ch42):** it follows her to the edge and stops — the door is hers and she does not
invite it — and its far-side half stays with the sentry. Book 3 residue: **a bird on both sides
of a sealed wall.**

## The far side as a place (rev4 — so her chapters are a campaign, not a walk)

**THE WICK RULE (author, 2026-10-04 — binding on every drafter):** "Wick's chapters and the far side need to be fucking awesome and shit actually needs to happen. It cannot be bland or all inner monologue." Every Wick chapter is
a run of things that happen to her and things she does — a fight, a chase, a find, a trap, a choice
made with her hands. Her thinking rides on the action and never replaces it. The test: the chapter
can be told as a list of events, and the list is exciting.

- **Terrain = the thing's back** (Book 1 Ch42: "There wasn't any ground. There never had been. She'd been
  sitting on it."). Ridges like a spine; plates that grind; grey stalk-forests she can hide in; pores that
  breathe. When it is pleased the hide is warm under her; when it is not, the country moves.
- **Scars:** every place it ever pushed a finger into the world is a puckered wound on the hide.
  One is the root under the Warden's wild, **cauterized where the crew killed the finger** (Book
  1 Ch26–27). The thin place at Everset is the freshest scar — the wall's "near side."
- **Hollows:** the Hunger keeps what it eats as shapes (the Warden's otter kept its shape three weeks after the grey ate it, Ch25; the finger in the hollow wore an antlered one, Ch26). Fetchers who
  fell through, beasts, a salvage crew — filled with want, and what they want is the warmth under
  her door. She has no fire. What she has is Ch27's lesson, **holding aimed outward: she can
  hold a hollow still.** It costs her. It is her only spell and it is used on page one.
- **The rule of the place** stays learned in her body (Ch1) and stated by nobody: what is not
  held, goes. Her own marks don't stay. The master's do. Her voice goes four feet and stops ("like she'd said it into a coat"); there is no sky; her pocket is empty — the rock stayed on the world's side (Ch42).

## Theme / spine

Book 1: refuse the life that was written for you. **Book 2: write one** — the journeyman phase,
working without the master's hand in someone else's shop, signing nothing. Oscar builds a door
out of the architect's own craft for a purpose the architect never wrote; the horror is that
the hand he writes with wrote the cycle. **The third copy is the book's dark mirror of that
thesis:** he refused the written life too, a century earlier, alone — and became something that
only knows how to close doors. Wick, one rung down the ladder, proves the inversion on the far
side: every power is void there and the only discipline that matters is holding. Book 1's
*the kill is the crew's* (the 08-06 ruling; on the page, Ch8's "The three of them were doing it, and
letting him hold the pen") becomes **"the hold is the crew's."**

Arc questions: Arc 1 — *Can she be found?* Arc 2 — *What does a door cost, and who else wants
it?* Arc 3 — *Who built the ladder, and who is climbing it now?* Arc 4 — *What walks out?*

## The antagonists — three tiers, three reasons

- **The third (living antagonist; name he uses: [proposal] "Aldous" — a name he chose, not one
  he was given).** The grimoire's author. He woke, read the design, refused it, and ran — not
  north, *out*: he spent his century hiding in the seams of the world (waystations, dead-houses,
  the wild's edge), caching help for later wakers because he could not bear the design and
  could not stop it. He is what an unset journeyman becomes after a hundred years with no
  crew: brilliant, kind in small ways, and certain. **His aim: end the line — every copy,
  including Oscar — and never let a door to the far side open,** because once, from THIS side,
  he found the thin place and saw who waits behind it. He knows the prints are the master's.
  He believes a door lets the master *back*. He is not wrong to fear it. He presents first as
  ally ("I wrote you that book"), then as counsel, then as the hand that tries to take the
  token, then as the mirror at the door. Beaten not by refusal (he is a person) but by being
  **out-held**: the crew's hold is stronger than his certainty. His fate: see Ch43.
- **Provost Polperro (institutional).** The House's face at Everset after the seat died —
  polite, sincerely restorative, Sarn's arithmetic at cathedral scale. Runs **restoration**:
  the clerks re-gathered, the chair dusted, and **the Redactor recovered from the north and
  re-leashed on House paper as Everset's corrector** — the Book 1 monster set to correct a
  city instead of a ruin, striking out what doesn't belong in a place coming off its edit. The
  crew is what doesn't belong. Polperro's turn (Ch34): he *wants* the door open — a restored
  font needs the current at flood, and a wound in the world is a flood.
- **The heirs (the estate).** With the seat dead the line's human kin move: **Ambry Alberich
  [proposal name]**, Ash's cousin, master of the estate's paper at the family seat
  (**[proposal] Greymere** — the "-mere" declares the kinship with Evermere), means to refill
  the chair with a *compliant* copy: the heirs have **provisioned a cradle in the north** (the
  half-made copy Oscar finds in Ch4 is theirs — a twelfth, unwoken) and mean to bring it to
  Everset for Setting under their own hand. Oscar is, to them, property in the wrong hands. (They are the *blood*; Ash's "line" is the made one — "the line is you," Ch31 — and she never calls them by it.)
  Ash's thread lives inside this tier.

## 1. The third walked out ahead of Ash

Book 1 already fits it. The Setter (Ch38): the third "was the one of us who left things — figures,
refinements, a better way to take a stroke — cached where a later one of us might wake and need
them. It was against the design." Ash (Ch31): "somebody I trusted had already walked out ahead of me
and left the door standing open." That is the same man. She knew him before his century of hiding.

**What it buys:**
- **Ash's thread stops being "a different novel."** In her first chapter (new Ch8) she finds an old
  letter to her at Greymere in a hand the reader saw one chapter earlier, on the waystation wall
  (new Ch7). The reader connects the threads long before the characters do.
- **The climax gets its lever.** The man who wants every door shut is the man who once left one open
  for her. At the mirror duel she says so, and his certainty cracks from inside — Book 1's own climax
  rule ("what is fixed cannot also be asking").

## 2. Kessa's scar — the token marks her

Book 1 says nothing touched her hands (Ch41), so the scar has to come from Book 2. At the river
crossing the third tries to draw the token out of her pocket with a working only Oscar can see.
Kessa feels a hand she can't see, closes her fist on the rock, and doesn't let go. The pull is
strong enough that the disc's edge cuts **a ring** into her palm.

- She is the one who says "Marked" over the crew. Now she is.
- The night before the door (new Ch28) is the scar's scene: she looks at it and doesn't show anyone.
- When the token is spent as the lock (new Ch40), the ring in her hand is all that's left of it.

## 3. Doss's knife goes in the hearth

The rite wants kept things that matter, not fuel. Doss's whetstone is already gone (Book 1 Ch41).
The knife is the last thing of his she carries, and the thing that killed the Setter. She lays it in
the door-hearth, and the door that brings Wick home is kept by Doss's knife. She fights the rest of
the book with her crossbow and a plain blade. Whether she takes the knife back out of the cold hearth
afterward is a small choice for new Ch41.

## 4. Tarn stays a word short

The Setter took *door* out of him (Book 1 Ch39). It doesn't come back.

- **In his own chapter (new Ch5)** we're inside the hole: he reaches for the word for what the House
  wants and his mind slides off it. He calls it "the thing," "the gap," "her way home."
- **At the aisle (new Ch32)** the corrector reads him and finds a place already struck out in its own
  hand. It reads him as a corrected page and passes over him. That's how he holds it: the man no page
  can correct, because one already did.
- It's a cost that stays, which Book 1's readers asked for.

## 6. Oscar fights his own mirror

In rev5 Oscar narrates only 2 of the last 11 chapters, and the duel with the third — his own dark
mirror — is told from Kessa's view and won by Ash. Proposed: **the duel is Oscar's chapter.** The third
leans a century of certainty on the tear; Oscar leans his crew. Ash's line (decision 1) is the crack
he finds; the crew's hold is what keeps the door open while he finds it. Kessa keeps the Fair House
fight before it and the catch after it, where she gives the order.

## 7. Wick's clock — what she can't hold, she loses

Ch1 tells the reader she's alive, so the crew's "can she be found?" isn't the reader's question.
Her chapters need their own pressure. The far side's rule already gives it: *what is not held, goes.*
That applies to memories too. The hollows are what's left of people who couldn't hold themselves —
fetchers, a salvage crew — filled with want.

- **One concrete loss per Wick chapter**, least-held first: a smell, a street, a face at the edges.
  She counts the crew every night because the count is what she holds hardest.
- **At the siege (new Ch25)** she holds the line and loses her name.
- **At the near side (new Ch29)** she sits nameless in the master's last prints. Feathers says
  **"Wick."** in its rasp — Oscar's word, said to the owl in the world, carried along the bond — and
  then **"Four. Breathing."** Her name and the count are the first words through the wall.
- **Home (new Ch43)**: one thing never comes back. Recommendation: the village that called her Wick
  to be unkind. She keeps the name. Now it only means what Oscar meant by it.

It mirrors Oscar's fraying from the other side of the wall: both pay for the door in memory.

## 8. Cael's cost

Rev5 says only "a cost he will keep." Proposed: to keep the threshold human, Cael puts his own warmth
into it — his hall, his gift — and it goes through the door with her. She comes home "ordinary-warm,
person-warm" because he gave her his. His hands are cold after. He keeps fires the ordinary way.
"I can't be a person and a hole at the same time" (Book 1 Ch40); he makes her a person again, and it
costs him the thing he was.

With 2, 4, 7 and 8 every crew member pays something that stays: Oscar's Evermere, Wick's memories,
Cael's gift, Tarn's word, Kessa's token. "The hold is the crew's."

## 9. Geography fix — no second road south

Book 1's milestone is just outside Everset's west gate ("They went out through the west gate at dawn
and walked on to the milestone before they split"). Rev5 reunites the crew there (Ch12), then sends
them "south at speed" through Marrow (Ch18) — which is hundreds of miles north. Fix: **the token leans
back toward Everset** (the wall is thinnest there), Kessa's summons names the milestone, and Arc 2
happens in Everset's hinterland: the waystation where Corwen died, the scarp, the river. They split
at the milestone and come back together at the milestone. This also removes the "same road again"
problem from the review. Old Ch18 becomes Kessa scouting the city gate alone — the queue stepping
round the erased spots, the corrector on its chain, seen rather than rumored.

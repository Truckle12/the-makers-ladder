Oscar… Wick's later chapters might also be off voice, and Kessa's might need tweaking." Then,

## Headline

**The author is right, and it is systemic, not local.** The book has one narrator's cadence
wearing five names. Every non-Oscar voice — Wick, Kessa, Cael, Tarn, and in dialogue even
Corwen and Brida — drifts into Oscar's register in the *same three ways*, in narration and in
speech alike:

1. **The epigram landing.** A paragraph, a speech, or a chapter ends on a general truth.
   Kessa: "A crew is a thing with a job… when the job is over the shape goes." Cael: "a person
   is mostly what they do." Tarn: "Crowds don't come for a fight. They come for an ending."
   Wick: "For tonight, less was the best thing she'd ever held." About two-thirds of all
   flagged dialogue is this shape. Every Kessa chapter ending but one is this shape.
2. **Oscar's lexicon in other heads.** Grammar, page, seam, script, clause, written, text,
   read, arithmetic, sum, ledger, column, entry, instrument, load-bearing. Kessa's tally has
   become Sarn's bookkeeping ("column" ×12, "sum" ×7, "ledger" ×3 across her chapters, 18 of
   them in Ch32 and Ch34). Cael thinks "there is no mending a sentence while the writer holds
   the pen." Wick narrates a witch "losing the grammar of her own fury."
3. **The balanced antithesis.** "It's not a hiding place. It's an appointment." "It isn't a
   monster. It's a *bereavement.*" "Cities aren't sealed. Cities *leak.*" "Not gone. Just
   less." Oscar's reversal cadence, issued to whoever speaks last in a scene.

Plus one Wick-specific failure the profile names outright: **the mystery explained instead of
felt** — the coal, the door, the far side rendered as image or mechanism (~11 instances,
four of them in Ch40).

**The author's catch is the tic in miniature.** "I'd like it noted" is Oscar's (Ch35, Ch37).
It came out of Wick's mouth in a chapter I had just rewritten *for* her voice, and survived a
review pass. That is the self-editing-loop fingerprint named in the gauntlet synthesis: the
same hand keeps re-minting the house cadence while fixing it. The Ch22–42 dialogue auditor
flagged the identical line independently. Fixed today, with two more in Ch42 ("You can't
leave a hole behind" — a second aphorism; "Right on cue" — stage idiom).

**Where it is *not* broken (protect):** short lines. Kessa on terrain and counts ("Empty
hedges. Sloppy." / "Is she alive." / "Then get better." / "Marked."). Cael doing rather than
explaining ("He's a person." / "Eat." / "Go home whole."). Wick wanting ("There's *three* pie
stalls." / "your bird's broken" / "Zero." / "Course. Course it is."). Tarn from Ch26 on ("Not
told to yet." / "Best kind of debt… Doesn't pay, either."). Every auditor said the same thing:
the voices are instantly tellable apart *when short*, and stop being tellable the moment a
character is handed the scene's summary — because from Ch13 onward, the summarizer's voice is
Oscar's regardless of speaker.

## Proposed Batch 6 — "Voice separation" (author's call; nothing below is done)

**Principle.** Almost every fix is a *deletion of a closing clause* or a *word swap out of
Oscar's lexicon*, not a rewrite. Three scoped re-narrations only. No scene changes shape.

- **6a — Kessa narration.** Real pass on **Ch34** and **Ch32**: retire the ledger device
  (column / sum / ledger / entry → tally / count / marks / set aside — the tally is right, the
  words are Sarn's); cut the chapter-ending aphorisms; re-render Oscar's and Wick's interiors
  as *observed behaviour* (what his face did, where her feet went), never as their meaning.
  Line edits on **Ch25** (closing paragraph + 4), **Ch41** (7, mostly clause deletions),
  **Ch29** (final two paragraphs + 3).
- **6b — Wick narration.** **Ch30**: re-narrate "Her name was Ash" → "You can see why I
  waited" through her body (third bowl going cold, who's looking at her, heat) — leave the
  stew opening and the stairs alone. **Ch12**: the door block and the admiration coda.
  Line edits (6–8 each) on **Ch27, 36, 40**: every "mystery explained" → the symptom in her
  throat/chest/hands; drop the narrator tic "since before she had a word for anything" (×3,
  nobody's voice); make fear shorten her sentences at the door (Ch12), the erasure (Ch36),
  Kessa's stillness (Ch40).
- **6c — Cael, Ch33.** The collar sequence and the two closing paragraphs; thin the similes by
  a third; end on "went to see about the living."
- **6d — Dialogue, all chapters.** The 16 MUSTs first (Kessa Ch13/32/34, Cael Ch15/19/24/30/
  37/38, Tarn Ch22/23/32, Wick Ch16/24). Then the "cut the last clause" sweep over the
  SHOULDs (Kessa's briefing run Ch28–37 especially). Break Tarn's Ch22–23 lectures into
  answers to questions. Flatten Corwen to procedure. Brida to one epigram per scene.
  Oscar: consolidate the Tithe speech to the square (Ch17), the "reading isn't lifting" thesis
  to Ch37, retire "arithmetic" from his mouth so it stays the Tithe's word, and let one landing
  each misfire in Ch24 and Ch30 (embarrassment after sincerity, per the guardrail).
- **6e — Instruments, so this stops recurring.** Add a **lexicon lock** to the guardrail: Sarn's
  words (arithmetic, sum, ledger, column, entry) and Oscar's (grammar, page, seam, script,
  clause, written, instrument, "noted") are *banned in every non-Oscar POV and mouth* unless
  deliberately borrowed and marked. Extend `tools/cadence.mjs` to flag them per chapter by
  POV. Add "I'd like it noted" and its kin to Wick's and Kessa's profiles as named Oscar tics.
  Every future chapter gets an independent voice check *before* commit, not after.

**Judgment calls to leave alone unless the author says otherwise.** "So don't use the world."
(Ch40) — the Ch22–42 auditor flags it as the one perfect line from the character who
shouldn't land them; five gauntlet readers named it; keep. "Halfway and home" — load-bearing,
keep. "Is she alive." / "Then get better." / "Zero." / "Marked." — the target, keep.

**Size.** ~16 chapters touched; ~200 line edits; three scoped re-narrations (Ch34 whole, the
Ch30 section, two Ch12 blocks). Method as Batch 4: one agent per voice with its profile and
its audit file as the work order, then cadence + repeater, then the Priya/June gauntlet
personas on the diff, then the author reads.

**What this pass cannot do.** It separates the voices; it does not make Oscar's own narration
less uniform (the gauntlet's "monophony of rhythm"). That is a different, later pass — and a
matter of taste the author should rule on after reading the separated version.

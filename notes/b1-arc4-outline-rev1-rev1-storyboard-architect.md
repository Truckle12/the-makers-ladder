## 3. The dependency cascade — resolve these in ORDER (this is the reviewer's "invalidate later chapters" concern)

rev1 lists 15 `[FOR THE AUTHOR]` items as a flat menu. They are **not** flat — several are upstream of others, and answering a downstream one first wastes work. Resolve top-to-bottom:

**TIER 0 — Decide FIRST; everything at Everset hangs on it.**

1. **Terminology + substrate of the font (rev1 gaps #11 → #1).** The bible says Everset is "apex **living** magic + the font"; the Decisions Log says "high **new**-magic … deep/arcane." rev1 papers over this with a clever split (font = arcane/made-adjacent so Oscar can partly read it; "living" reserved for Wick's sense). **This is not a wording choice — it is the load-bearing decision of the whole climax.**
   - If the font is **made/arcane** → Oscar's gift (made-only) can touch it → **the sweep** can be "turn the erasure-engine's logic outward" (rev1's proposal) → Ch40 works as written.
   - If the font is **living** → Oscar is *blind* to it (per the hard-scope rule the bible enforces everywhere else) → the sweep **cannot** be Oscar reading/unmaking the engine → Ch38, Ch40, and the entire progression-payoff ("reader → field architect wins by orchestrating") must be re-mechanized around someone/something else carrying the font.
   - **Invalidates if wrong:** Ch38 (font mechanics), Ch40 (the sweep), Ch41 (how the confrontation resolves), and the Ch39 reveal's texture. **Nothing in the back half can be drafted until this is locked.** Flag to canon-keeper: the bible and Decisions Log currently *contradict each other* on this and need reconciliation into one canon line.

**TIER 1 — Decide before the manse chapters (Ch31–32) and the climax.**

2. **What Ash actually wants (rev1 gap #2) — and a likely CANON CONFLICT to resolve.** rev1's Ch31 premise says *"Ash wants something the copy carries that only an Alberich can carry, and she is steering him toward Everset for it."* But the bible's Ash entry defines her motive as *"a rebel of the line who seeks out made Oscars that break the cycle and quietly **helps** them."* Those are in tension: "wants something the copy carries" frames Ash as a **user**; the bible frames her as a **helper**. This matters structurally because **Ash departs at Ch32, before Everset** — so if she "wants something done at the font," she is not present to receive it, and the climax has a dangling purpose.
   - **Recommended reframe (resolves both the canon conflict and the structural dangle):** Ash wants *nothing from the font for herself.* She steers Oscar to Everset because that is where **he** will see what he was made for and get the chance to refuse it — the *help* is the steering plus Quill plus the withheld hint. She can leave at Ch32 because her gift was **knowledge and the owl**, not an errand. This keeps her a rebel-helper (canon), removes the "why isn't she at the climax she set up?" problem, and lets Everset be **Oscar's** reckoning, not Ash's transaction.
   - **Invalidates if wrong:** Ch31 (what Ash says), Ch38–41 (whether the climax owes Ash a deliverable). Decide before drafting Ch31.

3. **The Ch29 double-snatch mechanics + the Ch31 "pre-explained offscreen" problem (rev1 gaps #6, #8).** See §4 (Ch29/Ch31) — there is a real POV-sequencing trap here that changes who can hold Ch31's POV.

**TIER 2 — Decide before the climax pair (Ch40–41), but downstream of Tier 0/1.**

4. **How Wick's sacrifice mechanically saves the crew (rev1 gap #10).** rev1 asserts "the sweep isn't enough alone" and Wick gives herself to the door — but **never says what the door DOES** to the font or to Corwen. This is the emotional peak of Book 1 and it currently has no causal engine. See §4 (Ch41).

5. **What the sweep is (rev1 gap #3)** — downstream of Tier 0 #1. Can't be finalized until the font's substrate is locked.

6. **The Ch34 cost (rev1 gap #4)** and **how Corwen dies (rev1 gap #5)** — locally containable, decide after the above.

**TIER 3 — Cosmetic / flexible; safe to leave last.** POV assignments (#12), chapter count (#13), working titles (#14), final-note flavor (#15), manse permanence (#7), Quill's on-page behavior (#9), architect-hint dosage (#8).

> **Bottom line for the reviewer's concern:** the outline's back half (Ch38–41) is **not yet draftable** because Tier 0 #1 is unresolved and self-contradictory in canon. Everything the reviewer worried might "invalidate later chapters" traces to that one node and to Ash's want (#2). Lock those two, and the rest of rev1 holds.

## 4. Structural / chapter-level notes (anchored)

### Ch29 (Oscar POV) — the double-snatch: POV must hard-cut at the blink
The plan is sound, but flag one hard requirement so Ch30 works: **Ch29 must END on the blink and must NOT show the manse interior.** If we stay in Oscar's head after Ash blinks him away, the reader learns "guest / not a cage" before Kessa's Ch30 reunion — which kills the vertigo Ch30 depends on. Recommend Ch29 goes dark the instant Ash takes him (Oscar's POV cuts mid-blink), so the reader shares the crew's ignorance through Ch30. Worth stating explicitly in the outline as a POV boundary, not leaving to the drafter.

### Ch30 (Kessa POV, LOCKED) — solid. One continuity seed to add.
Good as written. Add a note that whatever "wrongness" Kessa reads at the conjured manse must be **made/worked**, not living (the manse is conjured Alberich opulence, not the font) — so her unease is "this house shouldn't be here" (geometry/terrain) rather than any living-magic pressure, which is reserved. rev1 says this in the reveal-discipline line; just make sure the drafter treats it as binding.

### Ch31 (Oscar POV) — **the reveal cannot be "discovery" in Oscar's POV. Reconsider.**
This is the sharpest structural wrinkle in rev1. The locked staging says Ash *"has pre-explained what she'll share"* **before** the crew bursts in (Ch30 shows Oscar already "at ease"). That means by Ch31, **Oscar already knows** guest / niece / the hint — he learned it offscreen between Ch29 and Ch30. So an Oscar-POV Ch31 **cannot deliver the reveal as fresh discovery**; it can only show Oscar re-hearing it while the *crew* discovers it. Two clean fixes:
- **(a) Keep Oscar POV, but reframe Ch31's beat as ACCEPTANCE, not discovery.** Oscar's interior arc is processing "I am a copy of a real person this woman grieved," and chewing the architect-hint — while the *crew's* first-time reaction is the external drama he observes. Legitimate, but the drafter must know the reveal energy belongs to the crew, seen through Oscar.
- **(b) Give Ch31 to a crew POV so the reveal lands fresh for the POV character.** Kessa already has Ch30; Cael's one POV is spoken for (Ch34); Tarn gets none. That leaves Wick — but Wick is proposed for Ch32. One option: **swap — Wick POV for Ch31 (she receives the reveal with jealous, betrayed kid-logic), Oscar POV for Ch32 (the Quill handoff, which is genuinely his beat).** This arguably serves the succession-wound theme better and fixes the discovery problem. Worth the author's consideration.

Either way, **flag it** — rev1 currently assigns Oscar to Ch31 as if he's discovering, and the staging says he isn't.

### Ch32 (Wick POV) — Quill: reconcile the bible's own bundling
rev1's give-at-manse / bond-at-climax split is the right call and elegantly satisfies "he can't summon Quill until he accepts he's a person." **But note a bible ambiguity to send to canon-keeper:** the bible's Arc-4 PLANNED bullet lists "Quill given to Oscar" *alongside* "Corwen dies" as **climax** beats, which a literal reader would place at Ch41, not Ch32. The Decisions Log staging (giving at the Ash meeting) is what rev1 follows and is correct — but the bible's Full Synopsis / Arc-4 line should be updated to say "Quill **given** at the manse, **bonds** at the climax," or the two will keep drifting. Recommend a canon-keeper ticket.

### Ch33 + Ch35 (road chapters) — probe for one merge, or add incident
With Ash gone by Ch32, the outline has **five** consecutive road/approach chapters (33, 34, 35, 36, 37) before the gates (38). rev1 honestly flags the "saggy road" risk and answers it with the tightening-spiral principle — good — but Ch33 ("road south again," Oscar) and Ch35 ("the gap closes," Kessa) are both largely *pursuit + itch + Wick-dread ratchet* with the waystation (34) between them. Ask: does the arc need **both** a pre-waystation and a post-waystation travel chapter, or can one be folded so every road chapter carries a distinct, non-repeating turn? Not a defect — a tightening opportunity. If they stay separate, make sure 33 and 35 don't ratchet the *same* three dials in the same way.

### Ch34 (Cael POV — the one Cael chapter) — the cost, and a POV-economy caution
Good placement (bodies/mercy/cost = Cael's field). One caution to surface: rev1's **recommended cost is "Cael gravely wounded,"** which would make Cael's *single* POV chapter also the chapter of his own wounding, and then he spends Ch38–41 diminished **without a POV to render it from the inside.** That's a legitimate choice (his interiority at the moment of sacrifice is potent), but the author should decide it *knowingly*: the payoff of Cael's locked "independent at Everset" power (Ch38) will be observed from Oscar's/others' POV, not Cael's. If the author would rather feel Cael's hearth-light hold *from inside Cael* at Everset, the single-POV must move to a later slot — which the Decisions Log currently forbids (Cael = Ch33/34 only). Flag the trade so it's a decision, not an accident. Also: keep the cost from pre-spending Book-2 guns (Tarn's hook, Wick) — rev1 already says this; endorse.

### Ch36–37 (approach; Oscar then Wick) — good. Keep the itch and the door on *separate* dials.
The Evermere itch (Oscar's made-sense) and Wick's door (living/deep) must not blur into one "the magic gets stronger" hum. They are different substrates and different POV bodies. rev1 keeps them separate; make sure the drafter does too, or the Tier-0 terminology muddle leaks in here.

### Ch38–39 (gates / the reveal) — gated by Tier 0 #1. Do not draft until the font substrate is locked.
See §3. Also add a currently-missing beat: **what is the font's FATE?** The climax confronts the erasure-engine, but neither the bible nor rev1 says whether it is destroyed, dormant, or left running at book's end. This shapes Book 2 and the Ch42 denouement's mood. Add as a `[FOR THE AUTHOR]`.

### Ch40 (the sweep) — gated by Tier 0 #1; strong once that's locked
The fusion of "new ability (progression)" + "command without control (his arc)" is exactly right for the sweep. Only concern is the dependency: the sweep's proposed mechanic ("turn the engine's logic outward") **requires** the font to be made/arcane. Do not finalize before #1.

### Ch41 (Corwen dies + Wick gives herself) — **the causal engine of the sacrifice is missing**
This is the most important content gap after Tier 0. rev1 says "the sweep isn't enough alone" and Wick gives herself to the door — but does not answer **what the door does that saves them.** Right now it reads as sacrifice-for-its-own-sake. The author needs to choose a mechanism, e.g.:
- **(a) The door as a sink:** the erasure-engine/font tries to "strike out" the crew (or Corwen weaponizes it); Wick opens her door and the hungry deep *swallows the erasure* — the thing that wants everything eats the engine's strike, and takes her with it as the price. (Ties door-hunger to the font thematically; "the thing that wants" vs "the thing that erases.")
- **(b) The door as the only lever on the living part:** if part of the confrontation is living (Tier 0 #1) and Oscar is blind to it, Wick's door is the *only* crew tool that can meet it — she meets it and is taken. (Pays off "his reading is the wrong tool" one last time.)
- **(c) Corwen's death routed through the door-collapse:** Corwen is caught/unmade in the same event that takes Wick, making his death and her loss one indivisible beat.
Also unresolved and load-bearing: **is the thing at Wick's door the same entity as the grey's source?** The bible says the grey source was reabsorbed by the wild (Ch28) and Wick's door is *separate* ("still there, still wanting"). So it should **not** be the grey — the drafter must not accidentally resurrect the grey. And **why is a deep-entity reachable at Everset?** rev1's answer ("apex magic thins the veil to the deep") is serviceable but should be stated as canon, since it's the bridge that makes the whole sacrifice geographically possible. Decide the mechanism, the entity's identity, and the reachability before drafting.

### Ch42 (denouement) — good; add the font's fate and confirm Ash does not return
Endorse "resolve toward rescue Wick" as the Book-2 engine. Two adds: (1) the denouement's mood depends on the **font's fate** (§Ch38–39) — a running erasure-engine is a darker close than a broken one; (2) confirm Ash's Ch32 departure is her last Book-1 appearance (rev1 recommends this — I agree; a reappearance dilutes the solo-Book-2 setup).

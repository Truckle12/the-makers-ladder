Chapters read cold: 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 26, 28, 31, 35, 37, 38, 39; Ch33 read for the Cael check. Each chapter was read as a reader first, then word-diffed against `scratchpad/base/`, then checked against logs C and D. Line numbers are the current files. Read-only; nothing edited.

Overall: the cuts are clean. Every cut in these fourteen chapters is a whole sentence or clause removed at a natural join, or a "Not X." fragment folded into its neighbour; no dropped quotes, doubled words, orphan italics or stray punctuation anywhere. ## 2. OSCAR'S REGISTER

Nothing was roughened. The folds (Ch20 L7, L91; Ch23 L15, L65; Ch26 L13; Ch28 L63, L81; Ch37 L143; Ch39 L87) keep his clause structure. Two places the editors *wrote* rather than cut:

1. **Ch31 L127** — base: "and she said 'your bird's broken,' and the failed joke in it was aimed so squarely at making him feel better about the morning that he almost couldn't breathe around it." Current: "and she said 'your bird's broken,' at him and not at the bird, and he had to look at the road for a while." This does exactly what Astra asked (let the action carry it), and it is the best-judged cut in the group. But "he had to look at the road for a while" is Kessa's cadence, not his; Oscar would construe it ("and the road required his attention for a while" is the shape). Flag for the author; not a break.

2. **Ch24 L31** (the new Cael beat's narration): "It came out short. He didn't take it back. He put the piece where he'd meant to put it." Three clipped sentences in a row inside Oscar's POV. The content is fine; the rhythm is group F's, not his. One join would fix it ("It came out short, and he didn't take it back; he put the piece…").

Acceptable rewrites: Ch28 L123 "and Oscar heard the shape of it anyway"; L125 "'You can't,' he said, because a kind lie would get her killed"; Ch35 L53 "A beat." (house usage, cf. Ch19 L59).

Caption cuts that went a notch too far (reader can no longer tell what Oscar concluded and the next beat leans on it): only Ch21 L55 and Ch24 L19, above. Everywhere else the next beat still stands on its own — checked specifically at Ch20 L111 (the brace), Ch23 L147 ("interest"), Ch26 L145 ("He shut it instead"), Ch37 L193 ("*Our crew*"), Ch38 L47 ("pleased").

## 4. THE THREE NEW BEATS

**Ch24 L11, Tarn's wood dove.** Native. It sits inside his own naming-things patter, is dropped and not explained ("Wood dove," he said. "Missed those." He walked on.), and the physical detail — head cocked, thumbs in the pack straps until it quit — is his economy exactly. It also, unplanned, sets up Ch26 L33 ("There were birds, then."), which now reads as a payoff rather than a lone note. Nothing to change.

**Ch24 L29–31, Cael's "Don't".** The content is right and it is his: fire, wood, the ribs he'd already checked, irritation at thanks plus the admission he is tired of being the one still up. It does not pre-empt the Ch33 crisis, which is about warmth being no use and the shield being older than any vow. It does graze Ch33 L89 — "hated the door, for the first time in his life, for being his to hold and no one else's" — since Ch24 now shows the same resentment in miniature; "for the first time" becomes a shade less true, but survives (hating ≠ chafing). The real problem is the word. Cael has already said "Don't. Don't do that again." to Oscar at Ch22 L153 and "Don't… *Don't.*" at Ch23 L31; and twelve lines after the new beat, Wick says "*Don't.*" (L43, pre-batch, unchanged) to the same man for the same offence — a kindness offered — in the same clipped shape ("Fast and thick" / "It came out short"). Oscar is now told "Don't" twice in a page by two people, and Wick's, the original and sharper beat, reads as the echo. Minimal fix: change Cael's one word (e.g. "Leave it," Cael said.) so Wick keeps "Don't" in this chapter; the rest of the beat stands.

**Ch31 L107, Ash's reason.** Native and hers. The law is gone; what replaces it is limit ("I've never settled whether there had to be; I only know it's the one time in my life I was sure the word was mine") and self-knowledge ("I don't trust myself in the room. I know what I'm like with a hand on a thing; I make it mine without meaning to… I'll take it before either of us has noticed."). It contradicts nothing else in the chapter — it pays L21 ("kept by a very hospitable knife"), L15 ("nothing here was hers, or everything was"), L53 ("the daily choice she'd been serving him"), and her refusal of Kessa's offer at L91 — and it answers Oscar's L99 charge ("*she thinks I have to lose*") directly. "Somebody I trusted had already walked out ahead of me" (a previous Alberich) sits fine beside "I broke alone". The one residual: "That's the inheritance, wizard. Not the bird. Somebody ahead of you, and a door left open." is still a thesis sentence, though now told as her own story; with "Every generation someone has to be the proof" gone, the notch of comprehensiveness has been taken. Keep as is.

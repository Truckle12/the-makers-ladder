## What it IS: a developmental editor's read of your arc
Report under these headings, under ~1,600 words total, with chapter numbers on every claim:

**A. The reading experience.** Where you leaned in; where you skimmed or put it down; where you were
confused (and whether the confusion was productive mystery or just unclear). Be specific.
**B. The question at each chapter's end.** One line per chapter: what the reader is worried about or
wants as the chapter closes. Mark chapters where the answer is "nothing much" — those are the sag.
**C. Characters.** Who is vivid, who is flat, who changes in your arc and how. Relationships that work;
ones that are told rather than earned. Is Oscar someone you want to follow?
**D. Set pieces and scenes.** The best and the weakest, and why. Are fights clear? Do they cost?
**E. Promises and payoffs.** What your arc promises (questions, mysteries, setups) and what it pays
(from earlier). Anything that feels paid too early, never set up, or forgotten.
**F. The three biggest developmental problems in your arc** — structure, pacing, stakes, motivation,
clarity, repetition of beats across chapters. For each: what it is, where, why it matters, and the
smallest structural fix you'd suggest (cut/merge/move/add a scene), not a line fix.
**G. Best chapter / weakest chapter** in your arc, one paragraph each.
**H. Verdict.** Would a reader of the comps keep going at the end of your arc? What would make them stop?

Be honest; the author explicitly wants pushback over flattery. Praise only what you'd defend.
Write your report to the path your prompt names and return it in full.

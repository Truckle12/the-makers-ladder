## 1. Pacing coherence — the clock

**FINDING (structural, the same failure Ch40 had). Quoted:** *"A minute, maybe less than a minute, and
then the big man would go over sideways"* → *"That was her clock."* The chapter then spends four
goodbyes (Oscar, Kessa, Cael, Tarn), a hug, and a token contemplation inside that minute. A stated
duration the beats do not support.

**Fix applied:** the hard numbers are gone (`nine seconds of air`, `a minute`) and replaced with a felt
reserve — *"An inch in the bottom of a cup."* Then the clock is **paid on-page**: Cael goes out mid-Tarn
beat, the gold stops, and the city immediately comes back in through the gap (Kessa mis-counts *six*,
Oscar loses a word and decides not to say which). *"Nobody had to tell her the goodbyes were finished."*
The clock now ends the scene instead of being outlived by it.

**Second clock added:** the erasure's advance is now visible in the middle of the chapter (*"Up the slope
behind them a whole terrace went."*), so the reader can see the ground shrinking, not just be told.

## 2. Problem → Plan → Consequence

**FINDING:** the middle third (the four goodbyes) was pure emotional beat — no problem, no consequence —
and the eleven-pace walk was **unopposed**, which is a soft crux for the biggest act in the book.

**Fix applied:** the walk is now a three-hander with a real problem.
- *Problem:* the runaway starts striking out **its own basin** — the floor of the ring stops being
  reliable at three paces. (Pre-seeded: it already ate its own clerks and the Setter's robe at the
  chapter's open, so this is consistent, not convenient.)
- *Plan:* **Kessa calls her feet** ("Left foot. Small. There's a seam. Now two quick") and **Oscar braces
  a two-foot strip of stone** all the way to the slab.
- *Consequence:* *"Four paces behind her the brace let go with a small dry sound, and the strip went out
  of true and fell in."* It holds exactly long enough and no longer.

Small physical friction added at the token so it isn't pure reflection: she has to pry her own fist open
finger by finger — she's been holding it since the gate.

## 3. No passengers

- **Oscar** — was reading + orchestrating only. The persona line is explicit: *reading is INPUT, the
  scene needs OUTPUT.* He now performs **one last piece of made-only wizardry — the brace** (the same
  figure the Setter flicked off him in Ch40), and the only thing his magic achieves at the climax is
  getting her to the place he told her to stand. That is the intended helplessness *and* real output.
  He is on both knees at the rim by the end of it. No reading of a living body. No fraying gauge.
- **Kessa** — was emotion only (grab / vow). Now also does **terrain work under fire**, her actual lane:
  reads a disintegrating basin and talks a kid across it.
- **Cael** — holds the fire, then breaks. **The break is now the chapter's hinge**, not an offstage
  cost. Correctly **no benediction** — he opens his mouth and nothing comes; Wick absolves *him.*
- **Tarn** — the ledger verdict (*"It's a good trade"*) **plus** a physical job: he takes Cael's whole
  weight the instant he folds. Not called big. Still can't hold the word *door* (Ch40 canon preserved —
  she gives it back, it doesn't stick, and she promises to keep saying it for him).
- **Quill** — one scream, in-lane, doesn't steal the beat.

## 4. The win must be felt — and not only by the POV

Passing. The cost ripples across four heads: **Kessa's left palm burned** with the shape of a girl's
arm (a Book-2 scar), **Cael collapsed and dark**, **Tarn's word gone twice over**, **Oscar's last figure
broken under his hands**. Body before mind at the sacrifice — she goes physical (heat, cold, the coal
spending out) and draws no meaning.

## 5. Enemy competence & motive

**FINDING:** *"It didn't fight. It couldn't."* The runaway rolled over too easily for a threat that had
just been declared unanswerable.

**Fix applied:** the engine gets **one real reflex** and it is the best beat in the chapter's mechanism —
it turns on the thing standing at its seat to read it and strike it out, *and finds nothing to read,*
three times, patiently, because there is no clause in the world for a hole. This retro-loads Wick's line
to Cael (*"I can't be a person and a hole at the same time"*) from pathos into **mechanism**: had Cael
put his warmth on her, the font would have had something to find. Canon respected — the font never
"reads a person" successfully on the page.

## KEEP (do not sand these off)

- **"So don't use the world."** — Wick answering Oscar's Ch40 wrong word. The hinge of the whole finale.
- **Oscar telling a child where to stand so it works properly**, and his hand being steady while he does
  it. The worst kindness in the book; his guilt engine for Book 2.
- **Wick prying Kessa's grip off with the coal** and never knowing whether she meant to.
- **Kessa's *"You're not gone. You're taken. That's a different word and it's the one I'm using."***
- **Cael having no words**, and the kid telling him he doesn't need any.
- **Tarn's *"It's a good trade… And I hate it. Write that down as well."***
- **"You can't hold two things."** — the reason the token is set down, in her own kid-logic.
- The gauge landing on **"Zero"** (the pressure isn't across the wall any more; there is no wall) and
  **Kessa answering "Marked"** — the book's whole protocol, one last time.
- The last image: a hollow the size of a chair, and a small grey rock going on being nothing at all.

   without losing its beat. Say the word and I'll take a knife to it.
2. **Kessa's burn is a new permanent mark.** A hand-shaped scar across her left palm, taken from Wick,
   carried into Book 2. Confirm you want it — it's a strong physical carry for the "I am going to come
   and get you" spine, but it is a new canon fact, not in the outline.
3. **The "one reflex" beat is new canon-adjacent:** the font tries to strike Wick out and *fails for a
   stated reason* (a hole isn't written). It's the cleanest justification for why she survives long
   enough to finish, and it does not explain the cosmology. Confirm it's Book-1 canon.
4. **Wick's retrospective frame.** The chapter uses *"she never told anybody, later, wherever later was"*
   and *"she tried, later"* — this quietly promises the reader she is **alive somewhere**, which supports
   the Book-2 rescue spine but slightly softens the gut-punch. Alt: strip the retrospect and end her
   perception hard. Recommend keeping it; it's the difference between a death and a wound.

# Wick POV voice audit — Book 1 (Ch12, 27, 30, 36, 40, 42)

All six open in Wick's head and stay there (Ch40's last line steps out after she's gone; unavoidable). Test = `pov-profiles/wick.md`. "Leak" = Oscar's register, or the book's default narrator (which is Oscar-shaped), inside her narration. Alternatives sketch the register, not copy.

## Ch12 — Kindling
**DRIFTING — 6/10.** Bones / spider / rescue is solidly hers; the door climax and the admiration coda belong to the narrator.

MUST
- "the way a great slow head turns in the dark, ancient and unhurried and *glad,*" — the door as majestic image, triad polish, at peak terror. → "Something down there turned round. Looked at her. And it was *glad*, and her throat shut."
- "It wanted to *keep* her. To draw her down through the door and make her part of whatever it was" — "she understood that much": mystery analysed, not felt. → "It wasn't going to kill her. Worse. It wanted her *down there.* Her edges were going."

SHOULD
- "The hardest thing in the world, Wick had decided a long time ago, was holding still while you burned." — aphorism as opener; she doesn't open on a thesis. → "Holding still while you burned was the worst thing there was. She'd known that since she was eight."
- "Not a monster, just a *beast,* an honest blind animal" — calm antithesis while it's coming at her. → "It wasn't a monster. It was an animal and it was hungry and she was warm."
- "Because Oscar would understand it, and she couldn't stand, just then, to be understood." — epigram landing. → "He'd get it in about a second and look at her with that face. She'd rather eat the ash."
- "out of a language no one living was supposed to be able to read" / "a want that was bigger than the heat and steadier than the fear" — perfectly framed admiration in balanced clauses, the exact thing the guardrail tells Wick to reduce. → "He just *knew* where air was. She wanted that so bad it hurt her teeth."

COULD: "the way moss grows in a cracked pot"; the "felt her edges… felt the heat… felt the hungry gladness" anaphora is organised where it should pile.

Checks: dark-funny yes ("practically the only kind of brave she had"; "you couldn't sell sad to a salvager"). Fear → piling: yes at the patrol, no at the door (sentences lengthen and ornament). Terms: the "keep her" paragraph is explained. Dialogue clean — "Kessa's not *here*" is exactly her.

## Ch27 — What Holds
**DRIFTING — 6.5/10.** The best sustained Wick in the book for two-thirds; three passages hand over the wheel.

MUST
- "It wasn't ready for that. Nothing had ever held it. Everything it had ever touched either ran from it or opened to it or got swallowed by it." — omniscient tour of the monster's psychology. → "It didn't know what to do with her. She could feel it not knowing. Everything it ever touched ran or opened or got eaten, and she was doing none of those."
- "Not gone. She'd never thought it would be gone. / Just less. / For tonight, less was the best thing she'd ever held." — lands on the lesson (and the title); she lands on the next want. → "Not gone. Course not. / Just less. / She'd take less. She was starving and somebody up there had a fire."

SHOULD
- "That half-inch between *want to* and *did* was the only place she'd ever lived." — the true insight, framed as an Oscar distinction-between-abstractions, then aphorised. → "Wanting to and doing it weren't the same. Want to drop it, don't drop it. Every morning since she was eight."
- "keeping the door so she could shut this one. Nobody had told them to throw themselves at the night for her." — neat parallel figure, literary phrase. → "up there getting hurt so nothing else came down. Nobody told them to."
- "burning it would only feed it and opening to it would only end her" — balanced tactical summary at the crisis. → cut; straight to "You couldn't beat it."
- "Wick felt the wild let out a breath it had been holding for a season" → "something huge and far down went easy, like a dog finally lying down."
- "A girl who'd done that didn't have to be quite so afraid of the small one she carried." — moral-summary paragraph.

COULD: "a thing somebody had to manage" ("watch"). Lexicon: "The deep current … went *clean*" — her profile gives her "the deep"; check.

Checks: dark-funny yes ("It wasn't funny."; "insufferable about him being right"). Fear → piling: yes at the pull, then the half-inch paragraph goes long and orderly exactly at the lip. Dialogue: "Am I still me?" is perfect.

## Ch30 — Tea With the Woman Who Took Him
**DRIFTING, reveal section OFF VOICE — 5.5/10.** Stew opening and stair scene are peak Wick. The Ash interrogation ("Her name was Ash" → "You can see why I waited") is the book's dry adult narrator wearing her name.

MUST
- "The line had the worn-round shape of a joke a person keeps for themselves … spent a hundred years saying her best lines to an owl." — polished wit, adult sympathy; pure Oscar. → "Nobody laughed. It sat there on the table. For a second the witch looked old. Like she'd been telling that one to the owl."
- "she lost the grammar of her own fury" — "grammar" is on Oscar's vocabulary list. → "she lost the end of her own sentence."
- "like Wick was a page she'd been told about and was finally getting to read" — his metaphor field. → "like Wick was something she'd heard about and wanted a proper look at."

SHOULD
- "land on Oscar like a hand laid on a bruise, gentle and exactly on it, and watched him let it" → "and Wick saw it hit him. He didn't flinch. That was worse."
- "Looked like a man hearing the second telling of a story, checking it against the first." — Oscar's analytical framing borrowed to describe him.
- "like a coin she didn't expect change from … in an order she had clearly decided on before any of them was born" — dry narrator wit.
- "like a housekeeper, like a queen … the word *Everset* lying on the table in the candle-grease" — antithesis plus curated closing image. → "And *Everset* just sat there in the middle of the table where nobody wanted to pick it up."
- "like a thing far off rolling over in its sleep, starting to dream about waking up" — the door as literary image. → "the door shoved once against the latch. Her stomach went with it."

COULD: "two days into good sleep and better arguments"; "wearing its clothes"; "reading something that had no seam"; "couldn't decide between floors".

Checks: dark-funny yes ("you got kidnapped wrong"; "You're three hundred years old."). Little fear; her anger piles correctly ("I *ate* the bread, it's good bread"). Dialogue all hers.

## Ch36 — The Door, Getting Louder
**DRIFTING — 7/10.** Dialogue-driven and the dialogue is excellent; leaks are two narration blocks.

MUST
- "No scream, no *event.* He simply got less. It started at his edges and went inward, and it was quick and it was extremely tidy" (plus "That was what Wick would keep, after") — a man is erased and she narrates like a coroner; calm at the moment of horror. → "Not blood. No scream. Nothing. He just got *less.* Edges first. Then the rest. His bundle hit the dust and rolled and it was still there and he wasn't."

SHOULD
- "It was the far side, the place that had wanted her since before she had a word for anything. And here … the wall between it and her had gone thin enough to lean on." — the far side explained on page one. → cut; "Her sternum had ached for two days. This morning it was *pressing.*"
- "*paused* there like a page being turned" / "page-turn" — his metaphor, twice. → "like somebody'd put a hand on their chest."
- "It should have been terrifying, and it was almost the opposite: somebody had finally admitted out loud … as big as it felt." — feeling explained, balanced antithesis. → "Weirdly it helped. Somebody had finally said it was as big as it felt. Eight years, and it was him, in a ditch."
- "she was going to be the one who saw it coming." — role-summary landing. → "She was the only one who could feel it. / Great."

COULD: "not hunger exactly. / It was *interest.*" — concept where a stomach-drop belongs (fine once; Ch40 reuses it verbatim); "in the manner of a man who'd already worked something out".

Checks: dark-funny yes ("Everset had a queue"; "I'm going to be rude to you about it"). Fear → piling: yes after the stagger, no during the erasure. Dialogue: the ice-over-a-pond line is the chapter's best; nothing adult.

## Ch40 — What She Gives
**DRIFTING — 6.5/10.** Emotionally the truest of the six ("I've got loads."; the string-you-burn paragraph). Leaks cluster where plot mechanics need explaining, and the narrator reaches for Oscar's lexicon to do it.

MUST
- "all that cold careful written light, an age of striking things out, pouring into a mouth that had wanted everything since before there was anything to want" — his vocabulary plus a cosmological aphorism, at her death. → "all that cold light, all of it, going down into a mouth that had been hungry longer than anything."
- "a hole isn't written, and there's no clause for it and no board and no stylus and no mark." — she explains why the trick works, in his grammar. → "There wasn't anybody there. There was a hole. It went over her twice, three times, like a clerk who'd lost his place — and then it didn't know what else to do."

SHOULD
- "All her life the far side had been behind something. She'd never seen the something" … "Not her door. Her door was *her.*" — three paragraphs of wall/door mechanics before a body beat. → "Her sternum wasn't aching. That was how she knew. There'd always been something to ache against."
- "Kessa had done the sum in the same second he had and come out the same place and hated it, and everyone on that floor knew it" — omniscient, orderly, Kessa's idiom, mid-panic. → "Kessa didn't move. Kessa, who always moved. Her face had gone wrong and Wick got it and hated her for a second."
- "She wanted that put right, afterward, if there was an afterward" — editorial self-correction frame (his "more precisely" move). Keep the distinction, drop the frame.
- "she understood the price about half a second after it stopped mattering: a door has to be *held* by something" — noticing-too-late-and-noting-it is Oscar's tic; the mechanics after the colon are explained. "I've got loads" does the work alone.
- "counting was the only prayer anybody in this crew had ever taught her" / "a man who counts things does not look away from the reckoning" — two aphoristic tags; the second states Tarn's idiom as a general truth.

COULD: "as if none of them had ever been the point"; "an age of patient nobodies"; "Wick's whole body understood it before any of the rest of her did" (tells body-first instead of doing it).

Checks: dark-funny yes, several. Fear → piling: yes ("Eleven paces." "She got to the chair."). Dialogue: "So don't use the world." is her one perfect-line-at-the-perfect-moment; the guardrail allows one, this is it.

## Ch42 — The Far Side (light pass)
**ON VOICE — 8.5/10.** The rewrite holds: short, physical, hungry, funny ("a dog that had got at the ham and wasn't sorry"; "and a wet nose"). Ends on action.
- COULD: "You don't eat the door. You keep the door." followed at once by "You can't leave a hole behind." — two aphoristic landings in one section; trim the second. Nothing else.

## Summary

**Worst → best:** Ch30 (5.5) · Ch12 (6) · Ch27 (6.5) · Ch40 (6.5) · Ch36 (7) · Ch42 (8.5).

**Three most common leak patterns:**
1. **Mystery explained instead of felt** — door / wall / far side as image or mechanism, or the thing's intentions narrated: ~11 (Ch12 ×2, Ch27 ×2, Ch36 ×2, Ch40 ×4, Ch42 ×1).
2. **Polished / aphoristic landings** — paragraph or chapter ends on a lesson or curated image: ~12 (Ch12 ×3, Ch27 ×3, Ch30 ×2, Ch36 ×2, Ch40 ×2). Ch27's "less" and Ch36's "saw it coming" are the clearest lesson-landings.
3. **Oscar's lexicon and dry adult wit in her narration** — grammar, page, reading, seam, clause, written, "event", plus balanced antitheses: ~11 (Ch30 ×6, Ch36 ×2, Ch40 ×2, Ch12 ×1). Ch30 is essentially this pattern.

Also: calm, organised syntax at the peak of fear (Ch12 door, Ch36 erasure, Ch40 Kessa) ×3; and the narrator tic "since before she had a word for anything" (Ch36, Ch40 ×2), which is nobody's voice.

**Fix scope:**
- **Real pass, scoped:** Ch30, only "Her name was Ash" → "You can see why I waited" — re-narrate through her body (third bowl going cold, who's looking at her, heat); leave opening and stairs alone. Ch12, the door block ("and there was more there than there had ever been" → "It shut.") plus the coda ("The part that stuck…" to end).
- **Line edits (6–8 each):** Ch27 (monster paragraph, half-inch, "wild let out a breath", ending); Ch40 (opening mechanics, "put right afterward", "written light", "no clause", two tags); Ch36 (erasure paragraph, far-side sentence, page-turn ×2, two landings).
- **Leave:** Ch42 (one optional trim).

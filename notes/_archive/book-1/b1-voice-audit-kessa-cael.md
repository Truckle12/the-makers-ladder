## Chapter 25 — Who Comes Knocking (Kessa) — **DRIFTING, 6/10**

The fight is hers; the last page is Oscar's.

- **MUST** — "The ones who meant it were the only people worth following. They were also the ones who got your people killed. Both, in her experience. Always both." — chapter closes on a four-beat epigram, the profile's named failure. *Alt: end at "Five. For now." — or: "Five. She kept the knife across her knees and did not sleep."*
- **MUST** — "calling it mercy—and meaning it, which was the part Kessa chewed on longest" — the guardrail's reserved "that was the part" pivot, used to interpret.
- **SHOULD** — "He was buying space with his skin. He was good at it. It was, she thought distantly, a terrible thing to be good at." — paragraph lands on a moral. *Alt: "He was buying space with his skin. Three lines, forearm, deep enough to matter later."*
- **SHOULD** — "because it didn't have to come through the wall if it could simply decline to be where the wall was" — witty paradox cadence. *Alt: "under the line. Cael's feet were planted; it went past them low."*
- **SHOULD** — "even when the math is bad, *especially* when the math is bad" — arithmetic metaphor. *Alt: "even when the count is wrong, especially then."*
- **SHOULD** — "A fox walked at her heel like a held breath." — ornamental simile. *Alt: "A fox walked at her heel. It didn't look at the crew either."*
- **SHOULD** — "a man who'd just met the only other person who spoke his language, and was already counting how little of it he'd get" — she narrates Oscar's interior in polished summary. *Alt: "watching the dark where the Warden had gone, and not blinking enough."*
- **COULD** — "which she would think about later" / "didn't have time to understand how much she'd hate it later" — Oscar's noticing-too-late tic, twice.

**Right:** "'Up,' she said, not loud, never loud, just the word that meant *now*… a bolt through the center of the reaching mass from six feet because that was the shot and you took the shot." / "She counted them. Five, breathing, bleeding, hers."

**Dialogue:** clean. "That's the toll, then." / "Marked." are the target.

## Chapter 29 — The Trail That Ends at a Door (Kessa) — **ON VOICE, 8/10**

The best Kessa in the book. The road-reading (lines 29–51) and the lane/hedge/manse reads (73, 95) are exactly the register — ruts with no weather, forty windows and no wagon, grass unbruised at the wall foot. Only the arrival page slips.

- **MUST** — "the hostage-math, the woman's throat under the knife if the numbers went to the worst place… and every sum she'd carried up that lane came apart in her hands" — arithmetic stacked twice. *Alt: "the doors, the smoke, the woman's throat under the knife if it went that way… and every plan she'd carried up that lane had nothing to hold."*
- **SHOULD** — "A trap she could read was Tuesday." — modern quip, Oscar's wit. *Alt: "A trap she could read. This she couldn't."*
- **SHOULD** — "and could not have said which of the two cost more." — balanced abstract close. *Alt: "Kessa stepped over the threshold. She kept her hand off the knife. It took some doing."*
- **SHOULD** — "which no captor in the history of holding anything had ever done" — hyperbolic aside. *Alt: "out of the line between them and the hall. Kessa had never seen a captor do that."*
- **SHOULD** — "her whole trade in ruins around her" — rhetorical. *Alt: cut; the "Five places" count that follows does the work.*
- **SHOULD** — "a faith that was worse to hold than the arm strapped wrong on Tarn would ever be" — abstract emotional comparison. *Alt: "staring at Kessa. Kessa looked at the arm instead."*
- **COULD** — "built complete this morning out of the idea of wealth" — "the idea of ruts" earlier is a tracker's phrase; "idea of wealth" is Oscar's.

**Right:** "Not faded. Not crossed. Not stepped over or brushed out or lost on hard ground — she knew every one of those and had beaten every one of them. These just ended." / "Taken means there's a place he is. Places have ground. Ground talks."

**Dialogue:** "It's a good lane. Empty hedges. Sloppy." — perfect. Nothing leaks.

## Chapter 32 — The Road South, Again (Kessa) — **DRIFTING, 5/10**

Her tally has become a ledger ("column" ×4, "sum/sums" ×3), and she narrates Oscar's mind in his own vocabulary.

- **MUST** — "the moment the man who read everything realized there was nothing here for reading to do. No seam. No script. A country closing like a hand, and the only text in it a choice." — seam/script/text are Oscar's nouns; Kessa cannot think them. *Alt: "Oscar had gone still while she talked and stayed still after. She'd watched him hunt seams in things for months. His face said there wasn't one."*
- **MUST (dialogue)** — "So we spend the story… He's built for a world where nobody stands… There's no story about the time somebody stood" — Kessa theorizing narrative. *Alt: "He doesn't lose them. So he's never had to fight for one. Collar, cordon, wage-men, waiting. That's a man nobody's ever stood against. We stood at the rim of the grey with worse odds and no walls."*
- **MUST** — "it had taken her until this morning to see she'd been on the wrong side of her own craft since Oressa. Reading ground to leave it. Time to read it to stay." — thematic chiasmus landing. *Alt: "Water, walls, two doors. She'd been picking ground to leave since Oressa."*
- **SHOULD** — "in her own head, where the sums were kept" / "she'd already done this sum too" / "ran the sums, which was what she had instead of prayers" — three ledger metaphors. *Alt: "where she kept the count" / "she'd walked this one already at dawn" / "ran the count, which was what she had instead of prayers".*
- **SHOULD** — "the column that had no name yet and was getting longer" / "the nameless column" / "the column of things that might matter later" — bookkeeping device. *Keep one "tally"; retire "column".*
- **SHOULD** — "the decision arriving not as an idea but as a settling, weight coming down onto a foot" — the not-X-but-Y house move; the foot half is hers. *Alt: "All right, she thought. Weight came down onto the foot that had been waiting."*
- **SHOULD** — "lean like a thing on the very lip of yes" — ornament. *Alt: "And the bird would lean, every time. Lean and not come."*
- **SHOULD** — "because it had started to hurt in a way that wasn't tactical" — abstract emotional summary. *Alt: cut the clause.*
- **COULD** — "She had an instrument for it, and the instrument was Wick's face" — "instrument" is his.

**Right:** "One horse. Two men. A House pennon, small, no bigger than a kerchief, hung where it could be seen from the track and nowhere else. Not hunting. Sitting." / "Water, walls, sight-lines, two doors… What's wrong with it?" / the close, "Come on, then… Come collect."

## Chapter 33 — The Waystation (Cael) — **DRIFTING, 5/10**

- **MUST** — "the mending half of him reading the breakage as surely as Oscar read script. He could have told anyone… what the shield's rim wrote there." — read/script/wrote. *Alt: "He felt through his arm what it did. He'd mended enough of them to know. Nobody would ever have to ask."*

- **MUST** — "because that was the vow, and the vow was the only door he knew to keep standing in." — closing benediction. *Alt: end at "went to see about the living."*
- **SHOULD** — "Brave men carry their fear gathered and pointed. This was absence." — epigram; the body-read around it is his. *Alt: "Brave men are afraid and walk anyway. This one wasn't afraid. There was nothing there to be."*
- **SHOULD** — "slow had always been the price of the rest of him" — aphorism. *Alt: "He was slow. He'd always been slow."*
- **SHOULD** — "which was all a shield ever promised" — neat close on the shield paragraph. *Alt: "It had been there for the weight."*
- **SHOULD** — "the whole fight hung on a long-dead kindness, roused by the one man alive who could still read the hand it was written in" — thematic summary. *Alt: "It held one door. The waypost had two."*
- **SHOULD** — "like men given back their lives, which they had been" — witty self-correction. *Alt: "Cael let them."*
- **SHOULD** — "the very sum they'd built the walls for" — arithmetic. *Alt: "what they'd built the walls for."*
- **COULD** — "like a floor swept every day for a thousand years by nobody"; "a man making an inventory"; "Cael marked what it cost her" (Kessa's verb).

**Right:** "Cael put the two men off his shield and left the door." / "Corwen went down the way a coat slides off a chair." / "He put two fingers to the finder's eyes and closed them, because his hands would not agree to leave a dead man staring."

**Dialogue:** on voice throughout — "It'll ask him to stop. Hard… Most men sit down." / "Go home whole."

## Chapter 34 — Thin Country (Kessa) — **OFF VOICE, 4/10**

The tally is now bookkeeping ("column" ×6, "ledger" ×3 in narration, "entry/entries", "sum/sums"), and she interprets Wick instead of reading her.

- **MUST** — "Life added up smaller the farther south she counted, a ledger with no false entries and a page quietly missing" — ledger. *Alt: "Less life every mile south. Nothing missing you could point at. Just less."*
- **MUST** — "She put it in the nameless column, and the nameless column was most of the book now." / "Oscar was no help on it, and that was its own entry." *Alt: "She set it aside with the rest. There was more set aside now than kept." / "Oscar was no help on it."*
- **MUST** — "It was nothing she could have put in front of a magistrate. It was a hundred small entries that each had an innocent answer" — proof/evidence field. *Alt: "Nothing she could have pointed at. A hundred small things, each with a reason, and all of them leaning one way."*
- **MUST** — "It was more like knocking. Far-off, patient, at long intervals, the knuckle-tap of something that knew the house was occupied and could wait for the door." — she narrates the meaning of Wick's fear. *Alt: "It wasn't pulling her. Her feet stayed in the line. She checked, touched the mark, walked on. Every water."*
- **MUST (dialogue)** — "I killed a man's story once in Oressa… Killing Corwen was meant to break a story like that" / "here's the price on the far pan" / "Don't spend it cheap" — story-theory plus scales. *Alt: "We killed Corwen so the paper would stop arriving. Five days of country since. Carts north. Farms leaning away. Game counted out. That's not a south missing its finder."*
- **MUST** — "There was nothing behind them anymore. Everything the road had left was ahead." — closing epigram, on top of "Nobody was closing the doors anymore. Nobody needed to." two paragraphs up. *Alt: "She caught herself not once checking the road behind them. She checked it then, on purpose. Empty."*
- **SHOULD** — "Some sums you didn't say over a man. You just carried them where he could see you carrying them." *Alt: "She marked it and kept her mouth shut, and stood his watch beside him where he could see her doing it."*
- **SHOULD** — "Nobody argued the ledger." / "the whole ugly ledger of the day before" — Tarn's field bleeding into her narration (his dialogue, "Ground collects fair", is correct).
- **SHOULD** — "pretending at her own crew was a coin she'd never spent" — coin metaphor. *Alt: "she didn't lie to her own crew."*
- **SHOULD** — "drama was for people who could afford to be wrong big" — epigram. *Cut.*
- **SHOULD** — "the count made flesh" — scriptural. *Alt: "one face at a time, five."*
- **SHOULD** — "which was a thing the hollow in the wild had bought and paid for" / "in the deliberate manner of hands being made to be steady" — interpretation + polish. *Alt: "That was new." / "her hands steady. Too steady."*

**Right:** "Skimmed. A deer's line here, old. One badger's habit, older. The birds went over high and kept going." / "the whole country standing the way Corwen had stood" (footing — hers) / the order of march speech is her dialogue at its best.

## Summary

**Ranked worst → best:** 34 (4) · 32 (5) · 33/Cael (5) · 25 (6) · 41 (6) · 29 (8).

**Three most common Kessa leaks (narration only):**
1. **Ledger vocabulary replacing tally/count** — "column" 12, "sum/sums/math" 7, "ledger" 3, "entry/entries" 3 ≈ **25 instances**, 18 of them in ch 32 and 34. The tally device is right; the words are Sarn's. Keep *tally, marks, sets aside, counts*; retire *column, sum, ledger, entry*.
2. **Aphoristic paragraph/section landings** — 25: 2, 29: 2, 32: 2, 34: 4, 41: 6 ≈ **16**. Every chapter ending except 32's is one.
3. **Narrating another mind in Oscar's terms** (seam/script/text/story; Wick's "knocking") — **5** passages (25 ×1, 32 ×2, 34 ×2), the ch 32 "spend the story" and ch 34 "killed a man's story" speeches being the two dialogue leaks.

**Fix scope:**
- **Real pass:** ch 34 (ledger device rebuilt throughout, Wick paragraph, fire speech, ending) and ch 32 (ledger device, Oscar-interior paragraph, "spend the story" speech, two landings). ~12 edits each, but they touch every section.
- **Real pass, Cael:** ch 33 — the collar sequence (lines 101–113), the two closing paragraphs, and a one-third simile thin.
- **Line edits only:** ch 41 (7 lines, mostly deleting a clause), ch 25 (closing paragraph + 4 lines), ch 29 (final two paragraphs + 3 lines).

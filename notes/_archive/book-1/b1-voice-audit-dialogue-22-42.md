# Dialogue Voice Audit — Chapters 22–42 (spoken lines only)

Scope: spoken dialogue of Oscar, Kessa, Cael, Wick, Tarn; others only where egregious. Narration excluded. Sources: the five POV profiles, `Oscar_StyleGuardrail.md` ("Per-character voice" + "Dialogue"), `book-bible.md` character summaries. Severity: MUST (breaks the character's stated rules), SHOULD (Oscar-bleed / too-perfect landing), COULD (borderline, roughen if convenient).

## Closing summary

**Chapters with the most dialogue drift (top 5):**
1. **Ch34** — Kessa's two fireside speeches (2 MUST + 1 SHOULD): ledger framing, chained aphorisms, "page he can't read ahead on."
2. **Ch32** — Kessa's rally ("built for a world where nobody stands") and Tarn's pit parable (2 MUST), plus "It's an appointment."
3. **Ch22** — Tarn's recruitment speeches (1 MUST, 2 SHOULD) and Cael's two polished lines.
4. **Ch30** — Cael's grief-read ("load-bearing"), Kessa's "Family's a fact," Oscar's triple-landing stairs scene.
5. **Ch23** — Tarn's cordon/wild lectures (1 MUST, 2 SHOULD), Oscar captioning the fingertip.
Honourable mention: **Ch37** (Cael's "It lives in kitchens" + two Kessa closers).

**Most common pattern:** the *briefing epigram* — a non-Oscar character summarizes the situation and closes on a balanced antithesis ("It's not a hiding place. It's an appointment." / "It isn't a monster. It's a bereavement." / "Cities aren't sealed. Cities leak."). Kessa does it most (about ten instances Ch28–37), Tarn second, Cael in his few speeches. The secondary pattern is the *scene-closing perfect line* landed by whoever speaks last (Ch22 Cael, Ch30 Oscar, Ch35 Kessa, Ch40 Wick). Wick is largely immune; Tarn recovers after Ch26.

**Recommended fix scope:** line-level, not structural. ~50 flagged lines across 14 chapters, 11 of them MUST. Order of work: (1) the 11 MUSTs — Kessa Ch32/34 ×3, Cael Ch24/30/37/38, Tarn Ch22/23/32, Wick Ch24; (2) Kessa's SHOULD run Ch28–37, where the fix is almost always "cut the last sentence"; (3) Tarn's Ch22–23 intro, breaking his lectures into answers; (4) consolidate Oscar's "reading isn't lifting" thesis to one on-page statement (Ch37) and roughen one landing each in Ch24 and Ch30. No scene needs rewriting; most fixes are deletions of a closing clause.

Method: each chapter read cold in its current form; log A/B read afterward; base consulted only where a passage snagged, and a word-level diff used only to locate the cut behind each snag. A mechanical scan over all sixteen files (doubled words, double spaces, space-before-punctuation, unbalanced quotes and italic asterisks per paragraph, lowercase after a full stop) came back clean. Every "kept" claim in both logs that a later chapter depends on was checked and holds (list below).

## 1. DAMAGE

Verdict: roughly a hundred cuts across these sixteen chapters left **two SEAMs, one NIT, no BREAKs**. Everything else that snagged on the cold read is identical in the base (listed at the end so nobody re-hunts it as cut damage).

**Ch6 L11 — SEAM.** `The script on the left-hand stone was wrong. This was the opposite of damage.` Log A #1 cut `Not damaged.` between the two sentences. Without it, "the opposite of damage" answers a question the reader was never asked — a half-beat of *what damage?* before "cleaned" rescues it. The paragraph now holds no "Not X." fragment, so restoring costs nothing under rule 3. **Fix:** restore `Not damaged.` (or `was wrong, though not damaged. This was the opposite of damage.`).

**Ch7 L41 — SEAM.** `And he wanted it. That was the thing he would not be able to explain to them afterward…` This paragraph now directly follows Cael's speech (`"…The kind ones don't have to talk you into them."`), so "he" reads as Cael for a clause. In the base it was a *return* to a wanting already stated at L27 (`He had spent a day discovering all the ways he was diminished. And here was the thing that would mend all of it at once… The font was not lying.`). Log A #2 cut that first statement and kept this one as the carrier — right choice, but the carrier is now the first statement and opens on an unanchored pronoun after another man's paragraph. **Fix:** `And Oscar wanted it.` (one word).

**Ch13 L47 — NIT.** `*Worse* than unknown, then, Oscar thought.` Log B #6 folded away `Not unknown, then,` so the comparative has no stated baseline. Kessa's `Never met anyone who'd seen one` supplies it well enough. No fix.

## 2. OSCAR'S REGISTER

No sentence in these chapters was rewritten plainer. Every Group A/B edit is a deletion or a fragment fold, and I compared each fold against the base:
- The folds keep his syntax — the colon-list, the subordinate clause, the italic stress: `before: not fear, but a deep devotional unease` (Ch7); `before: not suspicion, nearer to dread` (Ch13); `The second was that he had not the faintest idea why` (Ch11 — if anything more him than the base's `he had no idea why`).
- One fold trimmed a *classification* rather than a caption, the only place his precision thinned: Ch14 L15 `was a shield. Not a salvager's buckler. A real shield, broad and old` → `was a shield, a real one, broad and old`. The buckler/shield distinction was the scholar looking, not a caption. Not roughening; flagged for the author's judgment only. (Ch15 L103 `It was a fortune. Not in coin. In *workings*` → `a fortune in *workings*` loses nothing.)
- Ch3 L79 `"No." Plainly.` looks like a "flatly" substitute but is base text; the edit removed only the `Not unkind.` after it.

Everywhere else the conclusion survives in his mouth (Ch5 `Someone's been here before me`; Ch6 `I know what it's *for*`; Ch11 `That's about me… That paper's about me`; Ch13 `Here is the proof`; Ch15 `One hand… Same seal. ## 5. RESIDUE

The four worst remaining image → interpretation → statement stacks in these chapters (quoted, not fixed):

1. **Ch4 L53:** `The argument stopped. Something went out of the room and something colder came into it, and Oscar understood, a half-second too late, as he understood most things that weren't written on a page, that he had walked his clever feet directly into a grave.` — and Kessa then says exactly this aloud: `"You just *say* it… Straight out the mouth."`

He was doing the thing again, stepping into the wheel-shaped gap.` — the same beat as Ch4 L79 (`watched all three of them hear the authority in it, and watched none of them object`) and Ch9 L49; the wheel-gap gloss has now been paid three times.

# Book 1 — Reader Gauntlet: Synthesis

**Method.** Five simulated cold readers, each a distinct persona, each read all 43 chapters in
order with no access to any editorial material (no bible, no guardrail, no notes). Each logged
reactions after chapters 9, 17, 28, 35, and 43, then answered four experience questions (where
did you skim / where did you stop believing / where did you feel something / retell the book
from memory) plus persona-specific audits. Run 2026-08-15 on the post-Batch-4 text (125.5k
words). Caveat stated plainly: these are model-simulated readers — the same family of blind
spots that wrote the book. They are a *rehearsal* for human beta readers, not a substitute.
Their convergences are strong signals; their unanimities are near-certainties; anything only
one persona felt is a hypothesis.

**The panel.** Marta (58, casual reader, guiltless DNFer) · Dev (34, genre veteran, Goodreads
avg 3.1) · June (17, YA-crossover, teen-authenticity radar) · Priya (41, literary reader,
prose-first, machine-cadence suspicious) · Tom (45, commute reader, momentum-only).

---

## Headline

**Five out of five finished willingly. Zero DNF points — from a panel designed to DNF.**
Marta finished at 2:40 a.m. "on a school night"; Tom let two trains pass during Ch41; June read
the last ten chapters "in one uninterrupted feral sitting"; Dev rated it 4/5 against a 3.1
average; Priya — the persona built to distrust it — "never hit a genuine quit-point," which she
calls rare for genre-on-assignment. **All five would read Book 2, and all five want it to open
on Wick.** Ratings where given: 4.5 (June), 4.5-rounds-to-5 (Marta), 4 (Dev, "a real four").

**What is unanimously working (protect list — never sand these):** Ch28 (five out of five —
the temptation-as-rest scene is the book's consensus masterpiece); Ch38–41 as a sustained
finale run; "Zero." / "Marked." (named by all five as the hardest moment); the Ch42 rat (all
five, several crying); the count ritual as the book's emotional spine; Ch4's knife-sharpening;
the Ch32 owl-lean; the Setter entire ("I was, I'm told, a promising person" — stuck-line for
two readers); the four adult voices being instantly tellable apart; villains-as-clerks; the
magic system (Dev: passes the hard-magic standard, costs enforced at the climax); the ending's
honesty (no group hug, no cheap reunion).

## Convergent findings, ranked by independent votes

**1. The middle sags, epicenter Ch18–24 (5/5).** Dev: "a 43-chapter book with a 38-chapter
story," every cut living between Ch10 and Ch33. Tom: the one place a reader "sets the book on
the nightstand and maybe doesn't pick it up" (Ch18–20). Marta: skimmed Ch20–24, least-favorite
Ch24. Priya: skimmed the Ch10/24 connective tissue. June: skimmed early-lore instead but is the
same complaint one arc earlier. **Ch20 "Banked" is the individual consensus target** — Tom's
single lowest chapter ("the only chapter I'd have skipped entirely without loss"), Dev's
"cuttable entire" (fold its two load-bearing beats into Ch21), Marta's and Tom's shared skim
zone: it re-explains Ch12's coal/door interiority.

**2. Stop explaining images — "cut the captions" (3/5, and the two hardest graders' #1 note).**
Dev's one thing: "~40 sentences that tell me what the metaphor meant; each one is a small tax
on trust." Priya's one thing: "strike the statement and let the event stand… the reader you
want is precisely the one your captions insult." Tom, independently: the prose "says a good
thing and then says it again fancier — I take the first one and move." Both name the same
exhibit: Ch8's "He was built to *aim* one" — the exact line Batch 4 kept as the single
statement. The compression pass kept one caption per image; the strongest readers want zero.
Counter-evidence, honestly: Marta and June quote several captioned lines as favorites. This is
now an **author-taste decision** (see Recommended next actions).

**3. Believability wobbles, shared:** Ch17's Marrow revolution flips too fast (Dev "cheapest
victory in the book," Tom "felt myself deciding to go with it," June "the vote takes half a
page") — note this matches the bible's own deferred backlog item ("uprising friction Ch16–17").
Ch25's spring re-look reads authorially triggered (Tom + Marta). Ch27's calm about sending Wick
down alone (Tom + June). Ch34's zero-casualty arithmetic (Dev). Ch29's perfectly timed rescue
(Marta, self-retracted two chapters later).

**4. Dropped threads cold readers tracked (now addressed — see Actions taken):** the Redactor
never returns (Dev: "biggest unfired gun in the book"; Tom; Marta tracked it for twenty
chapters) → fired in the Book 2 outline. Sarn's promised report evaporates (Marta, Dev) → paid
with one line in Ch29. Tarn never reacts on-page to Oscar being a made thing (Marta) → paid
with a beat in Ch35. Quill's landing happened between paragraphs (Tom: "I earned that beat and
you skipped it"; the arrival and strike were on-page, the landing was not) → landed on the page
in Ch40, deliberately reusing Ch9's dream phrasing. The frost-figure in Ch1 stays unnamed (Tom,
Dev both self-stitched "an earlier copy that never arrived") → left as-is; the self-stitch is
the intended reading and Book 2's outline now owns it.

**5. Reader comprehension is excellent — the memory tests all pass.** All five ~150-word
retells are accurate end-to-end; every reader correctly reconstructed the Evermere→Everset
crime, the copy mechanism, the sacrifice mechanics, and the token's significance. Three of five
independently stated Oscar is **the eleventh copy** (correct inference from the Setter's
"five through ten never arrived") — the count is legible and is now recorded in the Book 2
outline. Names that fell out of everyone's memory: Hauk and Borr (fine — they're local color).
Marta's honest confusions worth noting: Evermere/Everset read as *her own* mistake for ten
chapters (intended, and she forgave it, but it cost trust mid-book); the Ch4 sleep-stirring
(Quill's bond) was never connected to its payoff by any reader — consider one clarifying echo
at Ch32, or accept it as bonus texture.

**6. The footprints hook works, with a split verdict on identity:** Doss (June, Marta — "if
it's Doss I will buy ten copies"), the original Oscar (Tom, Dev), an un-arrived copy (Priya).
All five listed it as a reason they'd buy Book 2. The three-way split is healthy ambiguity;
Book 2's Open Decision 1 stands, with the poll as data.

**7. Wick is the book (5/5) and her narration still slips register (2/5 + the fellow author).**
June's audit: top-5% of adult-written teens, dialogue never once broke — but a handful of
narration lines are "an adult's polish on her thought" (she cites "the trick was being stronger
than what you were for" and "a coat in a shop you can't afford," both deliberate keeps from the
Batch 4 ration). Priya frames the same thing book-wide: real polyphony of perception,
"monophony of rhythm." Two independent votes against those specific keeps = the ration was one
notch too conservative in her chapters.

**8. Priya's machine-cadence verdict (1/5, the persona designed for it — report in full
honesty):** "Yes, this reads machine-polished to me in long stretches." Her mechanisms: every
chapter lands a stinger dismount (43/43); italicized pivot-words 5–8 per chapter; epigrams
issued evenly to every character regardless of station; an immaculate Chekhov inventory ("a
human book leaves lint"); and — her sharpest evidence — five local stitching errors "of
something rewriting passages brilliantly without rereading the whole." All five errors were
verified real and are now fixed (see Actions taken). Her counterweight: Ch28 and Ch41 read
"most human — syntax ragged in the right places," and she notes the book's own theme kept
answering her suspicion at its emotional peaks. The other four raised no authorship suspicion.

## Actions taken from the gauntlet (all committed to `claude/session-cass6g`)

1. **Five stitching errors fixed** (Priya): Ch16 broken mid-speech quote join (a compression
   artifact — the spans were verified byte-identical while the join was not: a blind spot in
   that verification now understood); Ch20 verbless sentence (a cadence-conversion scar);
   Ch28's fabricated "the Warden's brother" callback deleted (no such figure; it contradicted
   the adjacent "she'd known that without anybody telling her"); Ch27 doubled nobody-kindness
   construction varied; Ch25 "the evenings" anchored ("its evenings").
2. **Quill's landing put on the page** (Tom) — two short paragraphs in Ch40 between "Oh," and
   the Setter's "You *called* it," reusing Ch9's dream phrasing ("talons in the wool, a brush
   of feathers at his ear") as the deliberate payoff of that dream.
3. **Sarn's report paid** (Marta) — one sentence in Ch29 as the binding lands: "Somewhere
   south, a scarred tallyman had kept their word to the letter: *exactly who, exactly where,
   exactly what you can do.*"
4. **Tarn's made-thing beat** (Marta) — Ch35, at the breaking of Corwen's cane: "Made all
   through… So are you. You settle up like a man, though."
5. **Book 2 outline amended:** the Redactor fired (recovered by the Provost's restoration as
   Everset's corrector); rival copy numbered the twelfth; footprint poll recorded.

## Recommended next actions — author decisions, not yet executed

1. **The caption pass (the big one).** Dev counts ~40 image-explaining sentences; he and Priya
   independently made it their single note, and it extends the fellow author's "trust the
   metaphor" verdict one step further than Batch 4 went. Proposed method: sweep every
   post-image summary sentence; cut where the image is strong; keep only where the line is
   *dialogue in character* (Oscar aphorizing out loud is voice, not caption). Risk: Marta and
   June love some of these lines; a zero-caption book is drier. My recommendation: run the pass
   at half-strength — cut the ~20 weakest, keep captions that are spoken, and re-run the
   gauntlet's Priya/Dev personas on the diff.
2. **Ch20 restructure.** Fold its two load-bearing beats (the half-confession; "it takes more
   to hold every day") into Ch21 and retire the chapter, per three readers. This is a
   structural cut (~2.3k words) — author's call.
3. **Ch16–17 uprising friction.** One ugly-week beat between the speech and the surrender —
   the deferred backlog item, now confirmed by three cold readers.
4. **Ch18–24 tightening** beyond Batch 4's cuts (Dev: Ch10+11 could merge; Ch22 minus a third;
   Ch33's first half). Diminishing returns territory — I'd rank it behind 1–3.
5. **Optional micro-stitch:** one clarifying echo at Ch32 tying Quill's bond to Ch4's
   sleep-stirring (no reader connected them unaided).

## What this gauntlet cannot tell you

The five readers share one training distribution. They cannot be bored the way a human is
bored, cannot lose Tuesday's thread by Friday, and may be systematically blind to whatever the
model family that wrote the book cannot see. Treat this as the dress rehearsal it is: the
convergent findings above are what to fix *before* the manuscript goes to two or three real
humans — and the four experience questions, which produced everything useful here, are the
right questions to hand them.

## CHAPTER 29 — "The Gate That Walks" (Oscar POV) — **REVISE**

Narrow REVISE. One genuine choreography error (the ambush arithmetic doesn't close) that a careful reader or the author will catch. Everything else — scope, de-centering, villain-logic, cadence — is strong-to-exemplary. Fix the count and this is a GO.

### Blocker sweep — ALL CLEAR
- **Reading the living/minds/nature:** CLEAN, and modelled. "His gift gave him nothing off the man himself — the living always returned him silence — so he used his eyes" (L67); "Oscar's gift gave him nothing off the men and everything off their kit" (L89). He reads only the binding, the cudgel loop-marks, the House brand, the wax/mark — all made. The attempt to read Ash's fold "closed on nothing" (L163). No living-read.
- **Fraying as routine:** CLEAN — explicitly disclaimed. "It cost. Not fraying, he was rested and the wild had seen to that, but the working went out of him all at once" (L117).
- **Pet-name approval near Wick:** CLEAN. Corwen calls her "the girl" in his impersonal register; no approval pet-name anywhere.
- **D&D/game vocab:** CLEAN. "the drive" is the sanctioned reskin; loop-mark / binding / unstitch / working are lexicon.
- **Tarn called big:** CLEAN. Tarn is fists/half-step/forty-years; "big" attaches only to Cael (L23).
- **Tarn's pact-hook spent:** CLEAN. "Forty years" = pit experience, not the hook; the hook is not read or referenced.
- **Crew death:** CLEAN. Oscar is captured (then blinked away by Ash), not killed; Doss was already dead.
- **Wick's private terms (coal) leaking:** CLEAN. Zero "coal." Her fire is rendered externally ("the mark... sullen red," "the air around her had gone to shimmer"). "door" appears only as Oscar's own metaphor for the workings/leash he opens — not Wick's private door.

### Beat-by-beat findings

**1. [REVISE — the headline] The ambush is six men but the choreography spends ~seven-to-eight.**
- The force is fixed at six: "the pale reeds... came apart into men, four of them... And the ridge shed two more down its scree. Six." (L85–87), reconfirmed "there were four more" after Tarn's two (L97).
- Ledger of neutralizations, in order: Tarn drops 2 (L93–96) → Cael's ward folds 1 ("ran his shin into it and folded," L101) → Kessa takes 2 (scree-slide + Doss's-knife arm-cut, L105) → Oscar then unmakes **three** cudgels and drives one of those men clear (L115). That is 2 + 1 + 2 + 3 = **eight actions on six men.**
- It also fights its own tally: Kessa says "six down to three standing" (L107) — i.e. 3 still up — but by that line the running beats have already neutralized 5 (Tarn 2, Cael 1, Kessa 2), which would leave only 1 standing, not 3.
- Problem: Stage-6 legibility. The enemy count is the spine of the escape's "earned"-ness, and it doesn't reconcile either way you anchor it.
- Suggested lightest fix (reviser's pick; pure numeric, no restructure):
  - (a) **Raise the ambush to eight.** "six of them" from the reeds + "two more" from the ridge; Kessa's line becomes "eight down to three standing." Then Tarn 2 + Cael 1 + Kessa 2 + Oscar 3 = 8, closed. Keeps every beat intact.
  - (b) **Close it at six and lean the extra weight off Oscar.** Make Cael's ward a general repel (not a headcount), have Oscar unmake **two** cudgels + drive (acts on 2, not 3), and set Kessa's line to "six down to two standing." Nets 2 + 2 + 2 = 6. Bonus: trims Oscar's share of the fight, which serves the de-centering goal.
  - Either is a one-to-two-line change. (Not flagged `[FOR THE AUTHOR]` — this is a continuity number, well inside reviser latitude.)

**2. [MINOR] Wick is the only crew member with no physical beat in this fight.** Tarn (fists), Kessa (ground + knife), Cael (ward), and Oscar (unmaking + drive) all land; Wick "would not move," and her whole beat is being refused ("You can't... Fire feeds it," L127).
- This is not a passenger error — it is dramatized helplessness carrying real load (the withheld apprentice-word "he's my —" at L125; Corwen weighing whether "the one not on his paper was about to make herself a line on it," L127) and it does honest magic-economy work (establishes the binding is fire-proof, so her gift can't be the answer). It is also right on the heels of her Ch28 solo triumph, so she has nothing to prove here.
- Flagging only per the persona's instruction to say so explicitly: her inertness is **intentional and paid off** — keep it. If a reviser wants a hair more presence without giving her a tactical role, the single cleanest touch is a beat of her heat spiking uselessly against the cold binding (visible cost of wanting to burn what she can't), which she already gestures at. No change required.

**3. [MINOR] The post-escape Corwen monologue runs long after the action climax.** L131–143 is roughly a page of villain exposition (the "permitted" turn, "Kit. I'll draw more," the private-wax mark, "you learn what you are for") standing between the getaway and Ash's blink.
- It is deliberate and largely earned — it is where the chapter seeds the House's-own-seal / "the seat" dread and stages the Ash entrance — but choreographically it deflates the temperature: the fight is over and Oscar is inert (bound) while Corwen talks.
- Suggested lightest fix: none required if the seed-work is wanted intact. If trimming, the most compressible line is Corwen's second aside about the kit ("Kit. I'll draw more," L135) — the indifference already lands in the withheld-word beat and the mark exposition. Leave the "getting away had been *permitted*" turn (L133) untouched; it is the thematic hinge.

**4. [TRIVIAL — for the canon-keeper, not the reviser] Drive-origin phrasing.** The drive is described as "the slab of shoved air he'd found in the reeds by the spring" (L115); the bible logs the drive as landing Ch26 (the spring is Ch25). Likely the same continuous wild/spring sequence, so probably fine — just confirm the drive's first-use chapter isn't being quietly re-attributed. Not a fight issue.

### KEEP (do not sand these off)
- **Scope demonstration is a model to copy.** L67 and L89 show exactly how to keep reading MADE-only mid-fight (silence off the man/the men, everything off the kit) without stalling the scene. Preserve verbatim.
- **The self-save that fails is the chapter's engine.** Oscar reaches for his own leash first, reflexively, and can't get a nail under it because the binding "had written the counter to his reading into the reading itself" (L83, L111). He then stops reaching for himself and spends his last on the crew's gap. Potent-but-not-self-saving, exactly to brief. This is the beat that keeps him from over-centering — he can free everyone but himself.
- **The escape is authored by Kessa, not Oscar.** "That's the only sum that closes" (L107) — she reads the funnel-country ("It funnels," L55), calls the retreat, and picks the ground. Oscar's working only opens the physical gap. The win is genuinely distributed.
- **Corwen's choice to let them run is believable in his own terms.** He has Oscar bound, the others aren't on his paper, and the country funnels them back: "a thing that runs from a gate it can't shut is only choosing where to be caught" (L143); "Kit. I'll draw more." His indifference (not mercy, not sadism) is consistent with Ch33's read of him and the bible's "the dread is indifference." The reader believes the spare. This is the strongest thing in the chapter.
- **Fraying handled correctly** (named only to exclude it; cost attributed to spending a working all at once). Keep — though "grey and swaying... paying down to fumes" is the outer edge of acceptable; don't let a future pass push it toward overdraw-damage.
- **Body-before-mind at the binding:** "Oscar felt it before he understood it... the body first, the sense second. Cold closed on his wrists" (L81).
- **Cost is real and multi-headed:** Tarn's shoulder / "one arm hanging wrong" (L95, L129), Cael's spent ward (L101), Oscar spent to fumes, Wick's scream, Kessa "hating the sum" — and the chapter's true cost, Oscar taken.

### Beat-by-beat findings

**1. `[FOR THE AUTHOR]` — Corwen's finder-apparatus cancels Cael's GRANTED/devotional ward.** "He doesn't cut the working, he strikes out the warrant for it" (L77); Cael's "banked warmth... went grey and papery" (L75). Waking-and-then-unwriting the dead-maker *keeping* (L79–87) is unambiguously in-bounds — it is made work. But Cael's warmth is **granted/devotional** (a different source — the hall to what he answers to), and whether the House's unwriting can revoke a devotional working is a **new magic-interaction** not previously on the page.
- The prose has anticipated the problem elegantly: Corwen strikes the **warrant** (the authorization), not the source — consistent with the House's whole conceit that all magic must be licensed. It does **not** contradict the locked decision that "Cael's devotional power is unaffected at Everset" (different mechanism, different place). But it is a meaningful new rule and it slightly softens Cael's House-independence, so it wants the author's yes.
- Options (do not resolve here):
  - (a) **Ratify as written.** The House can locally strike the *warrant* of any working, granted included; the fact that ending the old *keeping* costs Corwen a whole second working (L87) already shows granted/old work resists him. Raises Corwen's menace; reads clean.
  - (b) **Narrow it** — Corwen doesn't cancel Cael's warmth, he overwhelms/outlasts it — preserving "granted power is House-proof." Costs a rework of the crux.
  - (c) **Clarify (a)** on the page — one beat making explicit that what Corwen strikes is the doorway's *standing/authorization*, not the warmth's divine source, so the "warrant" line reads as a rule, not a one-off. Smallest touch; keeps the beat.

**2. [MINOR watch] "grey strain" (L83).** "Grey" is the exact word the manuscript uses for Oscar's spent state in Ch29 ("grey and swaying"). Here it is fine — a modest strain from holding a foreign old working, and he's rested — but the reviser should keep it a strain of *effort*, not let it read as fraying/overdraw. No change needed now; just don't amplify it.

**3. [MINOR] Final clause is faintly benediction-adjacent.** "went to see about the living, because that was the vow, and the vow was the only door he knew to keep standing in" (L135).
- Cael's profile bans neat benedictions. This mostly earns its landing — it resolves on an **action** (going to the living) and on the **door/vow** motif that has run all chapter, in Cael's own plain register — so it is not the abstract spiritual summary the profile warns against.
- Lightest option if a reviser wants it even cleaner: end on the action ("went to see about the living") and let the door-vow be implicit. But this is a genuine judgment call and the current landing is defensible — flagging only so it's a conscious keep, not a reflex.

**4. [TRIVIAL] "That was the thing" (L93)** is the one mild cadence tic among the three "that was the" hits (the L107 "price of the rest of him" and L135 "the vow" landings are genuine). Optional single-word vary; not required.

## 0. Where Arc 4 starts (carry-in state from Ch28)

- **Crew = 5:** Oscar, Kessa, Cael, Wick, Tarn. (Doss still carried as grief; Quill not yet present.)
- **Place:** still inside **the wild** (the Warden's country), at the grey's now-clean hollow. The
  **Warden's leave to cross is earned**; the **road south is open** for the first time since the
  cordon (Ch24).
- **Bodies/mood:** the crew is bloodied from holding the rim (Ch28); Wick is wrung out but intact,
  carrying her door *lessened, not gone*. Oscar is **fully rested** (the wild replenishes him — do
  **not** invoke fraying for ordinary working).
- **What's waiting on the far edge of the wild:** civilization, the southern roads — and **Corwen**,
  the House finder, who has had the cordon up since Ch24 and now only has to wait at the tree line.
  The wild hid them; leaving it un-hides them.

**Engine of the arc:** the wild protected them. The road south is the road *out* of protection and
*into* the hunt. The thing they fought for (the open road) is the thing that exposes them.

### The locked spine Arc 4 must deliver (do not violate)

1. **Hunter turn OPENS Arc 4** (not the close of Arc 3): Corwen pressure → **Ash snatches Oscar from
   Corwen** → **~Ch30 Kessa-POV reunion**.
2. **Climax at Everset:** apex living magic + **the font**; the reveal **Everset = Evermere remade**
   (as the erasure-engine's seat — the Evermere/Everset "one tumbler off" itch finally pays off).
3. **Corwen dies** (on-page). **Quill is given to Oscar** (the Arc-1 "Quill-absence" motif pays off).
4. **"The sweep" debuts here** (Oscar's climax-tier ability; reskinned name, no game vocab).
5. **Living-magic spectacle ONLY at Everset.** Every prior setting in the arc stays old-magic /
   made-relic. Do not front-load the font.
6. **Ash = niece of the *original* Oscar Alberich.** The crew reads **captor**; Oscar learns he is her
   **guest**; her identity is a deliberate on-page reveal — **do NOT spend it early.**

### HELD for Book 2 — must NOT resolve in Arc 4 (hard line)

- **The architect's identity** (who makes the made things). Ash does **not** name them. The Warden's
  "they're getting better at you" remains scale-dread only.
- **"The fair copy" as a technique/concept** (how made things are produced). Ash may be *family to the
  line* and *know Oscar is a copy* without ever explaining the method. Keep the *how* off the page.

## 1. Brainstorm — the undefined MIDDLE (≈Ch30 reunion → Everset, ~10 chapters)

The two ends are fixed: a **Ch29–30 snatch-and-reunion** open, and a **~Ch41–42 Everset** close. The
question is the shape of the ~10 chapters between (≈Ch31–40). Three candidate shapes, then a
recommended spine.

### Shape A — "The Two Roads" (parallel convergence)

Oscar travels the whole middle **with Ash** (as her guest, learning) on one track; the **crew pursues**
on another (believing it a rescue); **Corwen hunts both**. The three lines converge at Everset. The
Ch30 "reunion" is a **near-miss / brief contact** (Kessa gets eyes on Oscar, can't take him).

- **Pros:** sustains the captor mystery longest; maximal structural irony (crew runs a rescue that was
  never needed); two independent tension engines; Oscar gets uninterrupted Ash-time so the
  guest/niece reveal can be built slowly and land hard.
- **Cons:** isolates Oscar from the ensemble for ~10 chapters — directly **fights the locked
  "ensemble decentering"** goal and the crew banter that carries the book's humor; strains the word
  "reunion" at Ch30 (a near-miss isn't a reunion); risks two thin braids instead of one strong one;
  POV management gets heavy (constant cross-cutting).

### Shape B — "The Uneasy Caravan" (truce-travel)

The crew **catches Ash fast**; the Ch30 reunion forces **crew + Oscar + Ash into one fractious party**;
**Corwen the shared threat** keeps the truce from breaking. The captor/guest ambiguity rides *inside*
the party for chapters — the crew watching Ash like a hawk, Oscar slowly realizing she isn't what they
think. Living-magic stays off; the road, a waystation, and Corwen's pursuit fill the middle.

- **Pros:** keeps the ensemble together (honors decentering + banter + per-POV rotation); "reunion"
  lands cleanly and literally at Ch30; Ash **on-page** lets her relationship to Oscar and the
  guest/niece reveal develop with weight and land **mid-arc** for maximum runway; Corwen pressure is
  continuous and motivates cooperation.
- **Cons:** "captor" read is harder to sustain when she travels cooperatively (needs craft — see
  mitigations); classic road-trip sag risk (needs a set-piece to break it).

### Shape C — "The Waystation / Intermediate Seat" (a place)

Ash takes Oscar to an **intermediate location** — a House holdfast, a relic-town, or an **Alberich-line
dead-house / waystation** — where most of the middle happens as a contained set-piece; the crew
infiltrates / lays siege / negotiates their way in; Corwen closes on the location. Then a final push to
Everset.

- **Pros:** a strong spine and a real set-piece (no sag); a chance to reveal Ash's resources and the
  line's history; can **seed Everset** structurally (a smaller version of the thing they'll meet).
- **Cons:** a second big location **competes with Everset's payoff** and risks front-loading
  living-magic spectacle (forbidden outside Everset); pacing trap — a static middle then a rushed
  climax; the dead-house could read as an Arc-1 retread.

### ★ Recommended spine — **B-dominant hybrid (B + one C beat + an A-flavored opening)**

A through-line that takes the best of each and respects every locked beat and design goal:

- **Open with an A-flavored true separation (Ch29–30):** Corwen genuinely *takes* Oscar; **Ash takes
  him from Corwen** and runs; the crew is scattered and Oscar is gone for a real, frightening beat.
  This earns the word *reunion* and gives Kessa's Ch30 POV its tracking-and-dread spine.
- **Resolve into B (the Uneasy Caravan) by ~Ch31–32:** the crew runs Ash down; the standoff can't be
  won (Ash won't surrender Oscar, won't kill them) and **Corwen's continuing hunt forces a brittle
  truce**. The mixed party travels south. Captor-read held for the crew; guest-read dawning for Oscar.
- **Spend one C beat mid-journey (a single intermediate set-piece, ~Ch33–35):** an **Alberich-line
  waystation / dead-house** — kept strictly **old-magic / made-relic** so it does not steal Everset's
  living-magic thunder. It breaks road-trip sag, lets Oscar *read* the line's history, tips the
  guest/captor truth for Oscar, and becomes the place **Corwen catches them** (cost paid).
- **Land the reveals mid-to-late arc (~Ch35–38), not at the very end:** Oscar learns *guest* first;
  the **crew** learns *guest + Ash's identity* later; the relationship has room to mean something
  before Everset rather than being a climax twist crammed into the font sequence.
- **Climb to Everset (Ch39–42):** approach → gates → the font → the **Everset = Evermere** reveal →
  Corwen dies, the sweep debuts, Quill given. Living-magic spectacle reserved entirely for here.

**Why this and not pure B or pure A:** pure A starves the ensemble and the humor and makes "reunion"
a lie; pure B risks a flat middle and a too-cozy captor; pure C duplicates Everset. The hybrid keeps
the crew together (design goal), gives one strong set-piece (anti-sag), sustains the captor read
through the early-middle, lands the guest/niece reveal with runway, and **protects the Everset
payoff** by keeping every intermediate beat old-magic.

**Mitigation for B's "cozy captor" risk:** keep Ash genuinely *withholding* — she controls the route,
won't let Oscar leave, won't explain herself, and is visibly steering toward Everset for her **own**
reasons. To the crew that reads as a captor with an agenda; to Oscar it slowly reads as a host who
won't say why. Same behavior, two readings — that gap is the suspense, and it's how you don't leak the
reveal early.

### Ch29 — "The Gate That Walks" — POV: **Oscar** *(proposed)*

- **Premise:** The crew walks out of the wild onto the open southern road — straight into the patience
  of **Corwen**, who has only had to wait. The finder closes on them like a door it walks past, and
  takes Oscar. Then a third party no one saw coming takes Oscar **from Corwen**.
- **Beat / turn:** Two turns. (1) The hunt the whole book has deferred finally *lands* — Corwen is not
  a fight, he's an apparatus; the dread is his indifference. (2) At the chapter's hinge, **Ash snatches
  Oscar** — the crew (and reader) read a **second captor**, a rival predator, nothing more.
- **Crew:** Kessa reads the cordon's geometry too late (the wild's edge lied to her sense of safe
  ground); Cael's warmth is **returning** as they near civilization (seed: he's stronger here than in
  the deep wood — a quiet reversal of Arc 3); Tarn covers a withdrawal; **Wick**, who believes Oscar
  is *her master*, is the one who can't accept it — set her up as the engine of the pursuit.
- **Advances toward Everset:** removes Oscar from the crew and puts him on Ash's road — which is, though
  no one knows it yet, **the road to Everset**.
- **Locked beat delivered:** **Hunter turn opens Arc 4** (Corwen pressure → Ash snatches Oscar). Ash's
  identity stays hidden — she's "the woman who took him," read as captor.
- **Reveal discipline:** No hint of *guest*, *niece*, or *Alberich line*. Corwen does not explain
  himself (indifference). No living-magic.
- **[FOR THE AUTHOR]:** *Mechanics of the double-snatch* — how does Ash take a captive **from** an
  impersonal House finder who "closes gates like doors"? Options: (a) Ash unmakes/over-writes Corwen's
  hold the way Oscar would (implying she shares the line's gift — risky, edges toward leaking); (b)
  Ash is simply *faster and unafraid*, exploits Corwen's indifference (he doesn't *care* enough to
  pursue hard, only to *close*); (c) Ash has House standing/credentials Corwen must defer to (sets up
  the "is she House?" misdirection). Recommend **(b)+(c)** to preserve the held reveal.
